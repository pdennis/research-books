Overview

In 2026, Doug Lloyd called for stronger enforcement of election laws and investigations into alleged voter fraud. He cited claims of illegal voting and isolated ballot theft cases to justify these efforts. Lloyd also argued the attorney general should have broad authority to take legal action, including suing the governor.

Doug Lloyd Cited Alleged Voter Fraud To Push For Strong Election Law Enforcement And Investigations And Greater Authority To Sue The Governor

Doug Lloyd Supported Strong Election Law Enforcement And Raised Concerns About Alleged Voter Fraud

Doug Lloyd Supported Election Integrity And Called For Strong Enforcement Of Election Laws And Accountability For Violations

2026: Doug Lloyd Said Election Laws “Mean Nothing If They Are Not Enforced” And Vowed To Enforce “Real Consequences” For Those Who “Attempt To Cheat The System.” According to Michigan GOP, “Election integrity begins with strong leadership from the Secretary of State and a clear, cooperative partnership between the Secretary of State, the Attorney General, and the Legislature. Each has a distinct role, but protecting the integrity of our elections only works when those roles are aligned, transparent, and focused on the rule of law rather than politics. As Attorney General, my responsibility will be to enforce the law. I will focus my efforts on investigating credible allegations of election fraud and prosecuting violations wherever the evidence leads. Election laws mean nothing if they are not enforced, and I will make it clear that anyone who attempts to cheat the system will face real consequences.” [Michigan GOP, Doug Lloyd, Archived 4/20/26]

2026: Doug Lloyd Said He Would Work “Closely With Local Prosecutors, Sheriffs, And Election Officials” To Strengthen Election Laws And “Close Gaps That Invite Abuse.” According to Michigan GOP, “That enforcement role also means working closely with local prosecutors, sheriffs, and election officials to ensure clear lines of communication before, during, and after elections. I have successfully worked with the election officials in Eaton County for over a decade, I want to bring that level of communication to the Attorney General’s Office. When issues arise, they must be addressed quickly, professionally, and without political bias. Voters deserve confidence that concerns will be taken seriously and resolved based on facts and evidence, not headlines or pressure. At the same time, election integrity is about more than punishment. It is about clarity and consistency. As Attorney General, I will work with the Legislature to strengthen election laws where needed, close gaps that invite abuse, and ensure election workers have clear guidance and legal support so they can do their jobs without fear or confusion. Protecting election integrity is essential to preserving public trust in our system. Every lawful vote must count, and every unlawful act must be met with accountability. As Attorney General, I will uphold the law, defend the process, and ensure Michigan elections are fair, secure, and worthy of the public’s confidence. ” [Michigan GOP, Doug Lloyd, Archived 4/20/26]

2025: Doug Lloyd Pushed For Investigations And Prosecutions Over Alleged Illegal Voting And Suggested Individuals Who Were “Not Legal Citizens” Were Voting. According to Lloyd on WMKT Special Edition Interviews, “When you're talking about those individuals who are in our country or are just living in our state and they're actually there, they're voting illegally. Well, we're going to need a good secretary of state who's actually going to be paying attention to that, because if the secretary of state is paying attention to that, then we're going to have the ability as the attorney general to actually get that investigation and actually prosecute. And I believe that's what has to happen, because every citizen is entitled to their vote. But there has to be a legal citizen in the state to actually be able to do that. If you're not a legal citizen in the state, then you shouldn't be voting in our elections.” [Doug Lloyd, WMKT Special Edition Interviews, 12/4/25] (AUDIO)
 

Lloyd Pointed To Isolated Mailbox Ballot Theft Cases To Support Claims That Ballot Theft Existed In Michigan

April 2026: Lloyd Pointed To Isolated Mailbox Ballot Theft Cases To Support Claims That Ballot Theft Existed In Michigan. According to Doug Lloyd on Michigan’s Big Show, “HOST: Guess you never know til you really know sometimes, but you would be especially savvy on something like that because of what you do for a living. As the prosecuting attorney, do you think, speaking of elections and so forth, that ballot theft truly exists in our state? LLOYD: Well, different versions of ballot theft actually exist. I mean, I've actually prosecuted people in Eaton County for stealing ballots from mailboxes. So, you know, when you talk about it, it's simplest form. It's actually happened. We've actually prosecuted people that I've told people since I got into this campaign that, you know, if that's an evidence of there, then we're going to look at a case and we're going to prosecute a case. Because, Michael Patrick, the funny thing is, when I talk to people, people are talking to me about, you know, are you going to sue somebody similarly?  They're usually looking at me and saying, hey, if the facts and evidence are there, are you going to prosecute?” [Doug Lloyd, Michigan’s Big Show, 4/23/26] (AUDIO) 

Doug Lloyd Asserted The Attorney General Could Take Independent Legal Action, Including Suing The Governor, Over Election Integrity Matters

2025: Doug Lloyd Said The Attorney General Was Not Accountable To The Governor And Could File Suit Against The Governor Over Election-Related Actions He Deemed Unconstitutional. According to Lloyd on WMKT Special Edition Interviews, “HOST: But in Michigan, the attorney general represents both the people of the state and the governor's administration. How would you handle a situation where your legal and your legal interpretation of statutes conflicts with the governor's preferred policy? LLOYD: We, the attorney general, is responsible to the people, responsible to the secretary of state. It's not responsible to the governor. Now, can we provide good legal counsel?  Yes, we can. But providing good legal counsel means that the client, whether that be, you know, the legislature, the secretary of state, the attorney general, any of the departments that we work with, they have to actually take that good legal counsel if they choose to go in a different direction. And they're affecting Michigan sovereignty. Well, it doesn't matter if it's a Republican or Democrat that are sitting in those positions. The attorney general has the responsibility to actually look out for its citizens. So for me, as the attorney general, if I'm providing good legal counsel, that is a taken. And the governor, let's say, chooses to go in a different direction that I don't believe is constitutional. Well, then it's the responsibility of the attorney general to actually file suit against the governor. And the attorney general is the only one that has the ability to do that.” [WMKT Special Edition Interviews, Doug Lloyd, 12/4/25] (AUDIO)
