# Rep. Darrell Issa (CA-48): Can't Be Trusted on Jobs and the Economy

The third-wealthiest member of Congress with a net worth of $283 million, Rep. Darrell Issa has consistently sided against working families on jobs and the economy. Issa was the only member of San Diego's congressional delegation to vote against the $1 trillion Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, which funded roads, bridges, ports, and broadband — calling it a bill that "does not build — it destroys." He voted to codify DOGE cuts that threatened San Diego's 47,000-strong federal workforce — the fifth-largest concentration of federal workers in the nation — and introduced legislation to make DOGE permanent. His vote for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's Medicaid cuts could cost San Diego County up to 15,200 jobs according to the UC Berkeley Labor Center, while his support for Trump's tariffs drove San Diego's inflation to the highest in the nation at 4% and forced local small businesses to raise prices or shut down entirely. Issa voted against raising the federal minimum wage, voted against the PRO Act's worker protections, and has earned a 10% lifetime score from the AFL-CIO. Throughout it all, the bill he championed delivered an average $66,000 tax cut to the richest 1 percent of Americans while the bottom 20 percent got almost nothing.

### Voted Against the Infrastructure Law That Every Other San Diego Member Supported

**Issa Was The Only Member Of San Diego's Congressional Delegation To Vote Against The $1 Trillion Infrastructure Law.** According to Times of San Diego, "all San Diego Congress members but Issa vote to pass $1 trillion infrastructure bill" on November 5, 2021. Issa rejected H.R. 3684, the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, which directed resources toward roads, bridges, ports, broadband expansion, and clean drinking water systems. Issa claimed the legislation "does not build — it destroys." [Times of San Diego, [11/5/21](https://timesofsandiego.com/politics/2021/11/05/all-san-diego-congress-members-but-issa-vote-to-pass-1-trillion-infrastructure-bill/)]

### Championing DOGE Cuts That Threaten San Diego's 47,000 Federal Workers

**Issa Voted To Codify $9.4 Billion In DOGE Cuts That Slashed The Federal Workforce.** According to the National Association of Counties, the House passed the Rescissions Act of 2025 (H.R. 4) on June 12 in a narrow 214-212 vote, canceling $9.4 billion in previously approved federal funding as the first formal attempt to codify DOGE cuts. Issa voted yes on the measure. [National Association of Counties, [6/12/25](https://www.naco.org/news/us-house-passes-rescissions-package)]

**Issa Introduced Legislation To Make DOGE Permanent By Codifying It Into Federal Law.** According to a press release from Issa's office, the DOGE Codification Act of 2025 would "build a statutory base under the President's reform effort so that the work can continue without interruption or delay." The bill aimed to make it harder for Congress or a future president to repeal DOGE. [Issa.house.gov, [5/25](https://issa.house.gov/media/press-releases/issa-moves-codify-doge-law)]

**San Diego Had The Fifth-Highest Concentration Of Federal Workers In The Nation, With 47,000 Jobs At Stake.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, nearly 47,000 workers in San Diego County are employed by the federal government, making the region the fifth-highest concentration of executive branch federal workers in the nation. Workers reported that "we're being kept in the dark" as DOGE-driven cuts swept through federal agencies. [San Diego Union-Tribune, [3/2/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/03/02/people-are-frustrated-people-are-angry-fear-and-chaos-grip-san-diegos-47000-strong-federal-workforce/)]

### Issa's Medicaid Vote Could Cost San Diego County Up To 15,200 Jobs

**Issa's Vote For Medicaid Cuts Could Cost San Diego County Up To 15,200 Jobs.** According to KPBS, citing the UC Berkeley Labor Center, the Medicaid provisions Issa voted for could "cost San Diego County 7,600-15,200 jobs" as healthcare sector employment losses cascade through local economies. The cuts would also eliminate coverage for more than 28,000 people in his district alone. [KPBS, [7/2/25](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2025/07/02/congressman-issa-falsely-claims-criminal-illegals-are-enrolled-in-medicaid)]

**Two Hospitals In Issa's District Faced A Combined $14 Million Revenue Loss From His Health Care Votes.** According to American Journal News, hospitals in Murrieta and Temecula in Issa's district "are expected to see a combined $14 million dip in revenue" as a result of Medicaid cuts and expiring ACA tax credits Issa voted against extending. [American Journal News, [1/28/26](https://americanjournalnews.com/darrell-issas-long-record-of-voting-to-repeal-obamacare/)]

**Issa Claimed The Medicaid-Cutting Bill Would Be "Good For Economy" While His Own District Faced Thousands Of Job Losses.** According to The East County Californian, Issa defended his vote by claiming the bill would "drive our economy, advance our national security, and uphold fundamental fairness." Rep. Juan Vargas called it "one of the worst pieces of legislation," describing it as a "wealth transfer from working families to billionaires." [East County Californian, [7/25](https://eccalifornian.com/issa-says-medicaid-cutting-bill-will-be-good-for-economy/)]

### Supporting Tariffs That Drove San Diego To The Highest Inflation In The Nation

**Issa Voted To Keep Trump's Tariffs On Canada As San Diego Households Faced $2,400 In Annual Tariff Costs.** According to the Associated Press, the House voted 219-211 to terminate the national emergency Trump declared to impose tariffs on Canada, but Issa voted against the measure, saying he does not want to "tie the president's hands on trade." San Diego Democratic Reps. Scott Peters and Sara Jacobs warned that tariffs were projected to cost local households approximately $2,400 per year. [AP via OPB, [2/12/26](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/02/12/house-votes-to-slap-back-trump-s-tariffs-on-canada-in-rare-bipartisan-rebuke/)]

**San Diego Had The Highest Inflation Rate In The Nation At 4%, Driven In Part By Tariffs Issa Supported.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, San Diego's inflation rate hit 4% in July 2025 — the highest among U.S. metro areas, well above the 2.7% national average. Nonalcoholic beverages and beverage materials were up 8.2% year-over-year, an increase that "could reasonably be attributed to rising costs" from aluminum tariffs. [San Diego Union-Tribune, [8/12/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/08/12/san-diegos-inflation-rate-remains-highest-in-nation-at-4/)]

**Tariffs Issa Supported Threatened 2,500 Cross-Border Businesses In Otay Mesa.** According to inewsource, the suspension of the $800 de minimis tariff exemption disrupted the cross-border economy between San Diego and Tijuana, where roughly 2,500 businesses in Otay Mesa are tied to cross-border imports and exports. A logistics executive estimated that "one in three businesses will be affected." [inewsource, [9/11/25](https://inewsource.org/2025/09/11/tariff-san-diego-de-minimis-exemption-imports/)]

### Tax Cuts For The Wealthy, Benefit Cuts For Workers

**Issa Voted For A Bill That Gave The Richest 1 Percent An Average $66,000 Tax Cut While The Bottom 20 Percent Got Almost Nothing.** According to the Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act delivered $117 billion in net tax cuts to the richest 1 percent of Americans in 2026 — "an average net tax cut of $66,000" — while the bottom 20 percent received just $1.5 billion total. ITEP found that "the $117 billion in net tax cuts going to the richest 1 percent next year will exceed the amount going to the entire bottom 60 percent of taxpayers." [ITEP, [7/25](https://itep.org/tax-provisions-in-trump-megabill-national-and-state-level-estimates/)]

**Issa Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Which Slashed $186 Billion From SNAP Over Ten Years — Threatening Food Assistance For 400,000 San Diegans.** According to CNBC, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act cut SNAP funding by approximately $186 billion over ten years — "a 20% cut that marks the largest reduction in the history of the program." Approximately 400,000 San Diegans rely on CalFresh benefits threatened by the cuts. [CNBC, [8/8/25](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/08/trumps-spending-bill-cuts-billions-in-snap-benefits.html)]

### A 10% AFL-CIO Score: Consistently Siding Against Workers

**Issa Earned A Lifetime 10% Score From The AFL-CIO, Indicating A Consistently Anti-Worker Voting Record.** According to the AFL-CIO Congressional Scorecard, Issa earned a lifetime score of 10% on votes related to workers' rights, wages, and labor protections — one of the lowest scores among members of Congress. [AFL-CIO, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://aflcio.org/scorecard/legislators/darrell-issa)]

**Issa Voted Against The PRO Act, Which Would Have Strengthened Workers' Right To Organize.** According to Fox 5 San Diego, Issa voted against the Protecting the Right to Organize Act of 2021, calling it "extreme and inflexible." The bill, which passed the House 225-206, would have expanded labor protections by limiting right-to-work laws and strengthening workers' ability to form unions. Issa referenced California's Assembly Bill 5, calling it "one of the most reckless job killers in recent memory" and warning against extending it nationally. [Fox 5 San Diego, [3/9/21](https://fox5sandiego.com/rep-darrell-issa-votes-against-the-extreme-and-inflexible-pro-act/)]

**Issa Voted Against Raising The Federal Minimum Wage To $7.25 Per Hour.** According to OnTheIssues, Issa voted no on H.R. 2, the Fair Minimum Wage Act, on January 10, 2007, which raised the federal minimum wage from $5.15 to $7.25 per hour. [OnTheIssues, [Viewed 2/16/26](http://www.ontheissues.org/CA/Darrell_Issa_Jobs.htm)]

[San Diego Union-Tribune, [10/23/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/10/23/its-hard-to-know-what-to-do-in-the-age-of-tariffs-san-diego-small-businesses-raise-prices-rethink-suppliers-and-settle-into-uncertainty/)]

**A San Diego Yarn Shop Owner Said Tariffs Made It Impossible To Plan Her Business.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, Sara Heckman, owner of Apricot Yarn & Supply in San Diego, said yarn prices rose up to $2 per skein due to tariffs — "a steep hike" and "a big change" — and that the unpredictability was the worst part: "We don't know what it is. That's the hardest part." [San Diego Union-Tribune, [10/23/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/10/23/its-hard-to-know-what-to-do-in-the-age-of-tariffs-san-diego-small-businesses-raise-prices-rethink-suppliers-and-settle-into-uncertainty/)]

**San Diego Federal Workers Described Fear And Chaos As DOGE Cuts Issa Championed Swept Through Their Agencies.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, San Diego's 47,000 federal workers — about 1 in every 34 employees in the county — faced widespread uncertainty as DOGE-driven layoffs swept through federal agencies. Workers reported being "kept in the dark" about their futures. One headline captured the prevailing mood: "People are frustrated. People are angry." [San Diego Union-Tribune, [3/2/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/03/02/people-are-frustrated-people-are-angry-fear-and-chaos-grip-san-diegos-47000-strong-federal-workforce/)]
