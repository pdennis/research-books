# Rep. Darrell Issa (CA-48): Can't Be Trusted on Affordability

The third-wealthiest member of Congress with a net worth of $283 million, Rep. Darrell Issa has consistently sided against his constituents on the issues that hit their wallets hardest. In February 2026, when a bipartisan House majority voted to block Trump's tariffs on Canada — tariffs projected to cost San Diego households $2,400 per year — Issa voted to keep them in place, saying he did not want to "tie the president's hands on trade." He voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which slashed $186 billion from SNAP over ten years — threatening food assistance for 400,000 San Diegans who rely on CalFresh — while delivering an average $66,000 tax cut to the richest 1 percent of Americans. San Diego's inflation rate hit 4% in mid-2025, the highest in the nation, driven in part by the tariff policies Issa championed. Local small businesses have been forced to raise prices or shut down entirely, while his constituents face meat and egg prices up 7.3%, medical care up 6.8%, and tuition and childcare up 9.4%. Issa has chosen to protect tax breaks for the ultra-wealthy and the president's trade agenda over the everyday affordability concerns of families in his district.

### Supporting Trump's Tariffs While San Diego Families Pay the Price

**Issa Voted To Keep Trump's Tariffs On Canada As San Diego Households Faced $2,400 In Annual Tariff Costs.** According to the Associated Press, the House voted 219-211 to terminate the national emergency Trump declared to impose tariffs on Canada, but Issa voted against the measure, saying he does not want to "tie the president's hands on trade" and would support the tariffs on Canada "at this time." Meanwhile, San Diego Democratic Reps. Scott Peters and Sara Jacobs warned that tariffs were projected to cost local households approximately $2,400 per year. [AP via OPB, [2/12/26](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/02/12/house-votes-to-slap-back-trump-s-tariffs-on-canada-in-rare-bipartisan-rebuke/)]

**San Diego Had The Highest Inflation Rate In The Nation At 4%, Driven In Part By Tariffs Issa Supported.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, San Diego's inflation rate hit 4% in July 2025 — the highest among U.S. metro areas, well above the 2.7% national average. The article noted that nonalcoholic beverages and beverage materials were up 8.2% year-over-year, an increase that "could reasonably be attributed to rising costs" from aluminum tariffs. [San Diego Union-Tribune, [8/12/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/08/12/san-diegos-inflation-rate-remains-highest-in-nation-at-4/)]

**San Diego Small Businesses Raised Prices Or Shut Down Due To Tariffs Issa Backed.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, San Diego small businesses faced steep cost increases from tariffs, with raw material prices jumping 30-100% for some businesses. Lynnerup Fine Shirts, a bespoke shirt maker, closed entirely rather than pass tariff costs to customers. San Diego Regional Chamber of Commerce CEO Chris Cate said, "Small businesses are going to probably have a more difficult time to absorb or broaden the impact." [San Diego Union-Tribune, [10/23/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/10/23/its-hard-to-know-what-to-do-in-the-age-of-tariffs-san-diego-small-businesses-raise-prices-rethink-suppliers-and-settle-into-uncertainty/)]

**Tariffs Issa Supported Threatened San Diego's Cross-Border Economy And 2,500 Businesses.** According to inewsource, the suspension of the $800 de minimis tariff exemption disrupted the cross-border economy between San Diego and Tijuana, where roughly 2,500 businesses in Otay Mesa are tied to cross-border imports and exports. A logistics executive estimated that "one in three businesses will be affected." [inewsource, [9/11/25](https://inewsource.org/2025/09/11/tariff-san-diego-de-minimis-exemption-imports/)]

### Voting to Cut Food Assistance for 400,000 San Diegans

**Issa Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Which Slashed $186 Billion From SNAP Over Ten Years.** According to CNBC, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act signed into law on July 4, 2025 cut SNAP funding by approximately $186 billion over ten years — "a 20% cut that marks the largest reduction in the history of the program." Issa voted yes on the bill, which passed the House 215-214. [CNBC, [8/8/25](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/08/trumps-spending-bill-cuts-billions-in-snap-benefits.html)]

**400,000 San Diegans On CalFresh Faced Benefit Cuts From The Law Issa Voted For.** According to inewsource, approximately 400,000 San Diegans receive CalFresh benefits, and California faced "over $2 billion in cuts" from the federal spending plan Issa supported. The law removed work-requirement exemptions for veterans, homeless individuals, older adults, and parents of older children, meaning these groups "could be the first to lose benefits if they can't find work." [inewsource, [7/16/25](https://inewsource.org/2025/07/16/federal-cuts-snap-calfresh-san-diego-eligibility/)]

**CalFresh Recipients Lost Utility Bill Deductions That Helped Stretch Their Food Budgets.** According to CalMatters, the law Issa voted for eliminated the Standard Utility Allowance deduction for most CalFresh households, meaning that "only households that include a member over the age of 60 or someone with a disability can claim such deductions for electricity, gas or water bills." For other families, this meant "a lower monthly food budget for already struggling families." [CalMatters, [12/25](https://calmatters.org/commentary/2025/12/calfresh-federal-law-california-families/)]

### Tax Cuts for the Ultra-Wealthy, Not Working Families

**Issa Voted For A Bill That Gave The Richest 1 Percent An Average $66,000 Tax Cut While The Bottom 20 Percent Got Almost Nothing.** According to the Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act delivered $117 billion in net tax cuts to the richest 1 percent of Americans in 2026 — "an average net tax cut of $66,000" — while the bottom 20 percent received just $1.5 billion total. ITEP found that "the $117 billion in net tax cuts going to the richest 1 percent next year will exceed the amount going to the entire bottom 60 percent of taxpayers." [ITEP, [7/25](https://itep.org/tax-provisions-in-trump-megabill-national-and-state-level-estimates/)]

**The Bill Issa Voted For Raised The Estate Tax Exemption To $15 Million, Benefiting The Ultra-Wealthy.** According to NPR, the One Big Beautiful Bill increased the estate tax exemption to $15 million per individual — $30 million per married couple. EY tax expert Tony Nitti said the provision "basically shrinks the universe of people who'll ever have to pay estate tax." The bill also permanently set the top marginal tax rate at 37%, down from 39.6%. [NPR, [11/5/25](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/05/nx-s1-5590112/trump-beautiful-bill-taxes-republican-rich-wealthy)]

**Issa Is The Third-Wealthiest Member Of Congress With A Net Worth Of $283 Million.** According to Times of San Diego, Issa's portfolio tracker showed a net worth of $283 million as of 2025, "his highest value as a House member since 2017." His 2024 financial disclosure listed 16 holdings valued between $5 million and $25 million each, plus a $3.1 million condo in Washington's Kalorama neighborhood. Only two members of Congress are wealthier. [Times of San Diego, [8/12/25](https://timesofsandiego.com/politics/2025/08/12/rep-issa-ranked-no-3-richest-member-of-congress-discloses-2024-financial-report/)]

### Skyrocketing Costs in Issa's District

**San Diego Residents Faced Meat And Egg Prices Up 7.3%, Medical Care Up 6.8%, And Tuition And Childcare Up 9.4%.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, year-over-year price increases in the San Diego metro area as of mid-2025 included meat, poultry, fish, and eggs up 7.3%, medical care up 6.8%, transportation costs up 5.1%, shelter and rent up 5%, and tuition and childcare up 9.4%. [San Diego Union-Tribune, [8/12/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/08/12/san-diegos-inflation-rate-remains-highest-in-nation-at-4/)]

**San Diego Households Paid $2,400 More Per Year Due To Tariffs While Issa Backed The President's Trade Agenda.** According to Times of San Diego, Democratic Reps. Scott Peters and Sara Jacobs convened business leaders to warn that tariffs were projected to cost San Diego households approximately $2,400 per year. Peters said, "We need to be very clear about what tariffs do to our businesses, consumers and our economy. Tariffs are a tax that our businesses pay, which increases prices for Americans already struggling with the cost of living." [Times of San Diego, [8/13/25](https://timesofsandiego.com/politics/2025/08/13/reps-expect-tariffs-to-cost-san-diego-households-2400-this-year/)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**A San Diego Clothing Importer Saw Costs Jump $80 Per Shipment Due To Tariffs Issa Supported.** According to inewsource, under the tariff changes Issa backed, a $20 purse imported from China would now cost $34. Eduardo Acosta, international business affairs chair for the San Diego Chamber of Commerce, described the price increases as businesses adapted to the elimination of the $800 de minimis exemption. [inewsource, [9/11/25](https://inewsource.org/2025/09/11/tariff-san-diego-de-minimis-exemption-imports/)]

**A San Diego Yarn Shop Owner Said Tariffs Made It Impossible To Plan.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, Sara Heckman, owner of Apricot Yarn & Supply in San Diego, said yarn prices rose up to $2 per skein due to tariffs — "a steep hike" and "a big change" — and that the unpredictability was the worst part: "We don't know what it is. That's the hardest part." [San Diego Union-Tribune, [10/23/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/10/23/its-hard-to-know-what-to-do-in-the-age-of-tariffs-san-diego-small-businesses-raise-prices-rethink-suppliers-and-settle-into-uncertainty/)]

**A Bespoke Shirt Maker In San Diego Closed His Business Rather Than Pass Tariff Costs To Customers.** According to The San Diego Union-Tribune, Rasmus Lynnerup, owner of Lynnerup Fine Shirts, shut down his San Diego business rather than raise prices on his $400-$700 custom shirts to absorb tariff costs, saying, "It's just a pain in the neck for me [...]  I don't need to mess around with that." [San Diego Union-Tribune, [10/23/25](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2025/10/23/its-hard-to-know-what-to-do-in-the-age-of-tariffs-san-diego-small-businesses-raise-prices-rethink-suppliers-and-settle-into-uncertainty/)]
