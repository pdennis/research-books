Overview

  • On May 14, 2026, the House Ethics Committee announced it was investigating Rep. Chuck Edwards over allegations of sexual harassment. It was reported that Edwards took young female staffers out to one-on-one dinners, wrote them intimate notes, vacationed with them in Las Vegas, and bought them personalized handguns among other gifts.
  • Edwards denied the allegations and dismissed them as gossip and rumors. On August 3, 2026, the House Ethics Committee found that Edwards had violated House rules regarding sexual harassment and recommended that he be censured.

Following A Months-Long Investigation, The House Ethics Committee Found That Edwards’ Inappropriate Behavior Towards Staffers Violated House Rules And Recommended He Be Censured

August 3, 2026: The House Ethics Committee Found That Edwards Violated House Rules Surrounding Sexual Harassment And Recommended That He Be Censured

8/3/26: The House Ethics Committee Found That Edwards Violated House Rules By “Failing To Adhere To The Spirit Of Rules Prohibiting Sexual Harassment Of And Unwanted Advances To House Staffers” And Recommended That Edwards Be Censured. According to Axios, "The House Ethics Committee found Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-N.C.) violated House Rules by ‘failing to adhere to the spirit of rules prohibiting sexual harassment of and unwanted advances to House staffers,’ according to a 25-page report released Monday. Why it matters: The bipartisan committee recommended that Edwards be censured after concluding he ‘violated the Code of Official Conduct by failing to act in a manner that reflects creditably upon the House.’ " [Axios, 8/3/26]

Edwards Continued To Deny That His Behavior Towards His Staffers Was Inappropriate

Edwards “Continued To Deny Any Wrongdoing” And “Refused To Acknowledge The Reality Of The Power Imbalance Between A Member Of Congress And Young Staffers.” According to The New Republic, "The House Ethics Committee began investigating Representative Edwards in late April for then-unspecified ‘allegations.’ Reports of inappropriate conduct with two young female staffers soon followed, including a handwritten note from the congressman that read, ‘Your kindness, encouragement, and light-heartedness have written a complex chapter in my heart that I will never stop reading.’ Despite this mountain of evidence to the contrary, Edwards continues to insist that he did nothing wrong.  ‘Representative Edwards has continued to deny any wrongdoing publicly and to the Committee. He has also refused to acknowledge the reality of the power imbalance between a Member of Congress and young staffers,’ the report states. ‘This shows a lack of self-awareness and accountability.’" [The New Republic, 8/3/26]
