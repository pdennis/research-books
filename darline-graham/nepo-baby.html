Overview

Nepo baby Darline Graham found herself hoisted into a US Senate seat based on little more than her family name. Despite having no legislative or policy experience in any elected office, Graham was immediately coronated as a replacement for her late brother, Senator Lindsey Graham. In the time since, she has demonstrated her shocking lack of knowledge on issues like national security, a policy area that touches every South Carolinian. South Carolina deserves a senator who has a handle on the issues and wins office on merit, not family name.

Graham Had No Experience In Elected Office Before Being Anointed Senator

Graham Never Held Or Ran For Elected Office Before Being Appointed To Her Late Brother’s Senate Seat

Graham Never Held Or Ran For Elected Office Before Being Appointed To Her Late Brother’s Senate Seat. According to NBC News, “Sen. Darline Graham, R-S.C., finds herself in a place she never imagined: the white-hot national spotlight that once tracked her brother’s every move. She had never run for elected office before. And she was not active in the local or state Republican Party political scene, despite her brother’s prominence. The 62-year-old mother of two spent a three-decade career in public service in South Carolina, focused on workforce issues and helping the blind and broader disability community. But her work never sparked controversy or generated headlines. That’s all changed for Darline Graham, who replaced her brother, Lindsey Graham, in the Senate last week after his sudden death at age 71.” [NBC News, 7/23/26]

GOP Opponents Attacked Graham For Nepotism And Inexperience During The Primary

GOP Opponents Attacked Graham For Nepotism And Inexperience During The Primary Runoff. According to NBC News, “Sen. Darline Graham defeated Rep. Ralph Norman in South Carolina’s Republican primary runoff, NBC News projects, a victory for President Donald Trump, who recruited, endorsed and campaigned for her following her brother Lindsey Graham’s death six weeks ago. […] To beat Norman, who’s spent nearly a decade in Congress, Graham had to overcome GOP attacks of nepotism and inexperience. She had never held or run for elected office before, though she did lead a 130-person state agency that helped blind people. In the initial primary earlier this month, Graham finished first with 33% of the vote, short of the 50% needed to avoid a runoff. [NBC News, 8/25/26]

Republicans Felt “In The Dark” About Graham’s Policy Positions. According to NBC News, “Few details are known about her policy positions or how she would vote in the Senate. Graham Admitted She Was “Not That Informed” Regarding National Security Issues

Graham Admitted She Was “Not That Informed” Regarding National Security Issues In Response To A Debate Question On Taiwan. According to CNN, “A moderator, journalist Greta Van Susteren, asked Graham, ‘Are Taiwan and the South China Sea national security issues for the United States? If so, why?’ Graham asked Van Susteren to repeat the question. She did, and Graham said she was ‘just going to be honest here.’ ‘I’m not … that informed on national security, so — but I do support the military,’ she said, noting her brother served in the Air Force and her dad in the Army. ‘I’m not a polished politician up here,’ Graham continued at the debate, hosted by South Carolina ETV and Public Radio. ‘National security is not my thing, not my area of expertise, but I do support the military.’” [CNN, 8/19/26]
