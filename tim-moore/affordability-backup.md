# Rep. Tim Moore (NC-14): Can't Be Trusted on Affordability

Moore tells North Carolinians he is fighting to lower their costs, but his record tells a different story. He voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which the CBO found will cut incomes for the bottom 80% of American households while the wealthiest gain — slashing $187 billion from SNAP food assistance that 1.4 million North Carolinians depend on and shifting $420 million in costs to the state. He has championed Trump's tariff agenda, telling constituents that "maybe we ought to pay a little more" for goods even as tariff-driven inflation pushed North Carolina grocery prices up 17% and furniture prices surged under new 25-50% tariffs that directly hit the state's signature industry. Meanwhile, Moore privately invested up to $245,000 betting against the very economy he publicly claims is thriving — purchasing a triple-leveraged fund that profits when small-cap stocks decline.

### Told Constituents To Accept Higher Prices

**Moore Said North Carolinians Should "Pay A Little More" For Goods If Tariffs Create Jobs.** According to the Charlotte Observer, Moore "said 'short-term pain' from the Trump administration's tariff policies is worth it to break an American addiction to 'cheap labor and cheap goods.'" Moore stated, "'If it creates jobs here and it helps sustain jobs, maybe we ought to pay a little more for a product made by Americans.'" [Charlotte Observer, [8/16/25](https://www.charlotteobserver.com/news/politics-government/article311705979.html)]

- **Moore Acknowledged Tariffs "Clearly Can Have Some Impact" On Inflation But Dismissed Concerns.** According to the Charlotte Observer, Moore acknowledged that tariffs "clearly can have some impact on" inflation but attributed the problem to "retaliatory tariffs" from other countries rather than accepting responsibility for the policy he supports. [Charlotte Observer, [8/16/25](https://www.charlotteobserver.com/news/politics-government/article311705979.html)]

**North Carolina Consumers Faced Tariff-Driven Inflation As Grocery Prices Rose 17% And Businesses Began Passing Costs To Shoppers.** According to WRAL, "rising inflation is starting to show up on North Carolina grocery receipts and in the state's factories," with grocery prices up 17.2% since January 2022. Duke University economist John Coleman warned, "'If you had to make some cutbacks or some adjustments in terms of what you had to buy, what you wanted to buy, you're going to have to continue to do that for quite some time [...] that's just the new economic reality that we're faced with.'" [WRAL, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.wral.com/story/new-economic-reality-nc-consumers-face-tariff-driven-inflation/22087128/)]

- **Three-Fourths Of Firms Surveyed Intended To Pass Tariff Costs Directly To Consumers.** According to WRAL, AllianceBernstein chief economist Eric Winograd said, "'You are starting to see scattered bits of the tariff inflation regime filter in,'" and Sarah Dickerson noted that "about three-fourths of firms locally [...] were intending to pass some of the tariff-related costs onto consumers." [WRAL, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.wral.com/story/new-economic-reality-nc-consumers-face-tariff-driven-inflation/22087128/)]

### Tariffs Hit North Carolina's Furniture Industry

**Trump Imposed 25-50% Tariffs On Furniture And Cabinets, And Moore Backed The Policy That Raised Prices On North Carolina Consumers.** According to WFAE, Trump announced tariffs of 25% on kitchen cabinets, vanities, and upholstered wooden furniture — with cabinet tariffs escalating to 50% — claiming the state "has lost its furniture industry to China." Home Furnishings Association CEO Peter Theran warned that the higher costs could be passed on to consumers, which would threaten demand. [WFAE, [9/30/25](https://www.wfae.org/2025-09-30/trump-cites-north-carolina-in-furniture-tariff-announcement)]

- **Furniture Prices Outpaced Inflation As Tariffs Rippled Through North Carolina's Signature Industry.** According to CBS News, furniture prices outpaced general inflation, with "living room, kitchen and dining room furniture rising 4.6% in November from a year earlier, compared with a 2.7% annual increase in the overall Consumer Price Index." [CBS News, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-tariff-increase-delay-furniture-kitchen-cabinets/)]

**Daimler Truck Announced Potential Layoffs Of 573 Workers Near Charlotte Due To Tariff Uncertainty.** According to WRAL, Daimler Truck announced a potential temporary layoff of 573 workers near Charlotte "due to reduced truck orders" driven by "customer uncertainty over economic policy and tariffs." N.C. State economist Mike Walden observed, "'Whenever there's economic uncertainty, that is not good for business.'" [WRAL, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.wral.com/story/new-economic-reality-nc-consumers-face-tariff-driven-inflation/22087128/)]

### Voted For Legislation That Raises Costs On Working Families

**Moore Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Which The CBO Found Will Cut Incomes For The Poorest Americans While Boosting The Wealthiest.** According to the Congressional Budget Office, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act will decrease resources "for households toward the bottom of the income distribution" while increasing resources "for households in the middle and toward the top." The CBO estimated the highest 10% of earners would see incomes rise by 2.7% by 2034, while the lowest 10% would see incomes fall by 3.1%. [CBO, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61387)]

- **Combined With Tariffs, The Legislation Would Cut Incomes For The Bottom 10% Of Households By About 7%.** According to the Yale Budget Lab, the combination of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and 2025 tariffs "would reduce after-tax-and-transfer incomes on average among the bottom 80 percent of U.S. households, with the bottom ten percent of households seeing an average reduction of about 7 percent in incomes, while those at the top would see an increase of nearly 1.5 percent." [Yale Budget Lab, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/combined-distributional-effects-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-and-tariffs)]

**Moore Voted To Cut $187 Billion From Food Assistance That 1.4 Million North Carolinians Depend On.** According to the NC Institute of Medicine, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act cuts SNAP funding by $187 billion over the next decade, affecting "1.4 million" North Carolinians who receive food assistance, "including about 580,000 children, of which more than 40,000 are under 18 months old." The law shifts "$420 million" in costs to North Carolina and an additional "$65 million" in administrative costs. [NCIOM, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://nciom.org/federal-changes-to-food-assistance-in-north-carolina/)]

- **Moore Claimed The SNAP Cuts Would "Strengthen" The Program.** According to WRAL, Moore framed the cuts as an incentive to reduce fraud, stating the bill "strengthens SNAP by keeping the focus on those who truly need help" — even as the CBO projects the cuts will reduce SNAP participation by 2.4 million people per month nationally. [WRAL, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.wral.com/story/advocates-say-federal-food-aid-cuts-scheduled-for-2026-will-make-nc-s-hunger-problem-worse/22117669/)]

**SNAP Benefits Already Did Not Cover The Cost Of A Moderately Priced Meal In Any North Carolina County Before Moore Voted To Cut Them.** According to the NC Institute of Medicine, in 2024 "North Carolina SNAP benefits did not cover [moderately priced meal] cost in all 100 counties" — and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act will further reduce benefits by changing how the Thrifty Food Plan is calculated. [NCIOM, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://nciom.org/federal-changes-to-food-assistance-in-north-carolina/)]

### Bet Against The Economy He Claims Is Thriving

**Moore Invested Up To $245,000 In A Fund That Profits When The Stock Market Declines — While Publicly Touting The Economy.** According to Notus, Moore "poured up to $245,000 of his own cash into an exotic investment fund that earns him money when a key stock market index falters." The fund, Direxion Daily Small Cap Bear 3X Shares, "delivers three times the inverse daily performance of the Russell 2000 Index." Moore made six purchases between late June and late August 2025 while simultaneously telling the public that Trump's policies are "'going to spur the economy.'" [Notus, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.notus.org/congress/tim-moore-investment-russell-2000-index-trump-economy)]

- **Moore Abruptly Switched From Bullish To Bearish Investments Mid-2025.** According to Notus, from early 2025 through mid-July, Moore invested "hundreds of thousands of dollars into the Direxion Daily Small Cap Bull 3X Shares fund" designed to profit when markets rise. After mid-July, "Moore has not invested in the bull-market fund since," pivoting entirely to the bearish fund — suggesting he privately expected the economic policies he publicly championed to fail. [Notus, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.notus.org/congress/tim-moore-investment-russell-2000-index-trump-economy)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**Western North Carolina Food Assistance Requests Doubled As SNAP Cuts Took Effect.** According to WLOS, the Asheville-Buncombe Community Christian Ministry reported that "food assistance requests have doubled from 35 to 80 families daily" following changes from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Rev. Scott Rogers, ABCCM Executive Director, said, "'Our neighbors are hurting right now. They're scared, anxious, worried most about their children.'" [WLOS, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://wlos.com/news/local/western-north-carolina-residents-snap-supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-big-beautiful-bill-department-health-human-services-united-states-department-agriculture-food-federal-eligibility-asheville-buncombe-community-christian-ministry)]

- **Approximately 187 Local Veterans Could Lose SNAP Eligibility Under The Law Moore Voted For.** According to WLOS, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act eliminated previous SNAP exemptions for veterans, and approximately "187 local veterans" in the Asheville-Buncombe area could lose eligibility. Rogers noted that "'having that kind of work that can show a paycheck or stub is a real challenge'" for residents with disabilities or inconsistent employment. [WLOS, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://wlos.com/news/local/western-north-carolina-residents-snap-supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-big-beautiful-bill-department-health-human-services-united-states-department-agriculture-food-federal-eligibility-asheville-buncombe-community-christian-ministry)]

**The Head Of North Carolina's Largest Food Bank Said Charity Alone Cannot Replace The Cuts Moore Voted For.** According to WRAL, Amy Beros, CEO of the Food Bank of Central and Eastern NC, said, "'This is not a problem charity alone can solve,'" noting the food bank distributed 100 million pounds of food last year but that "for every meal the food bank provides, SNAP provides nine." Beros warned that SNAP cuts will devastate rural communities by reducing grocery store and farm viability, potentially creating "'more food deserts.'" [WRAL, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.wral.com/story/advocates-say-federal-food-aid-cuts-scheduled-for-2026-will-make-nc-s-hunger-problem-worse/22117669/)]
