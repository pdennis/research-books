# Rep. Tim Moore (NC-14): Can't Be Trusted on Health Care

Moore spent a decade as NC House Speaker blocking Medicaid expansion that would have covered 600,000 uninsured North Carolinians, arguing that "the best thing that folks can do is to get a job" — even as 12 rural hospitals in the state closed or stopped providing inpatient care and the state forfeited an estimated $20 billion in federal funding. When expansion finally passed in 2023, Moore reversed course and took credit. Now in Congress, Moore voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which the CBO projects will cause more than 10 million people nationwide to lose Medicaid coverage and could trigger the very clause in NC's expansion law that would end coverage for nearly 680,000 North Carolinians who gained it. He also voted for the Lower Health Care Premiums for All Americans Act, which fails to extend enhanced ACA subsidies — a move projected to cause 4 million people to lose marketplace coverage and premiums to more than double on average.

### Blocked Medicaid Expansion for a Decade

**Moore Led A Decade-Long Effort To Block Medicaid Expansion That Would Have Covered 600,000 North Carolinians.** According to PBS, "North Carolina became one of the last states to approve Medicaid expansion Thursday, after more than a decade of fierce opposition from Republican lawmakers [...] the House voted 87-24 in favor." Moore was House Speaker for the final eight years of this blockade. [PBS, [3/23/23](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/north-carolina-approves-medicaid-expansion-reversing-long-opposition)]

**Moore Said He Had A 'Philosophical Opposition' To Medicaid Expansion And That 'The Best Thing That Folks Can Do Is To Get A Job.'** According to WFAE, Moore expressed "philosophical opposition to just the blanket Medicaid expansion," stating, "'I believe that any kind of benefit program should really be for those who cannot take care of themselves.'" He added, "'If someone is able-bodied, we ought to do all we can as a state to incentivize a person to get a job.'" [WFAE, [11/5/21](https://www.wfae.org/health/2021-11-05/nc-house-speaker-tim-moore-explains-his-chambers-opposition-to-medicaid-expansion)]

**WRAL Editorial Called Moore's Opposition To Medicaid Expansion A 'Prejudiced Fantasy.'** According to a WRAL editorial, Moore "irresponsibly misrepresents who is eligible for Medicaid expansion," embracing "the prejudicial notion that poverty is a choice." The editorial noted that "nearly all North Carolinians who would qualify for Medicaid under expansion do have jobs" and that since 2015, North Carolina had foregone $20 billion in federal Medicaid funding and $5.7 billion in hospital reimbursements due to non-expansion. [Editorial, WRAL, [6/25/19](https://www.wral.com/story/editorial-speaker-moore-s-opposition-to-medicaid-expansion-is-prejudiced-fantasy/18471701/)]

### Reversed Course and Took Credit

**After Years Of Blocking Expansion, Moore Reversed His Position And Called It A 'Historic Step Forward.'** According to PBS, Moore called the passage of Medicaid expansion a "historic step forward to increase access to healthcare for our rural communities." The deal came with a trigger clause tying expansion to the state budget and included work requirements. [PBS, [3/23/23](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/north-carolina-approves-medicaid-expansion-after-decade-of-fierce-opposition)]

### Voted to Gut Medicaid in Congress

**Moore Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Which The CBO Projects Will Cause More Than 10 Million People To Lose Medicaid Coverage.** According to Moore's own press release, he voted for H.R. 1 on July 3, 2025, claiming it "delivers real results for the American people" by "strengthening Medicaid." However, the Congressional Budget Office projects that new Medicaid restrictions in the bill will cause more than 10 million people to lose coverage over the next decade. [Tim Moore Press Release, [7/3/25](https://timmoore.house.gov/media/press-releases/congressman-tim-moore-votes-pass-one-big-beautiful-bill)]

**Moore's Vote Threatens The Very Medicaid Expansion He Took Credit For In North Carolina.** According to WFAE, North Carolina's Medicaid expansion includes a "trigger clause" that automatically ends the program if federal funding drops below 90% coverage. Nearly 680,000 North Carolinians have enrolled since expansion launched, and more than one-third come from rural counties. The One Big Beautiful Bill's Medicaid cuts could activate this trigger. [WFAE, [8/25/25](https://www.wfae.org/health/2025-08-25/medicaid-expansion-helped-hundreds-of-thousands-in-nc-now-its-future-is-uncertain)]

- **A North Carolina Doctor Warned That Losing Medicaid Would Be A Death Sentence For Young Patients.** According to WFAE, Dr. Laura Ucik, a family physician in Henderson, said of her young Type 1 diabetes patients: "'If they lose Medicaid, life is over. Like, it's just over. They're not going to make it to 30.'" [WFAE, [8/25/25](https://www.wfae.org/health/2025-08-25/medicaid-expansion-helped-hundreds-of-thousands-in-nc-now-its-future-is-uncertain)]

**Moore Voted For The Lower Health Care Premiums For All Americans Act, Which Fails To Extend ACA Subsidies That 20 Million Americans Depend On.** According to Moore's press release, he claimed the bill "puts patients first and delivers real relief to families in Western North Carolina." However, the bill does not extend enhanced premium tax credits set to expire, which the NEA notes would cause "premiums to rise significantly — more than double, on average" for marketplace plans and lead to an estimated 4 million people losing coverage. [Tim Moore Press Release, [12/17/25](https://timmoore.house.gov/media/press-releases/congressman-tim-moore-votes-pass-lower-health-care-premiums-all-americans-act); NEA, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.nea.org/advocating-for-change/action-center/letters-testimony/vote-no-lower-health-care-premiums-all-americans-act-hr-6703)]

### Constituents Demand Answers

**Moore's Constituents Held A Town Hall In His Absence To Protest His Health Care Votes.** According to NC Voices, constituents from Moore's district held a town hall at Kings Mountain High School — their second such event — after Moore declined to attend. Attendees raised concerns about Medicaid cuts and impacts of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Janice Robinson, NC Program Director of Red Wine and Blue, said, "'Your blind eye to vulnerable citizens and your constituents, while giving away our tax dollars to millionaires and billionaires, will only increase our tenacity and determination.'" [NC Voices, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://ncvoices.com/your-blind-eye-to-your-constituents-rep-tim-moore-constituents-host-town-hall-in-his-absence-to-address-federal-funding-concerns/)]

**Protesters Brought A Black Casket To Moore's Gastonia Office To Protest His Medicaid Vote.** According to Qnotes Carolinas, demonstrators delivered a casket decorated with signs reading "This coffin represents policy murder!" to Moore's Gastonia district office, demanding an explanation for his support of the One Big Beautiful Bill. Signs at the protest read "307,000 people in North Carolina will lose Medicaid." [Qnotes Carolinas, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://qnotescarolinas.com/medicaid-cuts-gastonia-protest-2025/)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**Nearly 680,000 North Carolinians Who Gained Medicaid Coverage Could Lose It Because Of Legislation Moore Supported.** According to WFAE, nearly 680,000 North Carolinians have enrolled in Medicaid since expansion launched in 2023, with more than one-third from rural counties. The expansion allowed patients to "afford tests, specialist visits, insulin, and transportation services previously out of reach." The trigger clause in NC's expansion law means federal cuts could end the entire program. [WFAE, [8/25/25](https://www.wfae.org/health/2025-08-25/medicaid-expansion-helped-hundreds-of-thousands-in-nc-now-its-future-is-uncertain)]

**North Carolina Rural Hospitals Stand To Lose $3.7 Billion Annually From Federal Medicaid Cuts Moore Voted For.** According to WFAE, rural hospitals in the state face approximately $3.7 billion in annual losses from federal cuts, while the One Big Beautiful Bill offers only roughly $100 million per year to North Carolina for rural hospitals — a fraction of what is needed. [WFAE, [8/25/25](https://www.wfae.org/health/2025-08-25/medicaid-expansion-helped-hundreds-of-thousands-in-nc-now-its-future-is-uncertain)]
