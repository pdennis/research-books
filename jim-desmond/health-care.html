August 2026: California Already Reported Tens Of Thousands Having Dropped Out Of Food And Healthcare Programs, Fewer Applicants, And Increased Costs 

Tens Of Thousands Of San Diegans Were Reported To Have Dropped Out Of Food And Healthcare Programs, Causing Healthcare Organizations To Cut Back Services. According to San Diego Union-Tribune, "Tens of thousands of San Diegans have dropped out of food and healthcare programs, many even before changes in eligibility for noncitizens took effect April 1 and before work requirements kick in.  Some healthcare organizations are already cutting back in anticipation of the reductions. Hospitals, some of which already are operating at a loss, expect increases in uncompensated care because they can’t turn away anyone who shows up at emergency rooms. That ultimately could affect services and costs for insured people." [San Diego Union-Tribune, 8/30/26]

Officials At Covered California Reported First-Time Applicants Are Down By A Third And Officials Estimated Medi-Cal Could Lose More Than $30 Billion Annually. According to San Diego Union-Tribune, "Officials at Covered California, a health coverage marketplace that utilizes federal subsidies through the federal Affordable Care Act, said first-time applicants are down about a third since the bill was approved. […] California officials estimate Medi-Cal could lose more than $30 billion annually once federal changes fully take effect in 2027." [San Diego Union-Tribune, 8/30/26]

Federal Cuts Affected Programs Covering About 1 In 3 San Diego County Residents, Causing Higher Premiums And Fewer Renewals For Insurance Plans. According to San Diego Union-Tribune, "Cuts, new requirements and other changes have also been rolling in for federally funded heathcare[sic] programs that cover about 1 million people in the region, or about 1 in 3 San Diego County residents, according to Paul Sisson of The San Diego Union-Tribune. […] In addition to fewer people signing up for Covered California, state officials say people facing higher premiums are increasing their deductibles, reducing coverage or going without it entirely. Many middle-class people who benefited from the Affordable Care Act will also be affected by the loss of enhanced tax credits. Premiums for middle-class residents will increase by an average of $500 per month, according to Covered California Executive Director Jessica Altman.  Covered California said nearly 374,000 renewing consumers had canceled or terminated their renewal plan in the past year. That’s 19% of renewing consumers, compared with 13% to 14% cancelling their coverage in prior years." [San Diego Union-Tribune, 8/30/26]
