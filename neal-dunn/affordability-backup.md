# Rep. Neal Dunn (FL-02): Can't Be Trusted on Affordability

Rep. Neal Dunn has consistently sided against his own constituents on affordability. Dunn voted to keep Trump's tariffs on Canada — tariffs that Florida TaxWatch warned could raise grocery bills by 10 to 15 percent and add $10,000 to the cost of a new Florida home and $7,000 to the cost of a car. He then cast a decisive vote for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which slashed $300 billion from SNAP and $700 billion from Medicaid — programs that nearly 3 million Floridians depend on for food assistance alone. The Yale Budget Lab found that the combined effect of tariffs and the OBBBA would cut incomes for the bottom 10 percent of earners by approximately 7 percent while handing the top 10 percent a 1.5 percent raise. Meanwhile, Dunn voted against capping insulin at $35 a month and against allowing Medicare to negotiate drug prices. His district, which stretches across rural north Florida, includes Gadsden County — where 35.5 percent of children are food insecure, the highest rate in the state — and 13 counties that exceed the state poverty level.

---

### Backed Trump's Tariffs Over Florida Families

**Dunn Voted Against Repealing Trump's Tariffs On Canada.** According to NBC News, the House passed a resolution to rescind Trump's tariffs on Canada by a vote of 219-211 on February 11, 2026, with six Republicans crossing party lines to support repeal. Dunn was not among them, voting to keep the tariffs in place. [NBC News, [2/11/26](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/house-votes-end-trump-tariffs-canada-rcna258661)]

**Florida TaxWatch Warned Tariffs Could Raise Florida Grocery Bills By 10 To 15 Percent.** According to Florida Politics, a Florida TaxWatch analysis conducted in collaboration with the University of North Florida found that "Floridians could see grocery bills increase by 10% to 15% if new trade tariffs are imposed." The report also found that the cost of shipping a container of goods had "risen by 80% since November." [Florida Politics, [7/9/25](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/746357-florida-taxwatch-state-consumers-will-see-higher-prices-as-a-result-of-donald-trumps-trade-tariffs/)]

**Florida TaxWatch Found Tariffs Would Add $10,000 To The Cost Of A New Florida Home And $7,000 To A Car.** According to the Business Observer, the Florida TaxWatch report found that tariffs would add approximately $10,000 to the cost of a Florida home and $7,000 to the cost of a car. TaxWatch officials stated that "while the tariffs aim to protect domestic industries and boost capital investment in the long-term, they pose significant risks to the broader economy in the short-term." [Business Observer, [7/11/25](https://www.businessobserverfl.com/news/2025/jul/11/report-tariffs-add-to-cost-florida-house-cars/)]

**A University Of Tampa Economist Called Tariffs A 'Category 5 Economic Hurricane' For Florida.** According to Newsweek, University of Tampa economist Abigail Hall Blanco described the tariffs as "a Category 5 economic hurricane created by policy" that would damage Florida businesses and hurt the state's economic growth. Florida is the eighth-largest U.S. import destination, receiving over $112 billion in merchandise goods from countries targeted by Trump's tariffs. [Newsweek, [4/4/25](https://www.newsweek.com/donald-trump-tariffs-impact-florida-jobs-economy-2055385)]

**The Tax Foundation Found Tariffs Would Cost The Average American Household $1,300 In 2026.** According to the Tax Foundation, Trump's tariffs amounted to an average tax increase of $1,000 per U.S. household in 2025 and a projected $1,300 per household in 2026. The Tax Foundation determined that the tariffs represent "the largest U.S. tax increase as a percent of GDP" since 1993. [Tax Foundation, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/trump-tariffs-trade-war/)]

### Voted For Massive Cuts To Food And Health Care Programs

**Dunn Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Which Cut $300 Billion From SNAP And $700 Billion From Medicaid.** According to GovTrack, Dunn voted Yea on H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, on May 22, 2025, which passed 215-214. The law slashed $300 billion from SNAP and $700 billion from Medicaid over the next decade. [GovTrack, [5/22/25](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/votes/119-2025/h145)]

- **The OBBBA Would More Than Double Florida's Cost Of Administering SNAP From $89 Million To $1.6 Billion.** According to WUSF, the law required states to pay 5 to 25 percent of food benefit costs for the first time, with Florida's share set at 25 percent based on its payment error rate. The No Kid Hungry Florida director said, "These dramatic increases put millions of Florida's children and families at risk of losing a critical program." [WUSF, [5/30/25](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-05-30/trump-big-beautiful-bill-cuts-medicaid-snap-what-that-means-for-floridians)]

- **The SPLC Found Nearly 1.75 Million Florida Families With Children Could Lose Food Assistance Access Under The OBBBA.** According to the Southern Poverty Law Center, the legislation "gutted $187 billion in federal SNAP funding through 2034," and if Florida's current payment error rate of 15.13 percent triggers cost-sharing provisions, "state legislators would need nearly $1 billion to cover benefits alone." Florida had the fourth-highest SNAP payment error rate nationally. [Southern Poverty Law Center, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.splcenter.org/resources/hopewatch/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-florida-food-assistance/)]

- **The OBBBA Could Threaten 33,000 Florida Jobs And Blow A $3 Billion Hole In The State Economy.** According to WUSF, the Medicaid provisions could "threaten approximately 33,000 jobs — half healthcare-related" and create "a potential $3 billion hole in state economy." Florida Policy Institute CEO Sadaf Knight said the provider tax ban "would really hamstring our ability to raise the dollars that we need to fund our Medicaid program." [WUSF, [5/30/25](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-05-30/trump-big-beautiful-bill-cuts-medicaid-snap-what-that-means-for-floridians)]

**The Yale Budget Lab Found The Combined Effect Of Tariffs And The OBBBA Would Cut Incomes For The Poorest Americans By Approximately 7 Percent While Raising Incomes For The Wealthiest By 1.5 Percent.** According to the Yale Budget Lab, "the combination of OBBBA and 2025 tariffs would reduce resources on average among the bottom 80 percent of households." The bottom 10 percent of households would see an approximately 7 percent reduction in after-tax income over 2026-2034, while the top 10 percent would see a 1.5 percent increase. [Yale Budget Lab, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/combined-distributional-effects-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-and-tariffs-0)]

### Voted Against Lowering Health Care And Prescription Drug Costs

**Dunn Voted Against Capping Insulin Costs At $35 Per Month.** According to the House Clerk, Dunn voted against the Affordable Insulin Now Act (H.R. 6833) on March 31, 2022, which would have capped out-of-pocket insulin costs at $35 per month for Medicare and commercial insurance patients. The bill passed 232-193, with only 12 Republicans voting in favor. [House Clerk, [3/31/22](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2022102)]

**Dunn Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Allowed Medicare To Negotiate Drug Prices For The First Time.** According to WFSU News, Dunn voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, which capped insulin at $35 for seniors, allowed Medicare to negotiate prescription drug prices for the first time, and capped annual out-of-pocket drug costs at $2,000 for Medicare beneficiaries. Dunn dismissed the law as "inflationary spending" and said "this is increasing taxes." [WFSU News, [8/17/22](https://news.wfsu.org/wfsu-local-news/2022-08-17/rep-neal-dunn-explains-vote-against-inflation-reduction-act)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**Gadsden County — In Dunn's District — Had The Highest Child Food Insecurity Rate In Florida At 35.5 Percent.** According to WUSF, Gadsden County ranked last among Florida's 67 counties for child food security, with 35.5 percent of children experiencing food insecurity — nearly four times the rate in St. Johns County (9.1 percent). [WUSF, [2/21/25](https://www.wusf.org/health-news-florida/2025-02-21/food-insecurity-major-concern-florida-children-well-being)]

**Nearly 3 Million Floridians Relied On SNAP For Food Assistance — And Dunn Voted To Gut The Program.** According to WUSF, approximately 3 million Floridians rely on SNAP, with 1 in 5 children already experiencing food insecurity. Dunn's vote for the OBBBA put this program at risk by shifting billions in costs to the state for the first time in SNAP's 86-year history. [WUSF, [5/30/25](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-05-30/trump-big-beautiful-bill-cuts-medicaid-snap-what-that-means-for-floridians)]

**Thirteen Counties In Dunn's District Exceeded The State Poverty Level, With Household Incomes Below The Statewide Median.** According to the Florida Public Health Review, Congressional District 2 stretches from the Alabama border through the Big Bend, and "thirteen of the counties exceed the state poverty level of 12.4% and have a household income less than the statewide median of $71,000." [Florida Public Health Review, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1289&context=fphr)]

**Florida's Statewide Food Insecurity Rate Jumped To 14.4 Percent, With 3.2 Million Floridians — Including Over 800,000 Children — Going Hungry.** According to Florida Politics, food insecurity has risen from 10.6 percent to 14.4 percent in four years, affecting 1 in 7 residents and 1 in 5 children, with nearly half of rural Floridians experiencing at least one symptom of food insecurity. [Florida Politics, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/584525-survey-shows-rising-food-insecurity-in-florida/)]
