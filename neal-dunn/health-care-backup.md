# Rep. Neal Dunn (FL-02): Can't Be Trusted on Health Care

Rep. Neal Dunn markets himself as a doctor who understands health care, but his record tells a different story. Dunn — a wealthy urologist who founded a 50-physician medical empire and a regional bank — has repeatedly voted against lowering health care costs for his own constituents. He voted to repeal the Affordable Care Act and gut protections for pre-existing conditions, voted against capping insulin costs at $35 a month, and voted against the Inflation Reduction Act's landmark provisions allowing Medicare to negotiate lower drug prices. Meanwhile, health care industry donors have been his top financial backers, and he has pushed legislation to let physicians own hospitals — a practice restricted under the ACA due to conflict-of-interest concerns. His district, which stretches across rural north Florida, has some of the highest uninsured rates in the state, with low-income residents in counties like Jackson seeing uninsured rates above 40 percent.

---

### Voted to Gut Health Care Protections

**Dunn Voted To Repeal The Affordable Care Act And Weaken Protections For Pre-Existing Conditions.** According to Dunn's own press release, Dunn voted to pass the American Health Care Act, which he described as a bill that "repeals Obamacare and replaces it with a patient-centered system." The bill allowed states to seek waivers to weaken insurance protections, including allowing insurers to charge higher premiums based on health status for those who let their coverage lapse. [Rep. Neal Dunn Press Release, [5/4/17](https://dunn.house.gov/2017/5/dr-dunn-s-statement-repealing-and-replacing-obamacare)]
- **The American College Of Physicians Called The AHCA Vote "Extremely Disappointing" And Said It Would "Result In Millions Of Americans Losing Their Coverage."** According to ACP, the organization was "'extremely disappointed' by House vote to pass the AHCA" and urged "Senate to reject the bill and start over." [American College of Physicians, [5/4/17](https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/acp-extremely-disappointed-by-house-vote-to-pass-the-ahca-urges-senate-to-reject-the-bill-and-start)]

**Dunn Voted Against Capping Insulin Costs At $35 Per Month.** According to the House Clerk, Dunn voted against the Affordable Insulin Now Act (H.R. 6833) on March 31, 2022, which would have capped out-of-pocket insulin costs at $35 per month for Medicare and commercial insurance patients. The bill passed 232-193, with only 12 Republicans voting in favor. [House Clerk, [3/31/22](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2022102)]

**Dunn Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Allowed Medicare To Negotiate Drug Prices For The First Time.** According to WFSU News, Dunn voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, which capped insulin at $35 for seniors, allowed Medicare to negotiate prescription drug prices for the first time, and capped annual out-of-pocket drug costs at $2,000 for Medicare beneficiaries. Dunn dismissed the law as "inflationary spending" and said "this is increasing taxes." [WFSU News, [8/17/22](https://news.wfsu.org/wfsu-local-news/2022-08-17/rep-neal-dunn-explains-vote-against-inflation-reduction-act)]

### Financial Conflicts of Interest

**Dunn Founded A 50-Physician Medical Practice And A Regional Bank Before Coming To Congress.** According to his official biography, Dunn founded the Panama City Urology Center, Bay Regional Cancer Center, and the Advanced Urology Institute, "a 50-physician practice with over 400 employees and offices throughout Florida." He was also "the founding Chairman of Summit Bank, a large regional bank headquartered in Panama City." [Neal Dunn Biography, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://dunn.house.gov/biography)]

**Health Professionals Were Dunn's Top Industry Donor, Contributing Over $273,000.** According to OpenSecrets, the health professionals industry was the top contributor to Dunn's campaign, with donations totaling $273,641 as of December 31, 2024. [OpenSecrets, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.opensecrets.org/members-of-congress/neal-dunn/industries?cid=N00037442)]

**Dunn Held Pharmaceutical And Health Products Investments Worth Over $32,000 While Voting On Health Care Legislation.** According to OpenSecrets, Dunn's 2018 financial disclosure reported pharmaceutical and health products holdings valued at $32,500, in addition to an estimated net worth of over $9.5 million. [OpenSecrets, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.opensecrets.org/personal-finances/neal-dunn/net-worth?cid=N00037442)]

**Dunn Violated The STOCK Act By Filing A Late Financial Disclosure.** According to OpenSecrets, Dunn was more than a month late disclosing that his wife purchased up to $50,000 in MicroStrategy stock on December 24, 2024. The trade was not disclosed until March 10, 2025, well after the 45-day federal deadline. Dunn's spokesman said trades were "made by an independent broker without any input from either the congressman or Mrs. Dunn." [OpenSecrets, [3/13/25](https://www.opensecrets.org/news/2025/03/rep-neal-dunn-becomes-latest-lawmaker-to-violate-stock-act)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**Dunn's Rural North Florida District Has Some Of The Highest Uninsured Rates In The State.** According to the Florida Policy Institute, low-income adults in Florida's rural areas face uninsured rates of 37 percent — 13 percentage points higher than metro areas. In counties within Dunn's district like Jackson County, rates are "even higher, in the 40 to 42 percent range." [Florida Policy Institute, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.floridapolicy.org/posts/florida-small-towns-and-rural-areas-are-disproportionately-hurt-by-floridas-failure-to-expand-medicaid)]

**Thirteen Counties In Dunn's District Exceed The State Poverty Level, With Household Incomes Below The Statewide Median.** According to the Florida Public Health Review, Congressional District 2 stretches from the Alabama border through the Big Bend, and "thirteen of the counties exceed the state poverty level of 12.4% and have a household income less than the statewide median of $71,000." [Florida Public Health Review, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1289&context=fphr)]
