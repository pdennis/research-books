Overview

  • Bouchard wanted the residents of MI-10 to believe he was running on his experience, not his father’s legacy. What he conveniently avoided talking about was that his father and a group of influential Michigan Republicans formed a super PAC to recruit Bouchard to run for office while he was still serving overseas. Additionally, on his campaign website biography Bouchard spends the entire first paragraph talking about the achievements of his father and grandfather.

Bouchard Claimed He Was Not Running On His Father’s Legacy, But He Was Recruited By His Father And Influential Michigan Republicans

bouchard Claimed He Was Running On his “Experience”

Bouchard Said He Was “Running On My Experience” And “What I Have Done,” Not On His Father’s Name Recognition And Legacy. According to The Detroit News, “Acknowledging his father's legacy as Oakland County sheriff, Bouchard said he is proud of his namesake's 30-plus-year career in law enforcement but is ‘running on my experience. I am running on what I have done.’” [Detroit News, 11/6/25]

Bouchard Cited Charlie Kirk’s Assassination As A Key Reason He Entered The Race For MI-10

Bouchard Said He Had Not Made The Final Decision To Run For Congress Until He Watched The Assassination Of Charlie Kirk. According to The Detroit News, “His bid already has the backing of several prominent Michigan Republicans who formed a committee in July to recruit the first-time candidate. Bouchard, however, said he did not make a final decision to enter the race until recently. ‘My sole focus while I was over there was leading our men and women. I think they deserve that,’ he said in a phone interview. ‘But in the back of my mind, what got me started thinking about it was when I watched the assassination of Charlie Kirk.’ Still deployed at the time, Bouchard described seeing news on his phone of Kirk's killing: ‘I was more worried about what's going on in my home country than I was in a war zone.’” [Detroit News, 11/6/25]

  • Bouchard Said He Felt The Need To Run For Congress Because The Country Needed To “Stand Up To A Lot Of The Socialist, Woke Waves We’re Seeing Right Now.” According to The Detroit News, “Mike Bouchard, an Army paratrooper and son of the Oakland County sheriff of the same name, is joining the GOP primary race for a key suburban Detroit congressional seat up for grabs next year. Bouchard is launching his bid Thursday, 12 days after returning to Michigan from a nine-month deployment to Iraq. ‘I truly believe we need leaders, we need fighters and we need veterans in D.C. to stand up to a lot of the socialist, woke waves that we're seeing right now,’ the Rochester Hills Republican told The Detroit News ahead of his campaign announcement. He added: ‘The toughest people I know jump out of planes into battle, and so I think we need to send a paratrooper to D.C.’” [Detroit News, 11/6/25]

Bouchard Was Recruited To Run By His Father And Influential Michigan Republicans While Deployed Overseas

While Bouchard Was Still Deployed Overseas, A Group Of Michigan Republicans Launched A Super PAC Designed To Recruit Bouchard To Run For MI-10. According to The Detroit News, “A new group of Michigan Republicans launched Thursday with the aim of recruiting and encouraging Michael Bouchard, son of the Oakland County sheriff, to run for Congress to succeed GOP U.S. Rep. John James in one of the state's most competitive U.S. House districts. Twenty elected leaders and business people signed on to serve as advisers to the Draft Captain Mike Bouchard committee, a super political action committee, in a show of support for Bouchard, who is currently deployed with the U.S. Army overseas and due back this fall.” [Detroit News, 7/24/25]

  • Bouchard’s Father Was One Of The Michigan Republicans Who Signed Onto The Committee To Recruit Bouchard. According to The Detroit News, “They're focused on Michigan's 10th District that covers southern Macomb County and Rochester and Rochester Hills in Oakland County, where James is serving his second term but running for governor. Bouchard's supporters include Macomb County Prosecutor Pete Lucido; former Michigan Republican Party Chairman Bobby Schostak; former ambassador David Fischer; Macomb County Treasurer Larry Rocca; and Sheriff Bouchard, as well as business leaders from the construction and automotive industries.” [Detroit News, 7/24/25]

March 2026: More Than Half Of The Money Bouchard's Campaign Had Accepted Came From Prior Donors To His Father

According To Analysis Conducted By The Detroit News, At Least 50.1 Percent Of Donors To Bouchard's Principle Campaign And Super PACS Had Donated To His Father

According To Analysis Conducted By The Detroit News, More Than Half Of The Money Bouchard’s Campaign Has Accepted Came From Prior Donors To His Father. According to The Detroit News, “Republican congressional hopeful Michael Bouchard, similar to other second-comers in Michigan political lineages, has so far relied heavily on donors to his father's past campaigns to fuel a 2026 run for a pivotal suburban Detroit U.S. House seat, a Detroit News analysis shows. More than half of the traceable money flowing to Bouchard's campaign and an outside group backing him has come from prior donors to his dad, the longtime Oakland County sheriff who shares a name with his 32-year-old son.” [Detroit News, 5/12/26]

  • Previous Donors To Bouchard’s Father Have Given At Least 50.1 Percent Of The Money Bouchard Has Raised Through His Principle And Super PACs. According to The Detroit News, “Previous donors to Sheriff Bouchard's two statewide campaigns have so far given at least $392,000 (or 43%) of the roughly $908,000 his son has raised from itemized donors in his bid for Congress through March 31. Contributions to a pro-Bouchard group called Honor, Discipline & Duty PAC have tilted more heavily toward past donors. About $179,000 (or 77%) of the committee's $232,000 in individual fundraising has come from past donors to the elder Bouchard. Those sums together indicate that at least 50.1% of the money raised so far in the race to boost the younger Bouchard has come from past family donors.” [Detroit News, 5/12/26]
  • The Detroit News Said Their Analysis Likely Underestimated How Many Crossover Donors There Were Between Bouchard And His Father. According to The Detroit News, “The News conducted its analysis of crossover donors between the two Bouchards using public federal and state campaign records of itemized gifts, which show individual names and addresses. That information makes it possible to match donors across campaigns, though name or address changes make it difficult to track all donors over time. For that reason, all figures presented from the analysis are likely underestimates. Only donations from individual people, rather than businesses or other candidate committees, are considered.” [Detroit News, 5/12/26]

Bouchard On The Donations: “I Absolutely Love The Support My Father And Some Of Our Lifelong Friends Have Brought.” According to The Detroit News, “To the younger Michael Bouchard, that's just the nature of tight-knit relationships formed over a lifetime. ‘I absolutely love the support my father and some of our lifelong friends have brought,’ the Army paratrooper-turned-candidate said in an interview. […] ‘Any time you go into politics, the first people that you go to for support are the people you know well and you trust, right?’ Bouchard said. ‘And so obviously those are going to be a lot of the same people that support my dad and that I've built up that trust with throughout my life.’” [Detroit News, 5/12/26]

  • Bouchard Admitted The Percent Of His Father’s Friends Donating To His Principle Campaign PAC “Makes Sense,” But Also Claimed “The Majority” Of His Donors Were New. According to The Detroit News, “He added: ‘So I would say, yeah, 40% (excluding the super PAC) makes sense. But then I would also say the majority of our donors are new to supporting a Bouchard, and I think that's because I'm the only one in this race with the experience that I bring to the table.’” [Detroit News, 5/12/26]
