2026: Diaz-Balart Voted Against Withdrawing US Troops From Iran. In September 2026, Mario Diaz-Balart voted against, according to Congressional Quarterly, “adoption of the concurrent resolution that would direct the president to remove U.S. armed forces from hostilities with Iran that Congress has not specifically authorized.” The vote was on adoption of the resolution. The House adopted the resolution by a vote of 220 to 204. [House Vote 307, 9/15/26; Congressional Quarterly, 9/15/26; Congressional Actions, H.Con.Res.93]

  • The House Had Adopted Two Similar Non-Binding Resolutions Previously, With This Vote Coming Over 60 Days After President Trump Notified Congress That Hostilities Had Resumed In Iran. According to Congressional Quarterly, “The House has twice adopted similar resolutions, including one time when the Senate then approved the same resolution. But as a concurrent resolution, the measure Congress cleared did not have the weight of law to force President Donald Trump to withdraw troops from the war. The resolution the House is voting on this time is sponsored by Rep. Seth Moulton, D-Mass., and comes more than 60 days after the Trump administration notified Congress fighting in the war resumed, a timeline that some Republicans have given weight to.” [Congressional Quarterly, 9/15/26]
  • The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) Reported That The Iran War Cost The Pentagon $38 Billion Through July And Would Cost An Additional Two To Three Billion Each Month It Goes On. According to Congressional Quarterly “The Iran war directly cost the Pentagon $38 billion through July and is likely to consume $2 billion to $3 billion more per month if recent hostilities in the region continue, the Congressional Budget Office reported Tuesday. The CBO report’s estimate of the war’s direct impact on the federal budget is made up mostly of the cost of replacing expended munitions, as well as the fiscal impact of equipment losses and expenses such as fuel and extra military pay.” [Congressional Quarterly, 9/16/26]
  • The Estimated Cost Did Not Include Repairs To Hundreds Of Damaged US Buildings In The Middle East. According to Congressional Quarterly, “The IG estimate, like CBO’s, does not include the price of potential repairs to what the IG said are ‘hundreds’ of U.S. military buildings and structures damaged or destroyed during the war in Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Oman and Jordan.” [Congressional Quarterly, 9/16/26]
  • The CBO Found That 40 Percent Of Inflation In “Personal Consumption Expenditures” In The Second Quarter Of 2026 Resulted From Higher Energy Costs Related To The War. According to Congressional Quarterly, “Besides the direct costs to the government and the opportunity cost of diminished munitions stocks, CBO’s analysts said more than 40 percent of inflation in ‘personal consumption expenditures’ in the second quarter of 2026 was due to higher energy costs. Those costs rose due to supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea, the report said. In the first quarter of 2027, CBO projected that the war will add roughly 0.5 percentage points to personal consumption inflation, compared to CBO’s estimate before the war. This higher inflation, in turn, will raise interest rates on Treasury securities, the analysts said.” [Congressional Quarterly, 9/16/26]

[WWSB (ABC), 7/13/26]                                                                    

  • The War In Iran Caused Fuel Inventories To Plunge To Historic Lows, Leading To A Major Surge In Gas Prices. According to the Washington Post, "Americans face a new round of punishing price increases for fuel and other products as oil and gas inventories plunge to historic lows around the world because of the war in Iran, energy executives and analysts warn. Fuel stocks are so low, experts said, that even an abrupt agreement between the United States and Iran and an immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz — through which one-fifth of the world’s oil and natural gas supplies passed before the war — may not stop fuel prices from soaring anew by the July Fourth holiday."
