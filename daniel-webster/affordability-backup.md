# Rep. Daniel Webster (FL-11): Can't Be Trusted on Affordability

Rep. Daniel Webster says he understands that "seniors on fixed incomes are particularly struggling as the costs of basic goods and groceries have skyrocketed." But his record tells a different story. Webster is a full-throated supporter of Trump's tariffs — which a nonpartisan Florida watchdog says will raise grocery bills 10 to 15 percent and add $10,000 to the cost of a new home in Florida. He voted to sustain tariffs on Canada even as six fellow Republicans broke ranks to oppose them. He voted for the "One Big Beautiful Bill," which gutted $187 billion from food assistance, shifted billions in new costs to Florida, and repealed consumer energy credits — while delivering $1 trillion in tax cuts to the richest 1 percent. He voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, which capped insulin at $35 a month for seniors and let Medicare negotiate drug prices. He voted against raising the federal minimum wage to $15 — a level Florida's own voters approved by a supermajority. And when more than 200 constituents packed a town hall in his district to confront him about these costs, Webster refused to show up.

### Webster Backed Trump's Tariffs — Then Voted To Keep Them

**Webster Endorsed Trump's Tariffs As "A Negotiation Tool" In An Op-Ed Published In His Own District's Newspaper.** According to Villages-News, Webster wrote: "President Trump is using tariffs -- and the threat of tariffs -- as a negotiation tool to force bad actors like China and Venezuela and enforcing fair trade [...]  I support President Trump's efforts to defend American industries and put American workers first." He added: "While the threats of tariffs and ongoing negotiations cause concerns and consternation as these conversations are happening in real time, I trust that President Trump will negotiate a deal that empowers every hardworking American to benefit." [Villages-News, [4/8/25](https://www.villages-news.com/2025/04/08/congressman-webster-wants-to-hear-from-small-businesses-hurt-by-tariffs/)]

**Webster Voted To Sustain Trump's Tariffs On Canada As Six Fellow Republicans Broke Ranks To Oppose Them.** According to CNN, "The House voted 219-211 to pass a Democratic-led resolution to terminate the national emergency Trump used to impose tariffs on Canada." Six Republicans voted with Democrats to rescind the tariffs. Webster was not among them — he voted to keep the tariffs in place. [CNN, [2/11/26](https://www.cnn.com/2026/02/11/politics/house-republicans-trump-canada-tariff-vote)]

### Tariffs Are Driving Up Costs Across The Board For Florida Families

**Florida TaxWatch Found That Tariffs Would Raise Grocery Bills 10 To 15 Percent And Add $10,000 To The Price Of A New Home.** According to Florida Politics, "Florida ranks fifth among the U.S. in grocery costs. An average Floridian's weekly grocery bill is expected to increase by 10 to 15 percent as a result of imposed tariffs." The report also found: "Higher construction costs can increase the price of a typical house built by Florida developers by $10,000. An increase in the cost of production in the automobile industry will lead to an increase in the price of a car, by at least $6,000-$7,000." [Florida Politics, [7/8/25](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/746357-florida-taxwatch-state-consumers-will-see-higher-prices-as-a-result-of-donald-trumps-trade-tariffs/)]

- **Florida TaxWatch CEO Warned That "The Average Floridian Is Going To Feel The Brunt" Of Tariffs.** According to Florida Politics, TaxWatch President and CEO Dominic Calabro said: "'The changing consumer sentiment indicates that Floridians are being cautious about the long-term implications of these trade policies. While some industries may see short-term gains, the broader economic outlook remains uncertain.'" [Florida Politics, [7/8/25](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/746357-florida-taxwatch-state-consumers-will-see-higher-prices-as-a-result-of-donald-trumps-trade-tariffs/)]

**A Central Florida Small Business Reported Paying "Almost Double" In Tariffs, Cutting Into Its Ability To Create Jobs.** According to Spectrum News 13, Rebecca Dobo, director of operations at Freshorize USA in Winter Springs, said: "'We are probably paying almost double what we have historically in tariffs, which has significantly cut into our bottom line and limits our capability to bring manufacturing into the states because now that profit is not there that we could reinvest here.'" She added: "'It is pretty scary to be a small business, especially one that imports so much.'" [Spectrum News 13, [11/5/25](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2025/11/04/scotus-hearing-on-tariffs-case)]

**Yale Budget Lab Found That Tariffs Cost The Average American Household $3,800, With Low-Income Families Hit Hardest.** According to the Yale Budget Lab, all 2025 tariffs raised the price level by 2.3 percent, "equivalent to an average per-household consumer loss of $3,800." For households in the bottom of the income distribution, annual losses were $1,700. [Yale Budget Lab, [4/2/25](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/where-we-stand-fiscal-economic-and-distributional-effects-all-us-tariffs-enacted-2025-through-april)]

### Webster Voted For The "Big Beautiful Bill" That Gutted Food Assistance And Shifted Billions In Costs To Florida

**Webster Voted For The "One Big Beautiful Bill" That Cut $187 Billion From SNAP, The Largest Food Assistance Cut In History.** According to the SPLC, the law "gutted $187 billion in federal SNAP funding through 2034." The Congressional Budget Office estimated roughly 4 million people, including 1 million children, would lose SNAP benefits. [SPLC, [12/26/25](https://www.splcenter.org/resources/hopewatch/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-florida-food-assistance/)]

- **The Law Shifted Up To $1 Billion In New SNAP Costs To Florida Because Of Its High Error Rate.** According to WUSF, the law requires states with high error rates to cover a portion of SNAP benefits the federal government previously paid in full. "Florida's rate last year was 15 percent." State Rep. Karen Gonzalez-Pittman, a Republican, said: "'That's going to cost us billions of dollars. So, is it a badge of courage to say that we have a lean administrative cost if we are going to have to pay billions of dollars out.'" [WUSF, [12/4/25](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-12-04/florida-faces-rising-snap-administration-cost-after-big-beautiful-bill)]

- **More Than 56,000 SNAP Recipients In Webster's Own District Faced Disruption, With Enrollment Surging 60 Percent In Sumter County.** According to the Daily Commercial, more than 50,000 SNAP recipients in Lake County and nearly 6,400 in Sumter County depend on food assistance. SNAP enrollment in Sumter County grew from roughly 4,000 in 2022 to 6,400 in 2025 — a roughly 60 percent increase. [Daily Commercial, [10/30/25](https://www.dailycommercial.com/story/news/local/2025/10/30/how-will-pause-in-snap-disbursements-affect-lake-and-sumter-counties-florida/86961225007/)]

**Florida Food Banks Reported Surging Demand After The Bill's SNAP Cuts Took Effect.** According to Treasure Coast Newspapers, food banks across Florida reported major increases: Mustard Seed Ministries in Fort Pierce served 45,000 so far in 2025 compared to 34,000 the prior year; House of Hope in Stuart served 1.6 million compared to 1.2 million; and GraceWay Village in Fort Pierce saw a 37 percent increase. Treasure Coast Food Bank's Krista Garofalo said: "'We're having lots of people tell us they've never had to seek assistance. The system is hard for them to navigate because they've never done it before.'" [Treasure Coast Newspapers, [10/9/25](https://eu.thedestinlog.com/story/news/local/florida/2025/10/09/inflation-snap-cuts-high-cost-of-living-raise-food-bank-demand-doge-big-beautiful-bill-florida/86375780007/)]

**Yale Budget Lab Found That The "Big Beautiful Bill" Combined With Tariffs Reduced After-Tax Income For The Bottom 80 Percent Of Households, While The Top 10 Percent Got Richer.** According to CNN's analysis of Yale Budget Lab data, "American households could pay $2,300 more on average as a result of current tariff policy -- nearly three times the average ~$800 benefit from the OBBBA's new tax provisions." Yale Budget Lab associate director John Ricco said: "'For households as a whole, the total tax increase they'll feel from tariffs is larger than the total tax cut they'll feel from the new provisions in the Republican tax package.'" [CNN, [9/15/25](https://www.cnn.com/2025/09/15/politics/tariffs-tax-cuts-trump-households-deficit-analysis)]

### Webster Voted Against Lower Prescription Drug Costs And The $15 Minimum Wage

**Webster Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Insulin At $35 A Month And Let Medicare Negotiate Drug Prices.** According to Florida Politics, the Inflation Reduction Act "passed on a strict party line vote" with all Florida Republicans voting against it. The law "empowers Medicare to negotiate fair drug prices directly with companies [...]  caps annual out-of-pocket prescription drug spending for seniors at $2,000 and monthly insulin costs for seniors at $35, while also penalizing pharmaceutical companies for unfair price hikes." [Florida Politics, [8/12/22](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/546772-florida-delegation-reacts-to-passage-of-inflation-reduction-act-along-party-lines/)]

**Webster Then Voted To Repeal The Inflation Reduction Act's Consumer Energy Credits Through The "Big Beautiful Bill."** According to Webster's own official website, the "One Big Beautiful Bill" that he voted for "repeals and phases out every green corporate welfare subsidy in the Democrat's so-called Inflation Reduction Act." Those provisions included tax credits for energy-efficient home improvements, heat pumps, solar panels, and electric vehicles that helped reduce household energy costs. [Webster.house.gov, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://webster.house.gov/obbb)]

**Webster Voted Against A $15 Federal Minimum Wage — A Level Florida Voters Themselves Approved By A Supermajority.** According to the House Clerk, Webster voted against the Raise the Wage Act (H.R. 582) on July 18, 2019, which would have raised the federal minimum wage to $15 an hour. Florida voters later approved a $15 state minimum wage via ballot amendment in November 2020 with 60.8 percent support. [House Clerk, [7/18/19](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2019496); Restaurant Business Online, [11/4/20](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/workforce/florida-voters-approve-15-minimum-wage)]

### Webster Attacked The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

**Webster Compared The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau To "The Gestapo."** According to Slate, at a House Rules Committee hearing, Webster said: "'This is more than just NSA-style, this is more Gestapo-style collection of data on individual citizens who have no clue that this is happening.'" The CFPB is the primary federal agency protecting consumers from predatory lending, unfair fees, and financial fraud. [Slate, [2/11/14](https://slate.com/news-and-politics/2014/02/video-dan-webster-compares-cfpb-to-the-gestapo.html)]

**Webster Voted To Gut Dodd-Frank Consumer Protections Through The Financial CHOICE Act.** According to Webster's office, he voted for the Financial CHOICE Act (H.R. 10), which would have significantly weakened the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and rolled back post-2008-crash financial regulations. Webster said: "'Dodd-Frank regulations disproportionately burden small companies and prevent them from competing.'" [Webster.house.gov, [6/8/17](https://webster.house.gov/press-releases?ID=D746AB28-2207-473A-841D-A0CE063C96C2)]

### Constituents Demanding Accountability — But Webster Refused To Show Up

**More Than 200 Constituents Packed A Town Hall In Webster's District — But He Refused To Attend.** According to Winter Garden Vox, "Orange County Democrats hosted a town hall Monday in Congressman Daniel Webster's District 11 where they blasted the long-serving Republican legislator for not holding his own in-person town halls to face his constituents. More than 200 people — a capacity crowd of Gen Zs to retirees, including a large contingent from The Villages — packed the John Bridges Community Center in Apopka to vent their anger and frustration." [Winter Garden Vox, [4/16/25](https://www.wintergardenvox.com/articles/voters-sound-off-at-webster-district-town-hall)]

[WUSF, [12/29/25](https://www.wusf.org/economy-business/2025-12-29/this-years-bump-to-social-security-benefits-wont-go-far-in-tampa)]
