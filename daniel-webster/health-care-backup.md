# Rep. Daniel Webster (FL-11): Can't Be Trusted on Health Care

Rep. Daniel Webster has spent over a decade working to dismantle health care protections for his own constituents — many of them seniors in one of the oldest congressional districts in the nation. In 2017, he switched his vote at the last minute to pass the American Health Care Act, a bill that would have stripped coverage from 1.8 million Floridians and allowed insurers to charge people with pre-existing conditions more — even though he had promised constituents just months earlier that Congress would "not pull the rug" out from under anyone's coverage. In 2022, he voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, which capped insulin costs at $35/month for Medicare patients and empowered Medicare to negotiate drug prices for the first time. In 2025, he voted for the "One Big Beautiful Bill," which cut $3.8 billion from Florida's health care system, slashed Medicaid — the program that covers 4.3 million Floridians — and is projected to eliminate 17,000 health care jobs statewide and put nearly 1 million Floridians at risk of losing coverage. And in December 2025, he voted for a health care bill that deliberately let ACA subsidies expire — subsidies that had kept premiums affordable for 4.6 million Floridians, the most of any state. When those subsidies expired on January 1, 2026, premiums doubled on average. A 30-year-old Florida woman saw her premium nearly triple, from $900 to $2,500 a month. A Central Florida father of three with diabetes saw his jump from $28 to $733. Webster's response to the fallout? He refused to hold a single in-person town hall.

### Switched His Vote At The Last Minute To Pass A Bill That Threatened 1.8 Million Floridians' Coverage

**Webster's Last-Minute Vote Switch Helped Push The GOP Health Care Bill Over The Top.** According to Villages-News, "Congressman Daniel Webster's decision to vote in favor of a GOP health care bill helped push it to victory in the U.S. House of Representatives." The bill passed 217-213 — Webster had opposed an earlier version of the bill in March, helping doom it. He reversed course after receiving "assurances from President Trump and other leaders" about Florida Medicaid-funded nursing home beds. [Villages-News, [5/4/17](https://www.villages-news.com/2017/05/04/congressman-websters-vote-switch-helps-put-gop-health-care-bill-top/)]

- **The Bill Would Have Stripped Coverage From 1.8 Million Floridians And Allowed Insurers To Charge More For Pre-Existing Conditions.** According to the USA Today Network, "A bitterly divided House on Thursday passed a health care bill that would give Gov. Rick Scott and state legislative leaders long-sought flexibility but also would significantly cut Florida's federal Medicaid and potentially price more than 1 million Floridians out of health coverage." The bill allowed states to let "insurance companies to charge people with pre-existing conditions higher premiums" and "of the 24 million Americans projected by the nonpartisan Congressional Budget Office to lose coverage under the bill for various reasons over the next decade, an estimated 1.8 million live in Florida." [Naples News, [5/4/17](https://www.naplesnews.com/story/news/politics/2017/05/04/health-care-bill-passes-partisan-florida-split/101297766/)]

- **Webster Called The ACA A "Nightmare" And Declared He Voted "To End" It.** According to a press release from Webster's office, "Today, I voted to end the nightmare that has been the [un]Affordable Care Act(ACA) and to provide Americans with the care they need, at a price they can afford, from the doctor they choose. For six years, I have been an advocate for repealing the failed Obamacare and replacing it with real healthcare reform." [Webster.house.gov, [5/4/17](https://webster.house.gov/2017/5/statement-from-fl-rep-daniel-webster-on-passage-of-american-health-care-act)]

### Promised Not To "Pull The Rug" — Then Did Exactly That

**Webster Told Constituents In 2017 That Congress Would Not "Pull The Rug" Out On Health Care Coverage.** According to Florida Politics, "In his newsletter to constituents of Florida's 11th Congressional District [...]  Webster pressed his assurances that the Affordable Care Act could be replaced." Webster wrote: "'The House is working on a plan to provide Americans with the care they need, from the doctor they choose, at a price they can afford. We do not intend to pull the rug of coverage and care out from underneath anyone.'" [Florida Politics, [1/18/17](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/230508-daniel-webster-congress-will-not-pull-rug-health-care-coverage/)]

### Voted To Let ACA Subsidies Expire — Hitting Florida Hardest Of Any State

**Webster Voted For Health Care Bill That Did Not Extend ACA Subsidies Helping Millions Afford Insurance.** According to Villages-News, "Congressman Daniel Webster has voted to pass H.R. 6703, the Lower Health Care Premiums for All Americans Act that does not include an extension of expiring tax credits that help millions of Americans pay for health insurance under the Affordable Care Act." [Villages-News, [12/18/25](https://www.villages-news.com/2025/12/18/webster-votes-for-gop-health-care-bill-without-obamacare-extension/)]

- **Webster Framed The Bill As A Fix While Letting Subsidies For 4.7 Million Floridians Expire.** According to a press release from Webster's office, Webster said: "'American families don't need another subsidy for health insurance companies, they need a health care system that's affordable and offers real choices.'" He called the bill "a real solution" — but it contained no extension of the tax credits that had kept premiums affordable for millions. [Webster.house.gov, [12/18/25](https://webster.house.gov/2025/12/webster-votes-to-fix-obamacare-s-broken-health-care-system)]

**Florida Had The Largest Number Of ACA Enrollees Of Any State — 4.7 Million — Making It The Hardest Hit By Subsidy Expiration.** According to the Palm Beach Post, "No other state has a greater percentage of people — about 4.6 million Floridians, or 25% of those under 65 — getting their health care insurance through the government-supported Health Insurance Marketplace created by the 2010 Affordable Care Act." The Florida Hospital Association warned "that cessation will put 1 million Floridians at risk for losing access to health insurance coverage." [Palm Beach Post, [11/11/25](https://www.palmbeachpost.com/story/news/healthcare/2025/11/11/obamacare-insurance-subsidies-expire-florida-affected/87193868007/)]

**When Subsidies Expired On January 1, 2026, Premiums Doubled On Average For More Than 20 Million Enrollees.** According to CBS News, "Enhanced tax credits that have helped Americans offset the cost of Affordable Care Act health insurance for the last four years expired overnight." On average, "the more than 20 million subsidized enrollees in the Affordable Care Act program are seeing their premium costs rise by 114% in 2026." [CBS News, [1/1/26](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/aca-health-subsidies-insurance-prices/)]

- **Experts Warned The Expiration Would Cause A "Huge Premium Shock" For 22 Million People.** According to CNBC, "The disappearance of these enhanced premium tax credits — a so-called 'subsidy cliff' — would cause average premiums to rise by about 75%, according to KFF, a nonpartisan health policy research group. That would amount to more than $700 in additional premium payments per year, on average." KFF's executive vice president said: "'For those 22 million people, it would be a huge premium shock on New Year's Day if these tax credits expire.'" [CNBC, [9/10/25](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/10/aca-enhanced-subsidies-expire-obamacare-premiums-rise.html)]

- **Health Experts Said The Crisis Hit Floridians Aged 55-64 Hardest — The Core Of Webster's District.** According to WUSF, advocates warned that "more than 1 million Floridians could be priced out of marketplace plans." KFF Health News chief Washington correspondent Julie Rovner said the crisis stems from a dramatic shift: "'The federal government compared to the share that's expected to be paid by the consumer is dramatically changing.'" Those aged 55-64 were "being hit the hardest" — a demographic heavily concentrated in Webster's Villages-heavy district. [WUSF, [1/12/26](https://www.wusf.org/health-news-florida/2026-01-12/aca-enrollment-deadline-arrives-health-expert-adds-context-subsidy-conundrum)]

### Voted For "Big Beautiful Bill" That Cut $3.8 Billion From Florida Health Care

**Webster Voted For The "One Big Beautiful Bill" That Cut $3.8 Billion From Florida's Health Care System.** According to Florida Politics, "President Donald Trump's 'Big Beautiful Bill' will cut $3.8 billion from Florida's health care system, with that money primarily affecting Florida hospitals." Florida's Medicaid director said the Hospital Directed Payment Program "will face a $3.5 billion cut by 2034." [Florida Politics, [10/8/25](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/759859-donald-trumps-big-beautiful-bill-means-3-8b-cut-to-florida-health-care-system/)]

- **The Bill Passed 215-214 With Webster Voting Yes Along Party Lines.** According to Florida Politics, "The U.S. House early Thursday passed a tax package, dubbed President Donald Trump's 'One Big Beautiful Bill.' The measure narrowly passed on a 215-214 vote, with Florida's congressional delegation breaking along straight party lines." [Florida Politics, [5/22/25](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/739429-florida-congressional-delegation-touts-condemns-house-passage-of-big-beautiful-bill/)]

- **The Law Slashed Medicaid, Which Covers 4.3 Million Floridians, And Was Projected To Eliminate 17,000 Health Care Jobs Statewide.** According to WUSF, "In Florida, more than 4.3 million people are enrolled in Medicaid. But the bill could have bigger impacts on more than just the enrollees." Georgetown University's Joan Alker said, "'It's going to result in eligible people who need health care not being able to get it.'" The Commonwealth Fund projected the cuts would eliminate approximately 17,000 health care jobs at hospitals, clinics, pharmacies, nursing homes, and community health centers across Florida, with another 16,000 jobs lost in other sectors. [WUSF, [6/12/25](https://wusf.org/text/politics-issues/2025-06-12/how-trump-big-beautiful-bill-act-could-impact-medicaid-for-floridians); Commonwealth Fund, [6/25](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/jun/how-medicaid-snap-cutbacks-one-big-beautiful-bill-trigger-job-losses-states)]

- **The Medicaid Cuts Were Projected To Put A $3 Billion Hole In Florida's Economy.** According to WUSF, research from the Commonwealth Fund projected "a $3 billion reduction in Florida's gross domestic product" from Medicaid cuts alone, with an estimated "$177 million in local tax revenue" jeopardized. Health policy researcher Leighton Ku warned that if federal Medicaid funding is significantly reduced, healthcare providers will need to "lay people off" and that "nursing homes, long-term care facilities, and community health centers could face closure." [WUSF, [4/8/25](https://www.wusf.org/health-news-florida/2025-04-08/medicaid-cuts-could-put-3-billion-hole-florida-economy-study)]

### Voted Against Lower Prescription Drug Costs

The law "empowers Medicare to negotiate fair drug prices directly with companies [...]  caps annual out-of-pocket prescription drug spending for seniors at $2,000 and monthly insulin costs for seniors at $35, while also penalizing pharmaceutical companies for unfair price hikes." [Florida Politics, [8/12/22](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/546772-florida-delegation-reacts-to-passage-of-inflation-reduction-act-along-party-lines/)]
