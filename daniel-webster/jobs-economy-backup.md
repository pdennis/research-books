# Rep. Daniel Webster (FL-11): Can't Be Trusted on Jobs and the Economy

Rep. Daniel Webster talks about fighting for "hardworking Americans," but his record tells a different story. Webster is ranked 225th out of 228 House Republicans in legislative effectiveness — with zero bills making it even to committee in the 118th Congress — and has an 11 percent lifetime score from the AFL-CIO. He backed Trump's tariffs that a nonpartisan Florida watchdog says will raise grocery bills 10 to 15 percent and add $10,000 to the cost of a new home, then voted to keep tariffs on Canada even as six fellow Republicans broke ranks. He voted for the "One Big Beautiful Bill" that gutted $187 billion from food assistance — affecting more than 56,000 SNAP recipients in his own district — while repealing consumer energy credits. He voted against raising the federal minimum wage to $15, a level Florida voters themselves approved by a supermajority. He blocked farmworkers from unionizing, voted to make it easier for companies to misclassify workers as independent contractors, and compared the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau to "the Gestapo." Meanwhile, his district has been hit by major job losses — 1,400 workers at a Kroger fulfillment center in Groveland and 500 at Frito-Lay in Orlando — while Sumter County's unemployment rate has nearly doubled. When more than 200 constituents packed a town hall to confront him, Webster refused to show up.

### One Of The Least Effective Members Of Congress On Any Issue — Including Jobs

**Webster Was Ranked 225th Out Of 228 House Republicans In Legislative Effectiveness.** According to the Tallahassee Democrat, "With an LES of 0.043 and a rank of 225 out of 228 Republican House members, U.S. Rep. Daniel Webster, R-Florida, was once again ranked the least effective Florida legislator in the 118th Congress." The Center for Effective Lawmaking found that "Webster only sponsored five bills, the CEL said, none of them significant and none of them making it to committee." [Tallahassee Democrat, [3/27/25](https://www.tallahassee.com/story/news/politics/2025/03/27/daniel-webster-jared-moskowitz-florida-least-effective-congress/82667620007/)]

- **Webster Missed More Than Twice The Median Rate Of Votes For House Members.** According to the Tallahassee Democrat, citing GovTrack data, "Webster missed 446 of 8,609 roll call votes from January 2011 to March 2025, or 5.2% of the total, far more than the median of 1.9% among the lifetime records of current active Representatives." [Tallahassee Democrat, [3/27/25](https://www.tallahassee.com/story/news/politics/2025/03/27/daniel-webster-jared-moskowitz-florida-least-effective-congress/82667620007/)]

**Webster Had A Lifetime Score Of 11 Percent From The AFL-CIO, Voting Against Working People On Nearly Every Key Vote.** According to the AFL-CIO Congressional Scorecard, Webster scored 9 percent in 2024 and 11 percent over his lifetime. The scorecard tracked votes on worker safety regulations, overtime protections, union rights, minimum wage, and health care access — and Webster voted against working people on virtually every one. [AFL-CIO, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://aflcio.org/scorecard/legislators/daniel-webster)]

### Backed Trump's Tariffs That Are Driving Up Costs For Florida Families

**Webster Endorsed Trump's Tariffs As "A Negotiation Tool" In An Op-Ed Published In His Own District's Newspaper.** According to Villages-News, Webster wrote: "President Trump is using tariffs -- and the threat of tariffs -- as a negotiation tool to force bad actors like China and Venezuela and enforcing fair trade [...]  I support President Trump's efforts to defend American industries and put American workers first." [Villages-News, [4/8/25](https://www.villages-news.com/2025/04/08/congressman-webster-wants-to-hear-from-small-businesses-hurt-by-tariffs/)]

**Webster Voted To Sustain Trump's Tariffs On Canada As Six Fellow Republicans Broke Ranks To Oppose Them.** According to CNN, "The House voted 219-211 to pass a Democratic-led resolution to terminate the national emergency Trump used to impose tariffs on Canada." Six Republicans voted with Democrats to rescind the tariffs. Webster was not among them — he voted to keep the tariffs in place. [CNN, [2/11/26](https://www.cnn.com/2026/02/11/politics/house-republicans-trump-canada-tariff-vote)]

**Florida TaxWatch Found That Tariffs Would Raise Grocery Bills 10 To 15 Percent And Add $10,000 To The Price Of A New Home.** According to Florida Politics, "Florida ranks fifth among the U.S. in grocery costs. An average Floridian's weekly grocery bill is expected to increase by 10 to 15 percent as a result of imposed tariffs." The report also found: "Higher construction costs can increase the price of a typical house built by Florida developers by $10,000. An increase in the cost of production in the automobile industry will lead to an increase in the price of a car, by at least $6,000-$7,000." [Florida Politics, [7/8/25](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/746357-florida-taxwatch-state-consumers-will-see-higher-prices-as-a-result-of-donald-trumps-trade-tariffs/)]

**A Central Florida Small Business Reported Paying "Almost Double" In Tariffs, Cutting Into Its Ability To Create Local Jobs.** According to Spectrum News 13, Rebecca Dobo, director of operations at Freshorize USA in Winter Springs, said: "'We are probably paying almost double what we have historically in tariffs, which has significantly cut into our bottom line and limits our capability to bring manufacturing into the states because now that profit is not there that we could reinvest here.'" She added: "'It is pretty scary to be a small business, especially one that imports so much.'" [Spectrum News 13, [11/5/25](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2025/11/04/scotus-hearing-on-tariffs-case)]

### Voted To Gut Food Assistance For His Own Constituents

**Webster Voted For The "One Big Beautiful Bill" That Cut $187 Billion From SNAP, The Largest Food Assistance Cut In History.** According to the SPLC, the law "gutted $187 billion in federal SNAP funding through 2034." The Congressional Budget Office estimated roughly 4 million people, including 1 million children, would lose SNAP benefits. [SPLC, [12/26/25](https://www.splcenter.org/resources/hopewatch/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-florida-food-assistance/)]

- **More Than 56,000 SNAP Recipients In Webster's Own District Faced Disruption.** According to the Daily Commercial, more than 50,000 SNAP recipients in Lake County and nearly 6,400 in Sumter County depend on food assistance. SNAP enrollment in Sumter County grew from roughly 4,000 in 2022 to 6,400 in 2025 — a roughly 60 percent increase. [Daily Commercial, [10/30/25](https://www.dailycommercial.com/story/news/local/2025/10/30/how-will-pause-in-snap-disbursements-affect-lake-and-sumter-counties-florida/86961225007/)]

- **The Same Law Shifted Up To $1 Billion In New SNAP Costs To Florida.** According to WUSF, the law requires states with high error rates to cover a portion of SNAP benefits the federal government previously paid in full. "Florida's rate last year was 15 percent." State Rep. Karen Gonzalez-Pittman, a Republican, said: "'That's going to cost us billions of dollars.'" [WUSF, [12/4/25](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-12-04/florida-faces-rising-snap-administration-cost-after-big-beautiful-bill)]

### Voted Against A $15 Minimum Wage That Florida Voters Approved By A Supermajority

**Webster Voted Against The Raise The Wage Act, Which Would Have Raised The Federal Minimum Wage To $15 An Hour.** According to the House Clerk, Webster voted against H.R. 582, the Raise the Wage Act, on July 18, 2019. The bill would have gradually raised the federal minimum wage from $7.25 to $15 per hour. Florida voters later approved a $15 state minimum wage via ballot amendment in November 2020 with 60.8 percent support — in a state that simultaneously went for Trump. [House Clerk, [7/18/19](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2019496); Restaurant Business Online, [11/4/20](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/workforce/florida-voters-approve-15-minimum-wage)]

### Repeatedly Sided With Corporations Over Workers

**Webster Introduced Legislation To Block Farmworkers From Unionizing.** According to a press release from Webster's office, he introduced legislation to block a Department of Labor rule that would have allowed temporary agricultural employees to organize. Webster said: "'The finalized rule from Biden's Department of Labor is a clear attack on the agriculture industry.'" [Webster.house.gov, [5/3/24](https://webster.house.gov/2024/5/webster-introduces-legislation-to-block-biden-s-agricultural-labor-regulation)]

**Webster Voted To Make It Easier For Companies To Misclassify Workers As Independent Contractors.** According to the House Report, Webster supported H.J. Res. 116, a Congressional Review Act resolution to overturn a Department of Labor rule on employee vs. independent contractor classification under the Fair Labor Standards Act. The rule had made it harder for companies to misclassify employees as independent contractors, denying them minimum wage protections, overtime pay, and benefits. [House Report 118-445, [4/5/24](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-118hrpt445/html/CRPT-118hrpt445.htm)]

**Webster Voted To Weaken Joint Employer Protections That Hold Companies Accountable For Workers They Control.** According to a press release from Webster's office, he voted for H.J. Res. 98, which overturned a National Labor Relations Board rule holding companies liable for employees they control but do not directly employ. Webster framed the vote as helping small businesses, but the rule had been designed to prevent large corporations from evading responsibility for working conditions by using subcontractors and franchisees. [Webster.house.gov, [1/12/24](https://webster.house.gov/2024/1/rep-webster-votes-to-protect-small-businesses-from-burdensome-regulation)]

### Attacked The Agency That Protects Consumers From Financial Fraud

**Webster Compared The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau To "The Gestapo."** According to Slate, at a House Rules Committee hearing, Webster said: "'This is more than just NSA-style, this is more Gestapo-style collection of data on individual citizens who have no clue that this is happening.'" The CFPB is the primary federal agency protecting consumers from predatory lending, unfair fees, and financial fraud. [Slate, [2/11/14](https://slate.com/news-and-politics/2014/02/video-dan-webster-compares-cfpb-to-the-gestapo.html)]

**Webster Voted To Gut Dodd-Frank Consumer Protections Through The Financial CHOICE Act.** According to Webster's office, he voted for the Financial CHOICE Act (H.R. 10), which would have significantly weakened the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and rolled back post-2008-crash financial regulations. Webster said: "'Dodd-Frank regulations disproportionately burden small companies and prevent them from competing.'" [Webster.house.gov, [6/8/17](https://webster.house.gov/press-releases?ID=D746AB28-2207-473A-841D-A0CE063C96C2)]

### Voted Against Lower Prescription Drug Costs And Consumer Energy Savings

**Webster Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Insulin At $35 A Month And Let Medicare Negotiate Drug Prices.** According to Florida Politics, the Inflation Reduction Act "passed on a strict party line vote" with all Florida Republicans voting against it. The law "empowers Medicare to negotiate fair drug prices directly with companies [...]  caps annual out-of-pocket prescription drug spending for seniors at $2,000 and monthly insulin costs for seniors at $35." [Florida Politics, [8/12/22](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/546772-florida-delegation-reacts-to-passage-of-inflation-reduction-act-along-party-lines/)]

**Webster Then Voted To Repeal The Inflation Reduction Act's Consumer Energy Credits Through The "Big Beautiful Bill."** According to Webster's own official website, the "One Big Beautiful Bill" that he voted for "repeals and phases out every green corporate welfare subsidy in the Democrat's so-called Inflation Reduction Act." Those provisions included tax credits for energy-efficient home improvements, heat pumps, solar panels, and electric vehicles that helped reduce household energy costs. [Webster.house.gov, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://webster.house.gov/obbb)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**Kroger Closed Its Groveland Fulfillment Center In Webster's District, Eliminating 1,400 Jobs.** According to the Orlando Sentinel, "National grocery retailer Kroger Co. announced plans this week to close its eCommerce fulfillment center in Groveland, a move that will lead to the loss of about 1,400 Florida jobs." The closing impacted about 935 employees at the facility and nearly 500 delivery drivers across Florida. The City of Groveland said: "'This is an especially difficult time for employees to hear this news, and our hearts go out to those affected.'" Groveland and Lake County had offered Kroger more than $3 million in financial incentives to locate there in 2019. [Orlando Sentinel, [11/18/25](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2025/11/18/kroger-closing-groveland-automated-facility-impacting-nearly-1400-workers/)]

- **Frito-Lay Also Shuttered Two Orlando Facilities, Eliminating 500 Jobs.** According to the Orlando Sentinel, "Food giant Frito-Lay — maker of the popular snacks Cheetos, Doritos and Lay's potato chips — announced Tuesday it will shutter two of its manufacturing and warehouse facilities in Orlando, eliminating 500 jobs." PepsiCo called the closing "a difficult decision" driven by "business needs" as consumers cut back on spending. The plant had been operating since 1965. [Orlando Sentinel, [11/5/25](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2025/11/05/frito-lay-eliminates-500-jobs-as-it-closes-orlando-plants/)]

**Sumter County's Unemployment Rate Nearly Doubled Year-Over-Year To 7.9 Percent.** According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Sumter County's unemployment rate was 7.9 percent in December 2025, a 2.5 percentage point increase from December 2024. The county sits in Webster's district and is home to The Villages, the largest retirement community in the country. [Bureau of Labor Statistics via USAFacts, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://usafacts.org/answers/what-is-the-unemployment-rate/county/sumter-county-fl/)]

**Florida Food Banks Reported Surging Demand After The "Big Beautiful Bill" SNAP Cuts Took Effect.** According to Treasure Coast Newspapers, food banks across Florida reported major increases: Mustard Seed Ministries in Fort Pierce served 45,000 people so far in 2025 compared to 34,000 the prior year; House of Hope in Stuart served 1.6 million compared to 1.2 million; and GraceWay Village in Fort Pierce saw a 37 percent increase. Krista Garofalo of the Treasure Coast Food Bank said: "'We're having lots of people tell us they've never had to seek assistance. The system is hard for them to navigate because they've never done it before.'" [Treasure Coast Newspapers, [10/9/25](https://eu.thedestinlog.com/story/news/local/florida/2025/10/09/inflation-snap-cuts-high-cost-of-living-raise-food-bank-demand-doge-big-beautiful-bill-florida/86375780007/)]

**A 69-Year-Old Tampa Woman On $1,000 A Month In Social Security Said "Everything Is So, So Ridiculous High."** According to WUSF, people 65 and older represent the largest group struggling to afford basic needs across Central Florida. A 69-year-old on $1,000 a month in Social Security and $100 in SNAP said: "'Everything is so, so ridiculous high. You're gonna buy a pack of eggs, and it's ridiculous.'" [WUSF, [12/29/25](https://www.wusf.org/economy-business/2025-12-29/this-years-bump-to-social-security-benefits-wont-go-far-in-tampa)]
