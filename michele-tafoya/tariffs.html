Trump’s Tariffs Wreaked Havoc On Minnesotans

Trump’s Tariffs Deprived Minnesota Farmers Of Export Orders

Trump’s Tariffs Deprived Minnesota Farmers Of Export Orders. According to the Minnesota Star Tribune, “It’s a bad year for a good soybean harvest as tariffs decimate export orders. Minnesota farmers were in the nation’s capital this week pleading for a bailout. Historically, Minnesota sends two out of every three rows of soybeans overseas, typically to feed livestock. China has often bought the bulk of those exports. As of early September, Chinese buyers have yet to book any shipments of American soybeans from the upcoming harvest. That’s a far cry from the 12 million to 13 million tons they’d booked by this time last year, traders told Reuters this week. […] Because of the trade war with China, U.S. soybeans cost about 20% more than South American beans. That’s due to trade duties imposed by the Chinese government in response to President Donald Trump’s increased tariffs on goods bound for the U.S. market.” [Minnesota Star Tribune, 9/11/25]

Tariffs Were Ballooning Grocery Costs In Minnesota

Tariffs Were Ballooning Grocery Costs. According to KARE, “At Colonial Market in Bloomington, owner Danny Hernandez says the pressures facing grocery stores have intensified in recent months, and shoppers are noticing it at the checkout line. ‘Less than three weeks ago, we paid $15.20 for a box of tomatoes… now it’s $75,’ Hernandez said during a tour of the store with Congresswoman Kelly Morrison. ‘That’s because of tariffs.’ […] President Donald Trump has repeatedly defended both his tariff policies and immigration operations, saying the economic impacts are temporary and prices will eventually come down. Meanwhile, Hernandez says families are already being forced to make difficult choices in grocery aisles right now.” [KARE, 5/22/26]

Trump’s Tariff Whiplash Threw Minnesota Businesses Into Uncertainty

Trump’s Tariff Whiplash Threw Minnesota Businesses Into Uncertainty. According to Twin Cities Business Magazine, “It took President Trump almost six weeks to reduce the high tariffs on China that he announced with much fanfare April 2 in a Rose Garden ceremony. […] The reality that American consumers will pay higher prices was brought into sharp focus May 15 when Walmart announced it was raising prices on products affected by tariffs. While Trump deployed some tariffs in his first term and talked about tariffs during the 2024 presidential campaign, the magnitude of tariffs he imposed across the world this year caught many U.S. businesses by surprise. Emily LeVasseur, president of the Association for Supply Chain Management-Twin Cities chapter, says she’s been seeing “a paralysis in strategic decision-making” among several Minnesota businesses. ‘Everybody is kind of stepping back and saying, “OK, we need to pause. We need to let this shake out before we make any significant investments in our supply chain, in our cost structures.” It’s really hampering decision-making because of that uncertainty,’ LeVasseur says.” [Twin Cities Business Magazine, 6/6/25]

Trump’s Tariffs Led To Layoffs In The Minnesota Steel Industry He Claimed To Protect

Trump’s Tariffs Led To Layoffs In The Minnesota Steel Industry He Claimed To Protect. According to MPR News, “About 600 Iron Range mine workers could be laid off by May, potentially spending six months off the job. This comes a little over a week after President Donald Trump imposed 25 percent tariffs on all steel and aluminum imports and Canada retaliated with its own 25 percent tax. […] ‘There is going to be demand for steel, and the Iron Range is where you get the iron ore in this country,’ he said. ‘They’re used to shutdowns. It’s just, somewhat ironic it’s happening at a time when tariffs are supposedly protecting the steel industry.’” [MPR News, 3/21/25]
