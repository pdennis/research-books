# Rep. Dan Meuser (PA-09): Health Care

Rep. Dan Meuser has repeatedly voted against legislation that would lower health care costs for his constituents, then claimed the programs he opposed don't help anyone. Meuser voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, which capped insulin costs at $35/month for Medicare recipients and authorized Medicare to negotiate prescription drug prices for the first time. He voted against extending ACA premium subsidies that kept insurance affordable for tens of thousands of Pennsylvanians in his district — and when those subsidies expired, 85,000 Pennsylvanians lost coverage statewide, with rural counties like those in PA-09 hit hardest. Meuser then voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which the Shapiro administration estimates will strip Medicaid from nearly 18,000 people in his district alone. When confronted with these numbers, Meuser dismissed the projections as "complete, utter nonsense."

### Voted Against Lower Prescription Drug Costs

**Meuser Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Medicare Prescription Drug Costs And Authorized Drug Price Negotiation.** According to a press release from Rep. Meuser's office, he voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, calling it "The Recession Extension Act" and arguing it "raises taxes on the American people, adds 87,000 new IRS agents, and appropriates over half a trillion dollars in new government spending." The law capped Medicare Part D out-of-pocket costs at $2,000 annually, capped insulin at $35/month for Medicare recipients, and authorized Medicare to negotiate drug prices for the first time. [Meuser.house.gov, [8/12/22](https://meuser.house.gov/media/press-releases/congressman-dan-meuser-votes-no-vote-inflation-reduction-act)]

### Voted Against Affordable Health Insurance

**Meuser Voted Against Extending ACA Premium Subsidies That Kept Insurance Affordable For His Constituents.** According to WVIA, Rep. Dan Meuser was among Pennsylvania Republicans who voted against extending enhanced ACA premium tax credits. The House passed the three-year extension 230-196 in January 2026 with 17 Republicans crossing party lines, but Meuser voted no. Without the subsidies, Pennie enrollees faced an average premium increase of 102%. [WVIA, [2/14/26](https://www.wvia.org/news/local/2026-02-14/1-in-5-pennie-enrollees-drop-health-insurance-after-expiration-of-affordable-care-act-tax-credits)]

**After Subsidies Meuser Opposed Expired, 85,000 Pennsylvanians Dropped Health Coverage — With Rural Counties Hit Hardest.** According to the Pennsylvania Health Insurance Exchange Authority, "Terminations were highest among older and rural Pennsylvanians, and those with incomes just above Medicaid or above the new income cliff." Fifteen of the top twenty counties by proportional disenrollment were rural counties — the kind of communities that make up much of PA-09. Roughly 79,500 new enrollees signed up, but new enrollment was 12% lower than the prior year. [Pennsylvania Health Insurance Exchange Authority, [2/9/26](https://agency.pennie.com/one-in-five-pennie-enrollees-drop-health-coverage-due-to-expired-federal-tax-credits/)]

  - **Berwick Resident Said She Would Skip Doctor Visits Because Of Higher Costs After Subsidies Expired.** According to WVIA, Jeanne Briggs, a Berwick resident and diabetic working in customer service, previously paid $0 monthly under the enhanced credits but now pays $17 plus a $35 copay — up from $20 previously. She said: "'I probably won't go, unless I can come up with $35 before I go.'" [WVIA, [2/14/26](https://www.wvia.org/news/local/2026-02-14/1-in-5-pennie-enrollees-drop-health-insurance-after-expiration-of-affordable-care-act-tax-credits)]

### Voted For Medicaid Cuts That Will Strip Coverage From Nearly 18,000 In His District

**Meuser Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill, Which The Shapiro Administration Estimated Would Strip Medicaid From 17,967 People In PA-09.** According to a fact sheet from the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, 17,967 Pennsylvanians in Rep. Meuser's 9th Congressional District will lose Medicaid coverage under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, and 8,185 will lose SNAP benefits. Statewide, approximately 310,000 Pennsylvanians could lose Medicaid and 144,000 could lose SNAP. [Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, [7/3/25](https://www.pa.gov/governor/newsroom/2025-press-releases/fact-sheet--how-many-lose-medicaid-and-snap-as-a-result-of-recon)]

**Meuser Dismissed Projections That His Constituents Would Lose Coverage As 'Complete, Utter Nonsense.'** According to ABC27, Meuser called Governor Shapiro's projections about coverage losses "complete, utter nonsense," arguing "It's not taking into account there are illegals on the rolls, there are those that are ineligible." He also attacked Shapiro personally, saying he was "a person running the fifth-largest state and not bringing any management skills or accountability, or even questioning the current status quo." [ABC27, [7/3/25](https://www.abc27.com/pennsylvania-politics/meuser-fires-back-at-shapiro-after-medicare-snap-callout/)]

**Meuser Claimed The Bill 'Strengthens Medicaid' Despite $20 Billion In Projected Cuts To Pennsylvania.** According to a press release from Meuser's office, he stated the bill "strengthens and sustains Medicaid benefits for those who truly need them" through work requirements and eligibility verification. But according to WESA and Spotlight PA, Pennsylvania expects to lose approximately $20 billion in federal Medicaid funding over 10 years starting in 2028, and a Hospital and Healthsystem Association of Pennsylvania analysis estimates "a dozen more facilities could close over the next five years without more help from policymakers." [Meuser.house.gov, [7/3/25](https://meuser.house.gov/media/press-releases/congressman-meuser-votes-send-one-big-beautiful-bill-president-trumps-desk); Spotlight PA, [2/15/26](https://www.spotlightpa.org/statecollege/2026/02/pennsylvania-rural-hospitals-medicaid-cuts-closure-health/)]

### Rural Health Care Access Already Under Threat In PA-09

**Healthcare Advocates Warned Medicaid Cuts Could Trigger Hospital Closures In The Region Meuser Represents.** According to WVIA, Robin Stelly of the PA Health Access Network warned that "If even one of them closes, that will overwhelm the remaining providers and lead to dangerously longer wait times, reduced access to care and worse outcomes." The report noted that approximately 300,000 Pennsylvania adults ages 18-65 could lose Medicaid — "about one in four people in that age group statewide." [WVIA, [5/21/25](https://www.wvia.org/news/local/2025-05-21/healthcare-activists-warn-medicaid-cuts-will-hurt-all-pennsylvanians-prompt-hospital-closures-in-region)]

**Hospital Association President Said 'Less Than Half' Of Pennsylvania's Hospitals Have Margins Necessary For Long-Term Stability.** According to Spotlight PA, Nicole Stallings, president and CEO of the Hospital and Healthsystem Association of Pennsylvania, said "At a time when less than half of Pennsylvania's hospitals have margins necessary for long-term stability, the status quo is not enough." The report noted that rural hospitals face structural challenges including older patient populations, heavy reliance on government-funded insurance with lower reimbursement rates, and Medicaid rates that have not kept pace with inflation. [Spotlight PA, [2/15/26](https://www.spotlightpa.org/statecollege/2026/02/pennsylvania-rural-hospitals-medicaid-cuts-closure-health/)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**Berwick, Pennsylvania Diabetic Faced Impossible Choice Between Paying For Prescriptions And Seeing Her Doctor.** According to WVIA, Jeanne Briggs of Berwick — a community in Columbia County within PA-09 — said she would likely skip doctor visits after her costs rose following the expiration of the subsidies Meuser voted against extending. As a diabetic requiring regular checkups, she said: "'I probably won't go, unless I can come up with $35 before I go.'" [WVIA, [2/14/26](https://www.wvia.org/news/local/2026-02-14/1-in-5-pennie-enrollees-drop-health-insurance-after-expiration-of-affordable-care-act-tax-credits)]

**Rural Pennsylvanians Who Lost Coverage After Subsidy Expiration Faced Already-Limited Health Care Options.** According to the Pennsylvania Health Insurance Exchange Authority, "Access to healthcare is already limited in rural counties, [and] many in these areas relied on enhanced premium tax credits to afford the higher premiums often seen in less populated areas." Fifteen of the top twenty counties with the highest proportional disenrollment were rural. [Pennsylvania Health Insurance Exchange Authority, [2/9/26](https://agency.pennie.com/one-in-five-pennie-enrollees-drop-health-coverage-due-to-expired-federal-tax-credits/)]
