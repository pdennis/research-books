# Rep. Jay Obernolte (CA-23): Can't Be Trusted on Affordability

Rep. Jay Obernolte claims to be a fiscal hawk fighting inflation, but his voting record tells a different story. Obernolte voted to shield Trump's tariffs from congressional oversight — tariffs that economists project will cost the average American household $1,700 per year in higher prices, with the poorest families hit hardest. When the House voted in February 2026 to overturn Trump's 25% tariffs on Canada, Obernolte sided with Trump over his constituents. He also voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which enacted the largest SNAP cuts in the program's history — projected to strip CalFresh benefits from 395,000 Californians and cut $2.3 to $5.1 billion in annual federal food assistance funding. Obernolte's district is one of the least affordable in inland California: the median household income in San Bernardino County falls 34% short of what a family of four needs for basic expenses, and the Inland Empire's logistics-dependent economy is directly threatened by tariff-driven trade disruptions. Obernolte talks about affordability, but he has consistently voted to make life more expensive for his own constituents.

### Voted to Shield Trump's Tariffs From Congressional Oversight

**Obernolte Initially Voted Against a Rule Blocking Tariff Disapproval Votes, Then Flipped to "Yes" Under Pressure From GOP Leadership.** According to The Dispatch, Obernolte was among six Republicans who "originally voted against the rule" that extended a prohibition on votes to rescind Trump's tariff emergency declaration until March 2026, but leadership "had to flip enough of the holdouts to advance the measure, getting Bacon, Obernolte, and McClintock to vote 'yes.'" The measure passed 213-211. [Dispatch, [9/16/25](https://thedispatch.com/article/congress-house-republicans-tariff-resolutions/)]
- **Obernolte Refused to Explain Why He Changed His Vote.** According to the Washington Times, when asked about his initial "no" vote and subsequent flip, Obernolte said, "We'll leave that between us and our conference leadership. I don't think there's any purpose that's served by airing those discussions publicly." [Washington Times, [9/16/25](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/sep/16/house-move-turn-lawmakers-authority-overturn-trump-tariffs-causes/)]

**Obernolte Voted Against a Bipartisan Resolution to Overturn Trump's 25% Tariffs on Canada.** According to NBC News, the House passed a joint resolution to terminate Trump's tariffs on Canadian goods by a vote of 219-211, with six Republicans crossing party lines to support it. Obernolte was not among them, voting to keep the tariffs in place despite their impact on consumer prices and the Inland Empire's trade-dependent economy. [NBC News, [2/11/26](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/house-votes-end-trump-tariffs-canada-rcna258661)]

### Backed Tariffs That Economists Say Hit Low-Income Families Hardest

**Economists Projected Trump's Tariffs Would Cost The Average Household $1,700 Per Year In Higher Prices.** According to the Yale Budget Lab, "the price level rises by 1.2% in the short run, representing a loss of $1,700 for the average household and $900 for households at the bottom of the income distribution." The tariffs fall most heavily on apparel, electronics, and motor vehicles, with clothing prices projected to increase up to 29%. [Yale Budget Lab, [11/17/25](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/state-us-tariffs-november-17-2025)]
- **Tariffs Are a Regressive Tax That Hits the Poorest Families Three Times Harder Than the Wealthiest.** According to the Yale Budget Lab, the tariff burden "expressed as a share of post-tax-and-transfer income on the first decile is more than three times that of the top decile (2.4% versus 0.8%)." [Yale Budget Lab, [11/17/25](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/state-us-tariffs-november-17-2025)]

**The Federal Reserve Found Tariffs Were Already Driving Up Prices on Everyday Goods by Mid-2025.** According to the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, "tariff measures are already exerting measurable upward pressure on consumer prices," with household appliances 3.9% above trend, furniture 3.1% higher, and electronics 5.7% higher as of August 2025. [Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, [10/25](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2025/oct/how-tariffs-are-affecting-prices-2025)]

### Voted for the Largest SNAP Cuts in the Program's History

**Obernolte Voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Which Enacted the Most Severe Cuts to Food Assistance in SNAP's History.** According to the California Association of Food Banks, the law includes "the most severe cuts to SNAP — known as CalFresh in California — in the program's history," including expanded work requirements, frozen benefit levels despite rising food costs, eliminated nutrition education programs, and reduced utility cost allowances. [California Association of Food Banks, [7/3/25](https://www.cafoodbanks.org/statement/congress-slashes-snap/)]

**The OBBBA Is Projected to Strip CalFresh Benefits From 395,000 Californians and Cut Up to $5.1 Billion in Annual Federal Food Assistance.** According to the California Association of Food Banks, the law will cut federal CalFresh funding by $2.3 billion to $5.1 billion annually, with approximately 660,000 Californians likely to lose benefits after work requirement screening and an additional 72,000 noncitizens losing eligibility entirely. [California Association of Food Banks, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.cafoodbanks.org/what-we-do/policy/calfresh-changes-hr1/)]

**Obernolte Praised the OBBBA as a "Generational Opportunity" While It Cut Billions in Food Assistance.** According to the Big Bear Grizzly, Obernolte stated the bill was "a generational opportunity to deliver results for the American people" that "extends the 2017 tax cuts, unleashes American energy, reins in the regulatory state, and restores fiscal responsibility to government programs." He did not mention the historic SNAP cuts in his public remarks. [Big Bear Grizzly, [7/8/25](https://www.bigbeargrizzly.net/news/obernolte-yes-on-one-big-beautiful-bill-iran-and-military-deployment-on-u-s-soil/article_663f4a8d-0adc-484d-8425-901426fcc914.html)]

### Voted Against the Inflation Reduction Act's Consumer Cost Savings

**Obernolte Voted Against the Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Insulin at $35 and Allowed Medicare to Negotiate Drug Prices.** According to GovTrack, Obernolte voted "Nay" on H.R. 5376, the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, which capped insulin costs at $35 per month for Medicare beneficiaries, allowed Medicare to negotiate prescription drug prices for the first time, and capped out-of-pocket drug costs at $2,000 annually for seniors. [GovTrack, [8/12/22](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/votes/117-2022/h420)]

**Obernolte Dismissed the IRA's Health Care Provisions as an Extension of "COVID-Era Obamacare Benefits."** According to a press release from Obernolte's office, the congressman stated the bill "spends over $700 billion while raising taxes during high inflation" and characterized its health care subsidies as extending "COVID-era Obamacare benefits." [Rep. Obernolte Press Release, [8/12/22](https://obernolte.house.gov/media/press-releases/rep-obernolte-opposes-inflation-reduction-act-fiery-floor-speech)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**San Bernardino County's Median Household Income Falls 34% Short of What a Family of Four Needs for Basic Expenses.** According to San Bernardino County Community Indicators, the median household income is $85,069, but a family of four with two working adults and two children needs approximately $128,000 to cover basic costs of living — a gap of more than $40,000. High rates of inflation "have reduced or reversed growth in real income," meaning families' purchasing power has actually declined despite nominal wage gains. [San Bernardino County Community Indicators, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://indicators.sbcounty.gov/income/median-household-income-and-cost-of-living/)]

**The Inland Empire's CEO of Its Leading Economic Partnership Warned Tariffs Would Drive Up Consumer Prices in the Region.** According to CalMatters, Paul Granillo, CEO of the Inland Empire Economic Partnership, stated that "certainly the tariffs can and probably will have a negative effect on the economy of the Inland Empire" because "those are prices that are passed onto the consumer and so people buy less." Granillo also warned that Canadian lumber tariffs would increase homebuilding costs, noting "Canadian lumber is a big part of the homebuilding industry; with tariffs and the current state of relations between the Trump administration and Canada, that's problematic." [CalMatters, [3/19/25](https://calmatters.org/economy/2025/03/tariffs-inland-empire-economy/)]

**Food Banks Warned They Cannot Compensate for SNAP Cuts, as SNAP Provides Nine Meals for Every One a Food Bank Distributes.** According to the California Association of Food Banks, SNAP "provides nine meals for every one distributed by food banks," meaning the historic cuts enacted by the law Obernolte voted for will create a hunger gap that charitable organizations cannot fill. [California Association of Food Banks, [7/3/25](https://www.cafoodbanks.org/statement/congress-slashes-snap/)]
