# Rep. Tom McClintock (CA-05): Can't Be Trusted on Affordability

Rep. Tom McClintock talks about affordability while voting to make life more expensive for his own constituents. He voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which the CBO found would cut incomes for the poorest 10% of Americans by 3.1% while boosting incomes for the richest 10% by 2.7% — giving households making over $700,000 a year a $13,600 tax cut while those making under $15,000 face a 9% tax increase. Despite calling himself "a tariff skeptic" who believes "tariffs are bad public policy," McClintock voted against H.J.Res. 72, which would have ended the 25% tariffs on Canadian goods that the Yale Budget Lab estimates cost the average American household $2,400 a year. He voted against the Inflation Reduction Act's $35 insulin cap and energy cost savings. And the SNAP cuts he voted for in the One Big Beautiful Bill could cost the average affected family $146 per month in lost food assistance — all while California groceries have surged 26% over five years and tariff-driven inflation pushes prices higher on everything from produce to coffee.

### Voted For A Tax Bill That Raises Costs On The Poorest And Cuts Taxes For The Richest

**The CBO Found That The One Big Beautiful Bill Would Cut Incomes For The Poorest 10% By 3.1% While Boosting Incomes For The Richest 10% By 2.7%.** According to the Medicare Rights Center, the CBO estimated that "the incomes for the highest 10% of earners would rise by an average of 2.7% by 2034, mainly driven by tax cuts" while "those of the lowest 10% would fall by 3.1%, mostly because of cuts to programs such as Medicaid and food aid." [Medicare Rights Center, [8/14/25](https://www.medicarerights.org/medicare-watch/2025/08/14/cbo-report-on-distributional-effects-of-the-budget-act-finds-richest-gain-and-poorest-face-cuts)]

**Households Making Over $700,000 Would See A $13,600 Boost While Those Under $15,000 Face A 9% Tax Increase.** According to the House Budget Committee Democrats, "families making over $700,000 a year will see a $13,600 boost, almost entirely from tax cuts" while "an average household making less than $15,000 a year will see a tax increase of over 9 percent in 2027." [House Budget Committee Democrats, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://democrats-budget.house.gov/resources/fact-sheet/trumps-big-ugly-law-steals-poor-give-ultra-rich)]

- **People Making Over $1 Million Would See Their Taxes Go Down By $97,000.** According to the House Budget Committee Democrats, the One Big Beautiful Bill gives people making over $1 million a year a tax decrease of "$97,000 in 2027" while those earning $40,000 see "an average tax decrease of only $393." [House Budget Committee Democrats, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://democrats-budget.house.gov/resources/fact-sheet/trumps-big-ugly-law-steals-poor-give-ultra-rich)]

**A Tax Expert Said 'Higher-Income Households Benefit The Most By Far' From The Bill's Tax Provisions.** According to NPR, the bill's estate tax exemption increases to "$15 million per individual and $30 million per married couple," while the pass-through deduction "effectively reduces the top tax rate for qualifying business income from 37% to 29.6%." A tax expert told NPR that "higher-income households benefit the most by far" from the provisions. [NPR, [11/5/25](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/05/nx-s1-5590112/trump-beautiful-bill-taxes-republican-rich-wealthy)]

### Called Tariffs 'Bad Public Policy' Then Voted To Keep Them In Place

**McClintock Called Tariffs 'Bad Public Policy' And 'Self-Sabotaging,' Then Voted Against Ending Them.** According to CNBC, McClintock said "tariffs are bad public policy and are self-sabotaging at least part of the recovery that we've set in motion." Yet when the House voted 219-211 on H.J.Res. 72 to end the 25% tariffs on Canadian goods, McClintock voted to keep the tariffs in place — one of 205 Republicans who sided with Trump despite expressing doubts. [CNBC, [2/10/26](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/10/gop-tariffs-trump-house-johnson.html)]

- **Only Six Republicans Voted To Actually End The Canada Tariffs.** According to the Council on Foreign Relations, the House voted 219-211 to revoke the tariffs Trump imposed on Canada, with only six Republicans crossing the aisle — Thomas Massie, Don Bacon, Kevin Kiley, Dan Newhouse, Brian Fitzpatrick, and Jeff Hurd. McClintock was not among them. [Council on Foreign Relations, [2/11/26](https://www.cfr.org/articles/the-house-votes-to-rein-in-trumps-canada-tariffs)]

**The Yale Budget Lab Estimated Tariffs Cost The Average American Household $2,400 Per Year.** According to the Yale Budget Lab, "the price level from all 2025 tariffs rises by 1.7% in the short-run, equivalent to an average per household income loss of $2,400 in 2025." The analysis also found that tariffs are regressive, with the lowest-income households paying a larger share of their income toward tariff-driven price increases than the wealthiest. [Yale Budget Lab, [9/26/25](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/state-us-tariffs-september-26-2025)]

**California Firms Incurred $11.3 Billion In Tariff Costs From January Through May 2025, The Highest Of Any State.** According to the Governor of California's office, California firms incurred "$11.3 billion in tariff costs from January through May 2025, the highest of any state in the country," and the tariffs are "projected to cost California households $25 billion and lead to a loss of over 64,000 jobs." [Governor of California, [7/30/25](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/07/30/trump-tariff-policy-continues-to-cause-chaos-in-american-economy/)]

### Voted Against Lowering Energy And Prescription Drug Costs

**McClintock Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Insulin At $35 For Seniors And Enabled Medicare To Negotiate Drug Prices For The First Time.** According to NPR, the House passed the Inflation Reduction Act 220-207 on August 12, 2022, with no Republican support. McClintock voted no. [NPR, [8/12/22](https://www.npr.org/2022/08/12/1117263829/the-house-votes-on-the-inflation-reduction-act)]

**McClintock Voted Against H.R. 3, Which Would Have Lowered Drug Prices To 120% Of The Average Price In Comparable Countries.** According to a statement on McClintock's official website, he characterized the bill as "classic price controls by a different name" and argued that "price controls [...] consistently and unerringly produce a shortage of whatever commodity they're trying to control — in this case, life-saving drugs." [Rep. Tom McClintock, [12/12/19](https://mcclintock.house.gov/newsroom/vote-notes/hr-3-pharmaceutical-price-controls-no)]

- **A Local Constituent Called Out McClintock For Siding With Drug Companies Over Patients.** According to the Mountain Democrat, Cameron Park resident Mary Lou Giles wrote that McClintock "votes against reducing prices on pharmaceuticals" while drug companies spend only 17% of revenue on research — "significantly less than they spend on advertising, marketing, and lobbying efforts." [Mountain Democrat, [10/8/20](https://www.mtdemocrat.com/letters/mcclintock-votes-against-reducing-prices-on-pharmaceuticals/)]

### Voted For SNAP Cuts That Could Cost Families $146 Per Month

**The One Big Beautiful Bill's SNAP Cuts Could Cost The Average Affected Family $146 Per Month In Lost Food Assistance.** According to CNBC, the One Big Beautiful Bill "cuts food stamps for millions" of families, with the average affected family losing approximately $146 per month in benefits. The Congressional Budget Office estimated that 3 million more Americans could lose food assistance due to stricter work requirements. [CNBC, [7/10/25](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/10/trumps-big-beautiful-bill-cuts-snap-for-millions-of-families.html)]

**55% Of CalFresh Participants In California Are Latino, And The Cuts Disproportionately Impact Working Families.** According to Newsweek, a UCLA Latino Policy and Politics Institute study found that "55 percent of CalFresh participants" are Latino, with "69 percent in the labor force" among Latino working-age participants. UCLA LPPI faculty director Arturo Vargas Bustamante warned that "reducing food assistance means forcing families to make impossible choices between meals, rent, and health care." [Newsweek, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.newsweek.com/snap-cuts-disproportionately-impact-key-california-demographic-2097308)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**California Groceries Rose 26% Over Five Years, With Tariff-Driven Inflation Pushing Prices Even Higher.** According to the Bay Area News Group, "California groceries are 26% costlier over five years, as the overall cost of living rose 23%." Prices in the "food at home" category "grew at an average 3.3% annual rate" during the summer of 2025 — double the 1.5% rate in 2024. The article noted that "labor shortages have shrunk harvests while tariffs have upped import prices" for fruits and vegetables. [Bay Area News Group, [9/13/25](https://www.siliconvalley.com/2025/09/13/california-shoppers-see-surging-grocery-inflation-once-again/)]

**A Food Industry Analyst Said 'Probably Almost Half' Of Supermarket Products Would Be Affected By Tariffs.** According to NPR, food industry analyst Phil Lempert estimated that "probably almost half of the products in a supermarket — about 40,000 products — will be affected" by tariffs, warning that produce faces "significant issues with both price and availability" and that olive oil prices, already through the roof, are "going to go even higher." [NPR, [4/4/25](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/04/nx-s1-5351324/tariffs-higher-grocery-prices-trump)]

**One In Five El Dorado County Residents Depend On Medi-Cal, And McClintock's Votes Threaten Their Coverage And Food Assistance Simultaneously.** According to the Mountain Democrat, approximately one in five El Dorado County residents depend on Medi-Cal for healthcare access, while Marshall Hospital in Placerville "operates with constrained budgets while treating many Medi-Cal and Medicare patients, whose reimbursement rates frequently fall short of actual care costs." The author warned that Medicaid cuts "may force rural hospital closures or service reductions, creating transportation barriers for seniors and low-income families." [Mountain Democrat, [8/8/25](https://www.mtdemocrat.com/opinion/mcclintock-s-vote-for-the-so-called-big-beautiful-bill-hurts-rural-health/article_a5443ba2-a74f-4804-90e5-a47f83c96e8e.html)]
