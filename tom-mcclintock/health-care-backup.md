# Rep. Tom McClintock (CA-05): Can't Be Trusted on Health Care

Rep. Tom McClintock has spent nearly two decades in Congress working to strip health coverage from his own constituents. He voted for the American Health Care Act, which the CBO projected would leave 23 million more Americans uninsured and allow insurers to charge people with pre-existing conditions dramatically higher premiums. He voted against the Inflation Reduction Act's $35 insulin cap and Medicare drug price negotiation. He voted against H.R. 3, which would have lowered prescription drug prices. And in 2025, he voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which cuts over $1 trillion from Medicaid — putting 56% of adult Medi-Cal enrollees in his own district at risk of losing coverage due to new work requirements. When hundreds of his constituents showed up to a town hall to plead with him to protect the ACA, he had to be escorted out by police — and voted to gut their coverage anyway.

### Voted to Strip Coverage From Millions

**McClintock Voted For The American Health Care Act, Which The CBO Projected Would Leave 23 Million More Americans Uninsured.** According to the Congressional Budget Office, the House-passed American Health Care Act "would increase the number of people who are uninsured by 23 million in 2026 relative to current law." McClintock voted yes on May 4, 2017. [Congressional Budget Office, [5/24/17](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/52752)]

**The AHCA Would Have Allowed Insurers To Charge People With Pre-Existing Conditions Higher Premiums, Potentially Pricing Them Out Of Coverage.** According to the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities, "people with pre-existing conditions could face substantially higher premiums under the AHCA in states that waive core consumer protections [...]  Coverage losses would be more concentrated among people with pre-existing conditions and serious health needs — the very people who need health insurance the most." [Center on Budget and Policy Priorities, [5/24/17](https://www.cbpp.org/research/new-cbo-estimates-23-million-more-uninsured-under-house-passed-republican-health-bill)]

**McClintock Helped Negotiate The AHCA's Final Version, Announcing His Support After Securing Changes He Claimed Would Protect Low-Income Americans.** According to a press release from Rep. McClintock, he and Reps. MacArthur, McSally, and Aderholt "welcome[d] changes to AHCA" and announced support for the bill after McClintock's motion to "request that additional resources be focused to assure access for low-income middle-aged Americans" passed the House Budget Committee. [Rep. Tom McClintock, [5/3/17](https://mcclintock.house.gov/newsroom/press-releases/mcclintock-macarthur-mcsally-aderholt-welcome-changes-to-ahca-announce)]

### Voted Against Lowering Prescription Drug Prices

**McClintock Voted Against H.R. 3, Which Would Have Lowered Drug Prices To 120% Of The Average Price In Comparable Countries.** According to a statement on McClintock's official website, he characterized the bill as "classic price controls by a different name" and argued that "price controls [...]  consistently and unerringly produce a shortage of whatever commodity they're trying to control — in this case, life-saving drugs." [Rep. Tom McClintock, [12/12/19](https://mcclintock.house.gov/newsroom/vote-notes/hr-3-pharmaceutical-price-controls-no)]

- **A Local Constituent Called Out McClintock For Siding With Drug Companies Over Patients.** According to the Mountain Democrat, Cameron Park resident Mary Lou Giles wrote that McClintock "votes against reducing prices on pharmaceuticals" while drug companies spend only 17% of revenue on research — "significantly less than they spend on advertising, marketing, and lobbying efforts." [Mountain Democrat, [10/8/20](https://www.mtdemocrat.com/letters/mcclintock-votes-against-reducing-prices-on-pharmaceuticals/)]

**McClintock Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Insulin At $35 For Seniors And Enabled Medicare To Negotiate Drug Prices For The First Time.** According to NPR, the House passed the Inflation Reduction Act 220-207 on August 12, 2022, with no Republican support. McClintock voted no. [NPR, [8/12/22](https://www.npr.org/2022/08/12/1117263829/the-house-votes-on-the-inflation-reduction-act)]

### Voted For Over $1 Trillion In Medicaid Cuts

**McClintock Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Which Cuts Medicaid Funding By Over $1 Trillion Over A Decade.** According to the American Hospital Association, the House passed the final version of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1) with "significant impacts to Medicaid" and "Health Insurance Marketplaces." McClintock voted yes. The bill was signed into law on July 4, 2025. [American Hospital Association, [7/3/25](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2025-07-03-house-passes-final-version-one-big-beautiful-bill-act)]

**McClintock Claimed Only People Who 'Refuse To Get Up Off The Couch' Would Lose Coverage Under The Bill.** According to a press release from Rep. McClintock, he stated that the bill would "relieve pressure on Medicaid by requiring able-bodied adults to look for work" and that "the only groups losing coverage are able-bodied grown-ups who refuse to get up off the couch and look for work, illegal aliens who refuse to go home and fraudsters." [Rep. Tom McClintock, [5/22/25](https://mcclintock.house.gov/newsroom/press-releases/big-beautiful-future)]

- **Experts Warned That Work Requirements Would Cause Even Eligible Workers To Lose Coverage Due To Bureaucratic Burdens.** According to CalMatters, "the red tape is so cumbersome that even people who work will lose Medi-Cal." In Arkansas, where work requirements were implemented in 2018, "roughly 18,000 people lost their Medicaid coverage — most of them, state officials said, not because they didn't qualify, but because they either didn't understand the new rules or couldn't navigate the maze of administrative details." [CalMatters, [5/30/25](https://calmatters.org/health/2025/05/medicaid-work-requirement-california-congress/)]

**56% Of Adult Medi-Cal Enrollees In McClintock's District Were At Risk Of Losing Health Coverage Under The Bill's Work Requirements.** According to the California Budget & Policy Center, in McClintock's congressional district, 56% of adult Medi-Cal enrollees faced potential loss of health coverage under the proposed Medi-Cal work requirements. The organization warned that the proposed reductions "would be devastating for communities across the state." [Gold Rush Cam/Sierra Sun Times, [3/1/25](https://goldrushcam.com/sierrasuntimes/index.php/news/local-news/65071-california-budget-policy-center-says-in-congressman-tom-mcclintock-s-district-56-of-adult-medi-cal-enrollees-at-risk-of-losing-health-coverage-by-medi-cal-work-requirements-as-new-data-reveals-how-the-house-budget-framework-could-harm-californians)]

### Constituents Pleaded With Him — He Ignored Them

**Hundreds Of Constituents Showed Up To McClintock's Town Hall To Demand He Protect The ACA; He Had To Be Escorted Out By Police.** According to NPR, "hundreds of protesters attended" a town hall at the Tower Theater in Roseville on February 4, 2017. After the event, "law enforcement surrounded McClintock and escorted him to a waiting vehicle while protesters chanted 'shame.'" McClintock said it was "the first time I've ever had a police department have to extract me from a town hall, and I've done well over 100 of them in Congress." [NPR, [2/5/17](https://www.npr.org/2017/02/05/513532390/hundreds-protest-trump-at-california-congressmans-town-hall)]

**A Retired College Professor Told McClintock His Wife Would Die Without ACA Coverage.** According to Health Access California, Lincoln resident David Emerson, a retired college professor, told McClintock about medications the ACA helps his wife of 53 years purchase, warning: "On my fixed income, we will not be able to afford the medication she is now on, she will die." [Health Access California, [2/6/17](https://health-access.org/rep-mcclintock-town-hall-yields-a-thousand-with-aca-other-questions/)]

**A 28-Year-Old Constituent Said She Would Have Been Over Half A Million Dollars In Debt Without ACA Coverage After A Paralyzing Car Accident.** According to Health Access California, Auburn resident Amanda Barnes, 28, said she was hit by a car and paralyzed from the waist down: "If I had not had my mother's insurance to cover my health care costs, I would have been over half million in debt" in just the first three days. [Health Access California, [2/6/17](https://health-access.org/rep-mcclintock-town-hall-yields-a-thousand-with-aca-other-questions/)]

**47,000 Residents In McClintock's District Depended On Medi-Cal Expansion At The Time He Was Voting To Gut It.** According to Health Access California, at the time of the 2017 town hall, 47,000 of McClintock's constituents depended on Medi-Cal expansion and 27,000 would face a significant tax increase under repeal. [Health Access California, [2/6/17](https://health-access.org/rep-mcclintock-town-hall-yields-a-thousand-with-aca-other-questions/)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**One In Five El Dorado County Residents Depend On Medi-Cal, And Local Hospitals Could Face Devastating Cuts.** According to the Mountain Democrat, approximately one in five El Dorado County residents depend on Medi-Cal for healthcare access. Marshall Hospital in Placerville "operates with constrained budgets while treating many Medi-Cal and Medicare patients, whose reimbursement rates frequently fall short of actual care costs." The author warned that Medicaid cuts "may force rural hospital closures or service reductions, creating transportation barriers for seniors and low-income families." [Mountain Democrat, [8/8/25](https://www.mtdemocrat.com/opinion/mcclintock-s-vote-for-the-so-called-big-beautiful-bill-hurts-rural-health/article_a5443ba2-a74f-4804-90e5-a47f83c96e8e.html)]

**Up To 3 Million Californians Could Lose Medi-Cal Coverage Under The Work Requirements McClintock Voted For.** According to CalMatters, between 1.2 and 1.4 million Californians could lose health insurance under the new work requirements, while Governor Newsom's administration estimated "as many as 3 million Californians could be thrown off Medi-Cal based on the work requirement alone." [CalMatters, [5/30/25](https://calmatters.org/health/2025/05/medicaid-work-requirement-california-congress/)]
