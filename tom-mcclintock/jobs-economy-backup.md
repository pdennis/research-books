# Rep. Tom McClintock (CA-05): Can't Be Trusted on Jobs and the Economy

Rep. Tom McClintock talks about supporting workers while voting against virtually every major jobs and economic investment bill of the past decade. He voted against the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, which has delivered billions to California for roads, bridges, and broadband — including $13.6 million for highway improvements in Amador County and $31.9 million for safety upgrades near Auburn in his own district. He voted against the CHIPS and Science Act, which invested $54 billion to bring semiconductor manufacturing jobs back to America. He voted against the Raise the Wage Act. He voted against the American Rescue Plan. He voted against the Inflation Reduction Act. Then he cheered when the Trump administration killed a $1.2 billion hydrogen energy project that would have created an estimated 220,000 jobs in California's Central Valley — in and around his own district — saying companies "should make their money by pleasing their customers and not by using government to take money that families have earned." Meanwhile, the One Big Beautiful Bill he voted for cuts SNAP benefits by $186 billion over a decade — even though every dollar of SNAP spending generates $1.50 to $1.80 in local economic activity. And the tariffs he voted to keep in place are projected to slow California job growth to just 3,000 jobs per month in 2026, half the 2025 pace.

### Voted Against The Biggest Jobs Bills Of The Decade — Then His District Got The Money Anyway

**McClintock Voted Against The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, Which Has Delivered Billions To California Including Projects In His Own District.** According to NPR, the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act passed the House 228-206 on November 5, 2021, with bipartisan support — but McClintock voted no. The law included $3.4 billion for wildfire risk reduction and billions more for roads, bridges, broadband, and clean water across California. [NPR, [11/5/21](https://www.npr.org/2021/11/05/1048745258/the-house-passes-bipartisan-infrastructure-bill-sending-it-to-bidens-desk)]

- **The Infrastructure Law Funded $13.6 Million In Highway Improvements In Amador County And $31.9 Million For Safety Upgrades Near Auburn — Both In McClintock's District.** According to Caltrans, $13,647,000 in infrastructure law funding supported a State Route 88 pavement improvement project in Amador County to "rehabilitate highway pavement, upgrade existing guardrails, modify traffic signals, and rehabilitate drainage systems." Separately, State Route 49 near Auburn received $31.9 million to construct two roundabouts and a concrete median barrier. [Caltrans, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://dot.ca.gov/caltrans-near-me/district-10/district-10-news/ctc-allocates-1-8-bill-improve-transportation-infrastructure)]

- **The Infrastructure Law Also Funded Over $100 Million In Broadband Grants For El Dorado And Amador Counties.** According to the California Public Utilities Commission, grants of up to $103 million from the Last Mile Federal Funding Account were approved for El Dorado County, and up to $61 million were approved for Amador County — connecting rural communities in McClintock's district to high-speed internet. [California Public Utilities Commission, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/internet-and-phone/broadband-implementation-for-california/last-mile-federal-funding-account/federal-funding-account-awards)]

**McClintock Voted Against The CHIPS And Science Act, Which Invested $54 Billion To Bring Semiconductor Manufacturing Jobs Back To America.** According to NPR, the CHIPS and Science Act passed 243-187, with 24 Republicans breaking from their party to support it. McClintock was not among them. Commerce Secretary Raimondo noted that America "used to make 40% of the world's chips, we make about 12% now" and that the bill would "increase domestic manufacturing and lower costs for families." [NPR, [7/28/22](https://www.npr.org/2022/07/26/1113470753/chip-production-semiconductor-senate)]

**McClintock Voted Against The American Rescue Plan, Which Delivered $350 Billion To State And Local Governments To Preserve Jobs During The Pandemic.** According to Wikipedia, the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021 passed the House with no Republican support. The $1.9 trillion package included $350 billion for state and local governments, $170 billion for schools, and direct stimulus payments to families. McClintock voted no. [Wikipedia, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Rescue_Plan_Act_of_2021)]

**McClintock Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Created An Estimated 170,000 Clean Energy Jobs In Its First Year.** According to NPR, the House passed the Inflation Reduction Act 220-207 on August 12, 2022, with no Republican support. The law invested $369 billion in clean energy and climate programs. McClintock voted no. [NPR, [8/12/22](https://www.npr.org/2022/08/12/1117263829/the-house-votes-on-the-inflation-reduction-act)]

### Cheered When The Trump Administration Killed 220,000 Jobs In His Own Region

**McClintock Supported The Trump Administration's Cancellation Of A $1.2 Billion Hydrogen Energy Project That Would Have Created An Estimated 220,000 Jobs In California's Central Valley.** According to the LA Times, the Trump administration canceled $7.56 billion in funding for energy projects across 16 states, with the biggest cut being "$1.2 billion for California's ambitious project to develop clean hydrogen known as the Alliance for Renewable Clean Hydrogen Energy Systems." The project would have "deliver[ed] an estimated 220,000 jobs, including 130,000 construction jobs and 90,000 permanent jobs, according to the state." The terminated projects spanned "large swaths of the Central Valley" — in and around McClintock's district. [LA Times via Yahoo News, [10/4/25](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/trump-slashed-8-billion-energy-100000759.html)]

- **McClintock Said He 'Strongly Supports' The Cuts, Arguing That Companies Should Not Use 'Government To Take Money That Families Have Earned.'** According to the LA Times, McClintock stated: "$7.5 billion comes out to about $60 taken from the average earnings of every family in America. Call me old fashioned, but I think that companies should make their money by pleasing their customers and not by using government to take money that families have earned." [LA Times via Senator Padilla, [10/4/25](https://www.padilla.senate.gov/newsroom/news-coverage/la-times-trump-slashed-8-billion-for-energy-projects-in-blue-states-in-california-that-could-be-felt-in-red-areas-as-well/)]

**McClintock Dismissed Job Cuts At Yosemite National Park — In His Own District — Calling Concerns An 'Absurd Level Of Hysteria.'** According to KMPH, McClintock said: "I did a little bit of checking. Do you know what the staff cuts are at Yosemite? Ten! 10 out of 500 full-time winter employees." He characterized concerns over DOGE-driven federal job cuts as "the absurd level of hysteria that we're seeing from the left." [KMPH, [3/4/25](https://kmph.com/news/local/valley-rep-tom-mcclintock-says-only-ten-jobs-cut-from-yosemite-10-out-of-500-president-donald-trump-volodymyr-zelenskyy)]

### Called Tariffs 'A Bad Public Policy" Then Voted To Keep Them As They Slow California Job Growth

**McClintock Called Tariffs 'Bad Public Policy' And 'Self-Sabotaging,' Then Voted Against Ending Them.** According to The Hill, McClintock said "tariffs are bad public policy and are self-sabotaging at least part of the recovery that we've set in motion." Yet when the House voted 219-211 on H.J.Res. 72 to end the 25% tariffs on Canadian goods, McClintock voted to keep the tariffs in place — one of 205 Republicans who sided with Trump despite expressing doubts. [The Hill, [9/16/25](https://thehill.com/homenews/house/5506897-gop-tariff-concession-rule-vote/)]

**Tariffs Are Projected To Cut California Job Growth In Half In 2026.** According to CalMatters, California job growth is expected to slow to "just 3,000 jobs per month in 2026" — down from 6,000 jobs per month in 2025 — "driven mainly by impacts from tariffs which are broadly assumed to dampen job growth across most sectors including manufacturing, leisure and hospitality, trade, transportation and utilities, and professional and business services." [CalMatters, [12/2025](https://calmatters.org/economy/2025/12/year-in-review-2025-economy/)]

- **California Firms Incurred $11.3 Billion In Tariff Costs From January Through May 2025, The Highest Of Any State.** According to the Governor of California's office, California firms incurred "$11.3 billion in tariff costs from January through May 2025, the highest of any state in the country," and the tariffs are "projected to cost California households $25 billion and lead to a loss of over 64,000 jobs." [Governor of California, [7/30/25](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/07/30/trump-tariff-policy-continues-to-cause-chaos-in-american-economy/)]

**California Almond Growers — A Key Industry In And Around McClintock's District — Saw Prices Drop From $2.50 To $1.40 A Pound After Trump's First-Term Tariffs.** According to CalMatters, research from the University of California Giannini Foundation of Agricultural Economics found that almond growers saw "their prices drop from $2.50 a pound to $1.40 a pound" after Trump's tariffs during his first term. California's agricultural and wine industries face renewed tariff effects including "higher costs" and threats to "long-term relationships with trading partners." [CalMatters, [4/23/25](https://calmatters.org/economy/2025/04/tariffs-california-agriculture-wine/)]

### Voted For SNAP Cuts That Drain Money From Local Economies

**McClintock Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill, Which Cuts SNAP Benefits By $186 Billion Over 10 Years — The Largest Reduction In The Program's History.** According to the Harvard Kennedy School, the One Big Beautiful Bill "will reduce SNAP funding by approximately $186 billion over 10 years—a 20% cut that marks the largest reduction in the history of the program." The bill raises work requirement ages to include individuals 55 to 64, who "must work a minimum of 20 hours a week or only get benefits for a maximum of three months over three years." [Harvard Kennedy School, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/faculty-research/policy-topics/social-policy/explainer-understanding-snap-program-and-what-cuts)]

- **Every Dollar Of SNAP Spending Generates $1.50 To $1.80 In Local Economic Activity.** According to the Harvard Kennedy School, "SNAP not only lifts families out of poverty, reduces food insecurity, and lowers health care costs, but also significantly bolsters local economies. Every dollar distributed through SNAP generates about $1.50 to $1.80 in economic activity by supporting jobs in grocery stores and providing income to workers." [Harvard Kennedy School, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/faculty-research/policy-topics/social-policy/explainer-understanding-snap-program-and-what-cuts)]

**The CBO Found That The One Big Beautiful Bill Would Cut Incomes For The Poorest 10% By 3.1% While Boosting Incomes For The Richest 10% By 2.7%.** According to the Medicare Rights Center, the CBO estimated that "the incomes for the highest 10% of earners would rise by an average of 2.7% by 2034, mainly driven by tax cuts" while "those of the lowest 10% would fall by 3.1%, mostly because of cuts to programs such as Medicaid and food aid." [Medicare Rights Center, [8/14/25](https://www.medicarerights.org/medicare-watch/2025/08/14/cbo-report-on-distributional-effects-of-the-budget-act-finds-richest-gain-and-poorest-face-cuts)]

### Voted Against Raising The Minimum Wage

**McClintock Voted Against The Raise The Wage Act, Which Would Have Gradually Increased The Federal Minimum Wage To $15 Per Hour.** According to Vote Smart, McClintock voted against H.R. 582, the Raise the Wage Act, on July 18, 2019. The bill passed the House 231-199 but was never taken up by the Senate. [Vote Smart, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://justfacts.votesmart.org/bill/26761/68044/9715/raise-the-wage-act)]

- **McClintock Argued That Raising The Minimum Wage Would 'Rip The First Rung In The Ladder Of Opportunity' For Workers.** According to AllGov, McClintock stated that raising the minimum wage would "rip the first rung in the ladder of opportunity for teenagers, for minorities, for people who are trying to get into the job market for their first job." He claimed that "if your labor as an unskilled person just entering the workforce is worth say $7 an hour at a job and the minimum wage is $10, you have just been made permanently unemployable." [AllGov, [1/26/15](http://www.allgov.com/usa/ca/news/unusual-news/rep-mcclintock-argues-minimum-wage-needs-to-be-low-for-minorities-150126?news=855479)]

**McClintock Tried To Defund The Economic Development Administration, Calling Federal Job-Creation Programs A 'Slush Fund.'** According to a floor speech by Rep. McClintock, he rose in support of an amendment to defund the EDA, calling it "solely and simply a slush fund that gives away money for the most dubious of local projects" and arguing that "Washington doesn't create jobs." [Rep. Tom McClintock, [5/8/12](https://mcclintock.house.gov/newsroom/speeches/amendment-to-defund-the-economic-development-administration)]

### Impact On Everyday People

**One In Five El Dorado County Residents Depend On Medi-Cal, And McClintock's Votes Threatened Their Coverage, Food Assistance, And Local Hospital Jobs Simultaneously.** According to the Mountain Democrat, approximately one in five El Dorado County residents depend on Medi-Cal for healthcare access, while Marshall Hospital in Placerville "operates with constrained budgets while treating many Medi-Cal and Medicare patients, whose reimbursement rates frequently fall short of actual care costs." The author warned that Medicaid cuts "may force rural hospital closures or service reductions, creating transportation barriers for seniors and low-income families." [Mountain Democrat, [8/8/25](https://www.mtdemocrat.com/opinion/mcclintock-s-vote-for-the-so-called-big-beautiful-bill-hurts-rural-health/article_a5443ba2-a74f-4804-90e5-a47f83c96e8e.html)]

**California Groceries Rose 26% Over Five Years, With Tariff-Driven Inflation Pushing Prices Even Higher.** According to the Bay Area News Group, "California groceries are 26% costlier over five years, as the overall cost of living rose 23%." Prices in the "food at home" category "grew at an average 3.3% annual rate" during the summer of 2025 — double the 1.5% rate in 2024. The article noted that "labor shortages have shrunk harvests while tariffs have upped import prices" for fruits and vegetables. [Bay Area News Group, [9/13/25](https://www.siliconvalley.com/2025/09/13/california-shoppers-see-surging-grocery-inflation-once-again/)]

**The Yale Budget Lab Estimated Tariffs Cost The Average American Household $2,400 Per Year — And Hit The Lowest-Income Households Hardest.** According to the Yale Budget Lab, "the price level from all 2025 tariffs rises by 1.7% in the short-run, equivalent to an average per household income loss of $2,400 in 2025." The analysis also found that tariffs are regressive, with the lowest-income households paying a larger share of their income toward tariff-driven price increases than the wealthiest. [Yale Budget Lab, [9/26/25](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/state-us-tariffs-september-26-2025)]
