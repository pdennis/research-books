The War In Iran Hurt California Farmers 

Farmers Were Hit By Prices Due To The War In Iran, On Top Of Already Challenging Farm Fundamentals. According to Cal Matters, "Farmers are accustomed to financial struggles. Volatile prices for crops and the rising costs of inputs like fertilizer have produced a steady decline in the number of independent and family farms, many of which have combined into larger farms or been sold to corporate owners, private equity or hedge funds. While the total number of farms in California decreased by about 20% from 2002 to 2022, the size of the average farm increased by more than 5% during that time, according to the most recent U.S. Census of Agriculture. The Trump administration’s unpredictable tariff scheme and the ongoing war in Iran — especially the resulting closure of the Strait of Hormuz — have only sowed more uncertainty. ‘It’s been a difficult 16 months,’ said Cameron, the Fresno County farmer, who noted that surging fuel prices have hit the industry at a particularly difficult time. ‘We use a tremendous amount of diesel for harvest, and it just puts another stress on us.’" [Cal Matters, 7/31/26]

Fertilizer Prices Increased By A Third Or More, Causing Market Shocks And Hurting California Farmers. According to Los Angeles Times, "Already there are reports that some fertilizers are up by a third or more in price. The rise is taking place in California and across the U.S. even though the country produces the majority of its nitrogen-based fertilizers, which are critical to improving crop yields. The fertilizers are typically applied by U.S. farmers either as liquid nitrogen, liquid ammonia or as pellets of urea, which is the most common nitrogen-based fertilizer in the world, said Veronica Nigh, chief economist at the Fertilizer Institute. While the vast majority of liquid nitrogen and ammonia is domestically produced, the U.S. imports about half of its urea, making it susceptible to the Middle East supply shock.  All nitrogen fertilizers are derived from ammonia, which is made using natural gas — with half of all exportable urea supplies coming from the oil-and-gas rich Mideast, where it has to pass through the disputed Strait of Hormuz, she said." [Los Angeles Times, 3/29/26]

  • The American Farm Bureau Federation Begged Trump To Escort Fertilizer Shipments Out Of The Middle East In Fear Of A Major Food Supply Chain Disruption. According to Cal Matters, "The American Farm Bureau Federation, the country’s primary advocate for farmers and ranchers, wrote a letter begging Trump to send the U.S. Navy to escort crucial fertilizer shipments out of the Middle East. ‘We are deeply concerned that failure to act could lead to disruptions to the food supply chain not seen since 2022 when food price inflation reached 40-year highs,’ wrote Zippy Duvall, the group’s president." [Cal Matters, 7/31/26]
