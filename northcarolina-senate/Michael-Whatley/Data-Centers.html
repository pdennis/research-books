Overview

Michael Whatley was a supporter of unpopular data centers. Whatley supported tax breaks for data centers and dismissed North Carolinian’s opposition to data centers by claiming the opposition wasn’t “organic.” 

Whatley and his family held stock in companies that were building data centers and the utility companies that was fueling the data center boom. 

Whatley is being backed by private equity executives who were developing a data center in Lee County, North Carolina that faced opposition from Lee County residents.  

Whatley Supported Data Centers 

Whatley Supported Tax Breaks For Data Centers 

Whatley Claimed To Support Tax Breaks For Data Centers. According to Cardinal & Pine, “However, Spectrum News reported that Whatley said he supports tax breaks for data centers. The two are related concepts, but technically different. A tax break would mean companies pay less in taxes, whereas a subsidy would involve the government providing money or other financial support directly to the data center developer.” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

Whatley Previously Dismissed Concerns Around Data Centers

  • Whatley Questioned If Opposition To Data Centers Was Organic. According to CBS 17, “In a radio interview in July, Whatley questioned whether opposition to data centers is organic, while arguing the facilities are needed to keep the U.S. competitive with China on artificial intelligence.” [CBS 17, 8/21/26]
  • Whatley Claimed That The Opposition To Data Centers Was Not Organic. According to NBC News, “‘This opposition to data centers is not organic,’ Michael Whatley, the GOP Senate nominee in North Carolina, told Fox News host Brian Kilmeade in an interview last month, later agreeing that governments should offer developers tax breaks but not subsidies to encourage their expansion. ‘This is being ginned up the same way that we saw opposition to energy projects around the country.’” [NBC News, 8/24/26]

Whatley And His Family Held Stock In Companies Involved In The Data Center Industry 

Whatley And His Family Held Stock In Companies Building Data Centers 

Whatley And His Family Held Stock In Companies Involved In The Data Center Industry Such As Apple, Nvidia, Google And Microsoft. According to Cardinal & Pine, “Whatley has also disclosed that his family holds investments in several companies involved in the data center industry, including Apple, Nvidia, Alphabet (Google), and Microsoft.” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

Whatley And His Family Held Between $246,000 And $690,000 In Energy Investments With Companies Fueling Data Center Growth 

Whatley And His Family Held Between $246,000 And $690,000 In Energy Investments With Companies Fueling Data Center Growth. According to Fox News, “Michael Whatley, the former chair of the Republican National Committee and the Republican nominee for U.S. Senate in North Carolina, and his family hold between $246,000 and $690,000 in energy investments with companies fueling data center growth in the Tar Heel State, according to his federal disclosure.” [Fox News, 9/23/26]

The Holdings Included Duke Energy, Arista Networks, Dominion Energy And GE Vernova. According to Fox News, “Whatley and his family have holdings between $149,000 and $410,000 in Duke Energy, between $1,000 and $15,000 in Arista Networks, from $80,000 to $200,000 with Dominion Energy, and between $16,000 and $65,000 in GE Vernova. While most of those, like Duke and Vernova, don’t directly build data centers themselves, they offer services or products used by them. Duke is a major utility provider in North Carolina, and Vernova produces on-site gas turbines used by data center facilities operated by companies like Amazon and Microsoft.” [Fox News, 9/23/26]

2025: Whatley Reported $361,000 From CAPCVentures LLC For Consulting Services And Who Lists GE Vernova As A Client

Whatley Reported $361,000 From CAPCVentures LLC For Consulting Services And Who Lists GE Vernova As A Client. According to Fox News, “In addition to those investments, Whatley reported $361,000 from CAPCVentures LLC, a D.C. firm, for consulting services in 2025, according to his financial disclosure. That firm lists GE Vernova— a major supplier of turbines and grid equipment used to power data centers — as a client, along with Centrus Energy and the Renewable Fuels Association.” [Fox News, 9/23/26]

Whatley Was Backed By Unpopular Data Centers 

Whatley Accepted $27,000 From The Private Equity Firm KKR Who Co-Owned Data Center Company CyrusOne 

Cardinal & Pine: Senate Candidate Michael Whatley Accepted $27,000 From Data Center Developer [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

Whatley Accepted $27,000 In Contributions From Partners, Executives, And Investors Affiliated With Private Equity Firm KKR Which Co-Owned Data Center Company CyrusOne. According to Cardinal & Pine, “He has also accepted $27,000 in contributions from partners, executives, and investors affiliated with private equity firm Kohlberg Kravis Roberts & Co. (KKR), which co-owns data center company CyrusOne. CyrusOne operates a data center campus in Durham and is building another campus in Lee County.” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

  • CyrusOne Managed More Than 55 Data Centers And Had One Already Operational In North Carolina. According to Cardinal & Pine, “Alongside Global Infrastructure Partners, KKR jointly owns CyrusOne, a Dallas-based company that manages more than 55 data centers, including facilities in North Carolina. CyrusOne’s DUR1-DUR2 data center campus, a 420,000 square-foot, 40 megawatt facility, is already operational and is located in the Research Triangle Park, located near Raleigh-Durham International Airport.” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

One Of Whatley’s KKR Donors, Raj Agrawal, Gave Whatley $5,000 And Referred To The Company As A “One-Stop Shop For Hyperscalers.” According to Cardinal & Pine, “One of Whatley’s KKR donors, Raj Agrawal, who gave Whatley $5,000, referred to the company as a ‘one-stop shop for hyperscalers.’” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

CyrusOne Was Developing A Data Center In Lee County Which Residents Oppose 

CyrusOne Was In The Process Of Developing A Data Center Campus In Lee County. According to Cardinal & Pine, “CyrusOne is also in the process of developing a data center campus in Lee County near Sanford.” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

Over 2,800 Lee County Residents Signed A Petition Calling For A Moratorium On Data Centers. According to Cardinal & Pine, “Over 2,800 Lee County residents signed a June petition calling for a moratorium on data centers.” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

A Third Generation Farmer Was Emotional At A Commission Hearing As He Discussed How The Data Center Could Impact His Farm. According to Cardinal & Pine, “A third-generation farmer in the community got emotional at an Aug. 17 county commission meeting as he discussed his concerns about the data center and how it could affect his farm, quality of life, and livestock. ‘I’ve been down there for 47 years. […] I’m thinking about selling every damn thing I got because of y’all,’ he said.” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]

At An Another Commission Meeting, A Different Farmer Expressed Concerns About Noise Pollution From The Data Center. According to Cardinal & Pine, “At a July commission meeting, another Lee County farmer, Jeff Kidd, expressed similar concerns about noise pollution coming from data centers. ‘Sounds like a helicopter landing on your house. This is what’s going to happen to us in Lower Moncure,’ Kidd said.” [Cardinal & Pine, 10/1/26]
