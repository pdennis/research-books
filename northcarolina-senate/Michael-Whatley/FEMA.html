Overview 

As Western North Carolina is recovering from Hurricane Helene, Whatley supportedcuttingback FEMA’s in national disaster relief which could leave states having to be responsible for millions of dollars in infrastructure rebuilding. Whatley’s support was reflected in the recommendations from the FEMA review council in which he was a part of. 

Also part of the FEMA review council’s recommendations, was a call to privatize flood insurance. Privatizing flood insurance could increase insurance rates for people and increase profits for insurance companies. Whatley held stock in several insurance companies that would benefit from privatization and increased rates. 

Whatley suggested to Trump that he should abolish FEMA.

The FEMA Review Council’s Recommendations Which Whatley Helped Write, Recommended Scaling Back FEMA’s Role In Disaster Assistance 

HEADLINE: Trump Task Force Recommends Sweeping Changes To FEMA — But Not Eliminating It [NOLA, 5/7/26]

The Recommendation Include Raising The Threshold For Disaster Declaration. According to NPR, “The FEMA Review Council is recommending raising that threshold by more than 50% and also changing how it is calculated, which means states would only qualify with higher levels of damage. During Trump's first term, his administration made a similar proposal just before leaving office in 2020. The report finds that if this threshold had already been adjusted, ‘29% of disasters declared between 2012 and 2025 would not have met the indicator, representing $1.5 billion.’” [NPR, 5/7/26]

  • Raising The Threshold Could Potentially Leave States On The Hook For Millions Of Dollars To Rebuild Infrastructure. According to NPR, “That could leave states on the hook for millions of dollars to rebuild roads, schools and other infrastructure after tornadoes, floods and other disasters that do not cause enough widespread damage to meet the new criteria for federal assistance. Such local weather events have devastated rural communities in recent years.” [NPR, 5/7/26]

Whatley Helped Write FEMA Reform Plan Which Would Privatize Flood Insurance

Asheville Citizen-Times: Senate Hopeful Whatley Backed FEMA Plan That Could Boost His Portfolio [Asheville Citizen-Times, 7/7/26]

The Council Recommended Privatizing Flood Insurance. According to NPR, “The other major recommendation is to shrink the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), which provides the majority of residential flood insurance in the United States. The program has struggled financially since it was created in the 1960s, and in the last few decades it has been on the verge of collapse multiple times. […] The council recommends shifting some flood insurance policies to private insurance companies, and moving faster to map flood risk accurately across the country, so that fewer homes are in harm's way.” [NPR, 5/7/26]

The Final FEMA Review Council Report Called For “A Strategic Shift Toward A Primary Role For The Private Market” And Called For A Program To Move Flood Insurance Policies Into The Private Market. According to the Asheville Citizen-Times, “The council’s final report, released months late on May 7, called for ‘a strategic shift toward a primary role for the private market’ for the National Flood Insurance Program, including a ‘take-out’ program designed to move flood insurance policies from the federal program into private markets.” [Asheville Citizen-Times, 7/7/26]

The Privatization Of Flood Insurance Would Increase Rates. According to the Asheville Citizen-Times, “The NFIP is subsidized by taxpayers, which helps keep rates low on high-risk properties insurers are hesitant to cover. In January, insurance shopping website Insurify noted that 90% of all flood insurance polices in the U.S. were through the NFIP at an average annual cost of $934. In the private market, those policies could end up costing more than $1,500 if the council’s recommendations are adopted.” [Asheville Citizen-Times, 7/7/26]

Whatley Owned Between $36,008 And $190,000 In Insurance Stocks Of Berkshire Hathaway, Fairfax Financial Holdings And Progressive Corporation Stock. According to the Asheville Citizen-Times, “Whatley’s 2025 Senate financial disclosure shows a $461,000 salary in 2025, when he served as chair of the Republican National Committee, and between $3.5 million and $12 million in investments. A small part of that portfolio is between $36,008 and $190,000 in Berkshire Hathaway, Fairfax Financial Holdings and Progressive Corporation stock.” [Asheville Citizen-Times, 7/7/26]

  • Berkshire Hathaway’s Subsidiaries Owned 16.6 Percent Of The U.S. Flood Insurance Market And Held The Largest Market Share. According to the Asheville Citizen-Times, “Through its subsidiaries, Berkshire Hathaway has the largest market share of the U.S. flood insurance market with 16.6%, according to Insurance Business, an industry magazine for insurance professionals. The company reported $67 billion in net income for 2025 across its insurance, manufacturing and transportation holdings.” [Asheville Citizen-Times, 7/7/26]
  • Fairfax’s Subsidiaries Offered Flood Insurance. According to the Asheville Citizen-Times, “Fairfax, also through subsidiaries, offers insurance and reinsurance including property coverage for losses from flooding. In 2025, the company reported $4.7 billion in net income.” [Asheville Citizen-Times, 7/7/26]
  • Progressive Held 5 Percent Of The U.S. Flood Insurance Market. According to the Asheville Citizen-Times, “That same year, Progressive reported $11.3 billion in net income. The company claims 5% of the U.S. flood insurance market in the, according to Insurance Business.” [Asheville Citizen-Times, 7/7/26]
