Overview

Lahn co-founded a private school in Kansas and touted Wonder, Inc. as a model for reinventing schools despite years of deficits and owing the Lahns $4.5 million by 2025. Public records exposed Wonder, Inc. relied heavily on outside funding, while the amount owed to the Lahns grew to $4.5 million and the Lahns’ roles as both school officers and major creditors triggered annual conflict-of-interest disclosures and went against IRS recommendations for nonprofit governance. In Wonder, Inc’s 990 for FY 2025, the school reported owing the Lahns more than $2.68 million each, which was more than seven times the amount originally loaned. 

In 2023, Lahn’s handgun and ammunition were stolen from Wonder, Inc. the school Lahn co-founded, and his campaign declined to explain how the gun was left somewhere it could be stolen. He later supported legislation to expand gun rights, including allowing permit holders to carry firearms on school property, arguing armed adults could help “protect” kids.

Lahn pushed policies that would expand taxpayer-funded private school vouchers while increasing scrutiny of Iowa’s public school teachers. Lahn proposed eliminating Iowa’s teacher licensing board and revoking the licenses of teachers accused of “indoctrination” or promoting a “political agenda” in the classroom. His proposals came despite co-founding Wonder, Inc., an unaccredited private school in Kansas that was not required to hire licensed teachers. Wonder, Inc. was not equipped to serve all students with special education needs

Lahn supported expanding school vouchers despite concerns the program diverted public funds to private schools and strained Iowa public schools. At the same time, Wonder, Inc., the private school Lahn co-founded, charged families up to $10,000 in annual tuition. Meanwhile, Iowa’s voucher program cost taxpayers more than $314 million in 2025-2026, subsidized 99% of private school students, and was projected to cost $350 million by 2027.

The SBA took a security interest in Wonder Inc.’s property, which was extended in 2025 for five years. Zach and Annie Lahn, the only publicly disclosed members of Wonder’s board of trustees, responded to the school’s financial distress with loans of $615,000.
