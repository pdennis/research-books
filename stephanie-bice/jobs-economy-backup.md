# Rep. Stephanie Bice (OK-05): Jobs and the Economy

Rep. Stephanie Bice says she is fighting for Oklahoma jobs, but her record tells a different story. She voted against the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law that sent $6.4 billion to Oklahoma for roads, bridges, and broadband — creating thousands of construction and engineering jobs — then tried to take credit for $20 million in earmarks she put in the bill she voted against. She voted for the "One Big Beautiful Bill," whose Medicaid and SNAP cuts are projected to eliminate 1.22 million jobs nationwide and reduce state GDPs by $154 billion, while Oklahoma hospitals face $8.7 billion in losses that threaten to close already-struggling rural facilities and the jobs they support. She voted to protect Trump's 25% tariffs on Canadian imports that economists say are driving up grocery prices and squeezing Oklahoma wheat and cattle producers with a "double squeeze" of lower export prices and higher input costs. She cheered DOGE's federal workforce cuts while approximately 30,000 federal workers in the Oklahoma City metro — nearly 4% of the local workforce — faced layoffs, uncertainty, and spending cutbacks, with each federal job cut costing an estimated 1.2 additional jobs in the local economy. And she voted against the PRO Act, which would have strengthened the right to organize for Oklahoma workers. Oklahoma's hiring rate fell sharply in the second half of 2025, and only 16% of Oklahoma firms expect to add jobs in 2026 — the third-lowest reading in a decade. Bice's priorities are tax cuts for the top 1% and loyalty to Trump's agenda, not the working families of OK-05.

### Voted Against The Infrastructure Law That Sent Billions To Oklahoma For Roads, Bridges, And Jobs

**Bice Voted Against The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law That Directed $6.4 Billion To Oklahoma.** According to Gaylord News, Oklahoma's Republican House members voted against the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, which directed nearly $5 billion to Oklahoma for roads and bridges, with additional funds for broadband, water, and energy projects totaling $6.4 billion. Oklahoma has 2,326 bridges and over 1,004 miles of highway in poor condition. [Gaylord News, [11/6/21](https://gaylordnews.net/6956/news/oklahoma-house-representatives-vote-against-bill-that-includes-oklahoma-projects/)]

- **Bice Earmarked $20 Million In The Same Infrastructure Bill She Voted Against.** According to the Norman Transcript, Bice requested and received $20 million in earmarks in the bill — $10 million for bridges on I-35 at interchanges between Memorial and 2nd Street in Edmond, and $10 million for the I-35/I-240 interchange in Oklahoma City — then voted against the overall package. [Norman Transcript, [7/2/21](https://www.normantranscript.com/news/oklahoma-delegation-opposes-infrastructure-bill-despite-earmarks/article_fafd4e98-dac1-11eb-ab8f-e76aed4c2bc9.html)]

- **Bice Called The Bipartisan Infrastructure Investment "Reckless Spending."** According to a column by Rep. Bice, she characterized the bipartisan infrastructure package as part of a "reckless spending spree" and claimed the bills "include minimal true infrastructure projects." The bill passed with bipartisan support — 13 House Republicans voted for it — but Bice was not among them. [Bice.House.Gov, [9/29/21](https://bice.house.gov/media/weekly-columns/infrastructure-package-vote-against-reckless-spending)]

### Voted For A Bill That Economists Project Will Eliminate Over A Million Jobs

**Bice Voted For The "One Big Beautiful Bill," Whose Medicaid And SNAP Cuts Are Projected To Eliminate 1.22 Million Jobs Nationwide.** According to the Commonwealth Fund, cuts to Medicaid and SNAP in the reconciliation law would cause state gross domestic products to fall by $154 billion and result in the loss of 1.22 million jobs across the country by 2029 due to reduced healthcare and food spending. [Commonwealth Fund, [6/2025](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/jun/how-medicaid-snap-cutbacks-one-big-beautiful-bill-trigger-job-losses-states)]

- **The Bill Threatens $8.7 Billion In Losses For Oklahoma Hospitals, Putting Rural Health Care Jobs At Risk.** According to the Oklahoma Indian Care Association, the Big Beautiful Bill's Medicaid cuts are estimated to lead to a loss of $8.7 billion for Oklahoma hospitals. Nine rural Oklahoma hospitals have already closed since 2005, and 67% of rural facilities operate at losses. [OICA, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.oica.org/bbb-oklahoma); KGOU, [5/22/25](https://www.kgou.org/health/2025-05-22/oklahoma-health-care-authority-addresses-impacts-to-medicaid-proposed-in-house-budget-bill)]

- **The Oklahoma Policy Institute Called The Bill "A Transfer Of Wealth From America's Poor And Middle Class Into The Pockets Of The Nation's Richest 1 Percent."** According to the Oklahoma Policy Institute, the legislation "threatens up to 1 in 6 Oklahomans who get their healthcare from SoonerCare" and delivers over $50,000 per year in tax cuts to the richest 1% while cutting the safety net programs working families depend on. [Oklahoma Policy Institute, [5/24/25](https://okpolicy.org/oklahoma-policy-institute-condemns-u-s-house-passage-of-federal-budget-bill-that-harms-vulnerable-oklahomans/)]

- **Bice Dismissed Criticism Of The Bill's Cuts As "Fear-Mongering."** According to KFOR, Bice said the bill was "about delivering real support to hardworking Americans" and dismissed concerns about Medicaid and SNAP cuts as "fear-mongering from Democrats." Her statement made no mention of the bill's projected job losses, $186 billion in SNAP cuts, or $880 billion in Medicaid cuts. [KFOR, [5/22/25](https://kfor.com/news/oklahoma-legislature/oklahoma-congressmembers-vote-to-pass-big-beautiful-bill-as-critics-warn-of-sweeping-cuts-to-social-services-healthcare/)]

### Voted To Protect Tariffs That Are Squeezing Oklahoma Farmers And Raising Grocery Prices

**Bice Voted Against Ending Trump's 25% Tariffs On Canadian Imports.** According to the Clerk of the House, Bice voted "Nay" on H.J.Res. 72, a joint resolution to terminate the national emergency declaration used to impose 25% tariffs on Canadian imports. The resolution passed 219-211, with only six Republicans crossing over. [Clerk of the House, [2/11/26](https://clerk.house.gov/evs/2026/roll065.xml)]

- **Oklahoma Wheat Farmers Faced A "Double Squeeze" From Tariffs — Lower Prices And Higher Input Costs.** According to KGOU, OSU Extension agriculture and food policy specialist Amy Hagerman said, "Wheat producers are an example of a segment of the agricultural sector that could get double squeezed by tariffs." Hagerman noted that "wheat has had lower prices in the last few years — it was already under some pressure there," and tariffs on imported fertilizer and machinery components are driving up costs simultaneously. Oklahoma exported nearly $2.4 billion in agricultural products in 2024. [KGOU, [4/22/25](https://www.kgou.org/business-and-economy/2025-04-22/how-will-tariffs-impact-oklahomas-agriculture-industry)]

- **OSU Economists Warned Tariffs Were Slowing Oklahoma's Economy And Projected Sharp GDP Deceleration.** According to Oklahoma State University, economists warned of sharp economic deceleration driven by "increased tariffs, reduced immigration, federal government dysfunction and uncertainty in Federal Reserve strategy," projecting U.S. GDP growth to fall from 3.8% in Q2 2025 to 0.8% in Q4. Oklahoma is projected to lag the national economy through most of 2026 due to weaker energy markets and slower workforce gains. [Oklahoma State University, [11/24/25](https://news.okstate.edu/articles/business/2025/economists_warn_slowing_growth.html)]

### Championed DOGE While 30,000 Oklahoma City Federal Workers Faced Layoffs And Uncertainty

**Bice Introduced The DOGE Act And Praised Federal Workforce Cuts While Approximately 30,000 Federal Workers In The Oklahoma City Metro Faced Uncertainty.** According to Fox Business, the Oklahoma City metro area has approximately 30,000 federal workers — nearly 4% of the local workforce — employed across the FAA, military installations, federal prisons, and other agencies. Bice introduced the DOGE Act and praised the effort to eliminate "waste," calling it "a significant leap forward in rethinking how our federal government operates." [Fox Business, [3/19/25](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/doge-cuts-rippling-through-local-economies-around-us); Bice.House.Gov, [12/10/24](https://bice.house.gov/media/weekly-columns/doge)]

- **FAA Workers At Oklahoma City's Mike Monroney Aeronautical Center Said They Felt "Betrayed" After Being Laid Off.** According to KFOR, FAA employees at the Mike Monroney Aeronautical Center in Oklahoma City — a hub for the agency's training operations — were among hundreds of federal workers fired as part of DOGE cuts. Former employees said they "feel betrayed and abandoned" and that they "are not faceless bureaucrats, but everyday people — your friends, your neighbors — who are now without a paycheck." [KFOR, [2/25/25](https://kfor.com/news/local/oklahoma-city-faa-workers-axed-in-federal-layoffs-feel-betrayed-concerned-by-rhetoric/)]

- **Each Federal Job Cut Costs An Estimated 1.2 Additional Jobs In The Local Economy.** According to Fox Business, Purdue University economics professor Ben Zou found that "when a military base downsizes, most of its dismissed workers pack up and leave," and each federal job elimination results in 1.2 additional job losses in the surrounding economy as affected workers reduce spending. Federal workers impacted by layoffs reported delaying vehicle purchases and cutting back on restaurant and entertainment spending. [Fox Business, [3/19/25](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/doge-cuts-rippling-through-local-economies-around-us)]

- **Bice Misled Constituents About The Scope Of Federal Cuts, Citing 10 VA Employees To Justify 1,000 Layoffs.** According to ReadFrontier, Bice told constituents at a town hall that the VA "has been processing payments for the HHS Office of Refugee Settlement. That's not their mission and they shouldn't be doing that." ReadFrontier's fact-check found the claim was true but misleading: the refugee payment work involved no more than 10 VA employees — a tiny fraction of the 1,000 VA jobs being eliminated. [ReadFrontier, [2/2025](https://www.readfrontier.org/stories/we-fact-checked-what-oklahoma-politicians-are-saying-about-spending-cuts/)]

### Oklahoma's Hiring Outlook Is The Worst In A Decade — And Bice's Priorities Are Elsewhere

**Oklahoma's Hiring Rate Fell Sharply In Late 2025, Ending The Year Below The National Average.** According to the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City, the hiring rate in Oklahoma fell sharply to 3.1% in the second half of 2025, ending the year below the national average. Hiring stayed below pre-pandemic levels throughout 2025. [Kansas City Fed, [2/2026](https://www.kansascityfed.org/oklahomacity/oklahoma-economist/hiring-in-oklahoma-slows-but-unemployment-remains-low/)]

- **Only 16% Of Oklahoma Firms Expected To Add Jobs In 2026 — The Third-Lowest Reading In A Decade.** According to the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City, only a net 16% of Oklahoma firms expected to increase employment in the next 12 months, the third-lowest reading in the last decade. Most firms reported they do not expect to increase employment, citing "uncertainty and an expected cyclical slowdown in sales growth." Over a third of firms said they did not intend to raise wages heading into 2026. [Kansas City Fed, [2/2026](https://www.kansascityfed.org/oklahomacity/oklahoma-economist/hiring-in-oklahoma-slows-but-unemployment-remains-low/)]

**Bice Voted Against The PRO Act, Which Would Have Strengthened Organizing Rights For Oklahoma Workers.** According to OnTheIssues, Bice voted against the Protecting the Right to Organize Act, which would have expanded labor protections for collective bargaining and organizing. The bill passed the House 225-206. [OnTheIssues, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://house.ontheissues.org/House/Stephanie_Bice_Jobs.htm)]

### Impact On Everyday People

**Oklahoma City FAA Workers Called Themselves "Your Neighbors, Friends And Family" After Losing Jobs To DOGE Cuts.** According to KFOR, former FAA employees at the Mike Monroney Aeronautical Center in Oklahoma City said they "are not faceless bureaucrats, but everyday people — your friends, your neighbors — who are now without a paycheck." Laid-off workers included maintenance mechanics, aeronautical information specialists, and aviation safety assistants. [KFOR, [2/25/25](https://kfor.com/news/local/oklahoma-city-faa-workers-axed-in-federal-layoffs-feel-betrayed-concerned-by-rhetoric/)]

**Federal Workers In Oklahoma Cut Back Spending After Layoffs, Hurting Local Businesses.** According to Fox Business, displaced federal workers in Oklahoma reported cutting consumer spending — delaying vehicle purchases, reducing restaurant visits, and curtailing entertainment spending — creating ripple effects throughout the local economy. [Fox Business, [3/19/25](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/doge-cuts-rippling-through-local-economies-around-us)]

**Historic SNAP Cuts Put 131,000 Oklahomans At Risk Of Losing Food Benefits, Including 255,000 Children Statewide.** According to Oklahoma Watch, "historic cuts to SNAP put 131,000 Oklahomans at risk of losing benefits." SNAP served nearly 700,000 Oklahomans in 2024 — about 1 in 6 residents — including 255,000 children and 68,000 seniors. Oklahoma could lose $628 million in SNAP funding in 2029 alone, a 39.3% reduction. [Oklahoma Watch, [7/24/25](https://oklahomawatch.org/2025/07/24/historic-cuts-to-snap-put-131000-oklahomans-at-risk-of-losing-benefits/)]

**Nine Rural Oklahoma Hospitals Have Closed Since 2005, And 59% Of Remaining Rural Facilities Face Closure Risk.** According to KGOU, nine rural Oklahoma hospitals have closed since 2005, and "67% of rural facilities operate at losses" with "59% facing closure risk." The reconciliation law's Medicaid cuts would further reduce revenue for these already-struggling hospitals, threatening the jobs of health care workers across rural Oklahoma. [KGOU, [5/22/25](https://www.kgou.org/health/2025-05-22/oklahoma-health-care-authority-addresses-impacts-to-medicaid-proposed-in-house-budget-bill)]
