# Rep. Stephanie Bice (OK-05): Affordability

Rep. Stephanie Bice has consistently sided against Oklahoma families on affordability. She voted to protect Trump's 25% tariffs on Canadian imports — tariffs that economists say are driving up grocery prices, which have already risen 29% since 2020 — while Oklahoma wheat and cattle producers face a "double squeeze" of lower export prices and higher input costs. She voted for the "One Big Beautiful Bill," which cuts $186 billion from SNAP and threatens to strip food benefits from 131,000 Oklahomans, including 255,000 children statewide who depend on the program. She voted against capping insulin at $35 a month, against allowing Medicare to negotiate drug prices, and against a bill to prevent gas price gouging — then called the gas price bill "socialist." Meanwhile, more than 7 in 10 extremely low-income Oklahoma renters spend over half their income on housing, and the state needs 85,000 more affordable rental units. Bice frames her record as preventing a "$1,200 tax hike," but the bill she voted for delivers over $50,000 a year in tax cuts to the top 1% while cutting the safety net programs her constituents rely on most.

### Voted To Protect Trump's Tariffs That Are Raising Prices On Oklahoma Families

**Bice Voted Against Ending Trump's 25% Tariffs On Canadian Imports.** According to the Clerk of the House, Bice voted "Nay" on H.J.Res. 72, a joint resolution to terminate the national emergency declaration used to impose 25% tariffs on Canadian imports. The resolution passed 219-211, with only six Republicans crossing over to vote in favor. Bice was not among them. [Clerk of the House, [2/11/26](https://clerk.house.gov/evs/2026/roll065.xml)]

- **Trump Threatened Republicans Who Voted To End The Tariffs.** According to NBC News, Trump warned via Truth Social that Republicans who voted against the tariffs would "seriously suffer the consequences come Election time." Only six Republicans defected — Bice sided with Trump. [NBC News, [2/11/26](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/house-votes-end-trump-tariffs-canada-rcna258661)]

**Grocery Prices Rose 29% Since 2020, And Economists Said Tariffs Are A Key Driver.** According to KGOU, grocery prices have increased 29% since February 2020. Michigan State University food economist David Ortega said, "Tariffs are simply a tax on imported goods. So that raises the cost, and ultimately consumers see that at the grocery store." [KGOU, [9/19/25](https://www.kgou.org/business-and-economy/2025-09-19/grocery-prices-have-jumped-up-and-theres-no-relief-in-sight)]

**Oklahoma Wheat Farmers Faced A "Double Squeeze" From Tariffs — Lower Prices And Higher Input Costs.** According to KGOU, OSU Extension agriculture and food policy specialist Amy Hagerman said, "Wheat producers are an example of a segment of the agricultural sector that could get double squeezed by tariffs." Hagerman noted that "wheat has had lower prices in the last few years — it was already under some pressure there," and tariffs on imported fertilizer and machinery components are driving up costs simultaneously. Oklahoma exported nearly $2.4 billion in agricultural products in 2024. [KGOU, [4/22/25](https://www.kgou.org/business-and-economy/2025-04-22/how-will-tariffs-impact-oklahomas-agriculture-industry)]

- **OSU Economists Warned Tariffs Were Slowing Oklahoma's Economy.** According to Oklahoma State University, economists warned of sharp economic deceleration driven by "increased tariffs, reduced immigration, federal government dysfunction and uncertainty in Federal Reserve strategy," projecting U.S. GDP growth to fall to 0.8% in Q4 after 3.8% in Q2 2025. [Oklahoma State University, [2025](https://news.okstate.edu/articles/business/2025/economists_warn_slowing_growth.html)]

### Voted For Massive SNAP Cuts That Threaten Food Benefits For 131,000 Oklahomans

**Bice Voted For The "One Big Beautiful Bill," Which Cuts $186 Billion From SNAP And Puts 131,000 Oklahomans At Risk Of Losing Food Benefits.** According to Oklahoma Watch, "historic cuts to SNAP put 131,000 Oklahomans at risk of losing benefits." SNAP served nearly 700,000 Oklahomans in 2024 — about 1 in 6 residents — including 255,000 children and 68,000 seniors. Oklahoma could lose $628 million in SNAP funding in 2029 alone, a 39.3% reduction. [Oklahoma Watch, [7/24/25](https://oklahomawatch.org/2025/07/24/historic-cuts-to-snap-put-131000-oklahomans-at-risk-of-losing-benefits/)]

- **The Oklahoma Policy Institute Condemned The Bill As A Wealth Transfer From The Poor To The Rich.** According to the Oklahoma Policy Institute, the legislation "threatens up to 1 in 6 Oklahomans who get their healthcare from SoonerCare" and characterized it as "a transfer of wealth from America's poor and middle class into the pockets of the nation's richest 1 percent." [Oklahoma Policy Institute, [5/24/25](https://okpolicy.org/oklahoma-policy-institute-condemns-u-s-house-passage-of-federal-budget-bill-that-harms-vulnerable-oklahomans/)]

**The Bill's Tax Cuts Flowed Overwhelmingly To The Wealthiest Americans, Not Oklahoma Families.** According to CNBC, the "One Big Beautiful Bill" tax provisions deliver more than $50,000 per year in tax cuts to the richest 1% and more than $14,700 to the top 10%, while the bottom 80% of earners see no benefit from the SALT cap increase to $40,000. The bill permanently raised the estate tax exemption to $15 million per person. [CNBC, [5/23/25](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/23/house-republican-big-beautiful-tax-bill-favors-the-rich.html)]

- **Bice Framed The Bill As Preventing A "$1,200 Tax Hike" But Did Not Mention SNAP Or Medicaid Cuts.** According to a press release from Rep. Bice's office, she said, "The One Big Beautiful Bill is an opportunity to extend tax relief for Oklahomans, preventing them from facing a nearly $1200 tax hike next year." The statement made no mention of the bill's $186 billion in SNAP cuts or $880 billion in Medicaid cuts. [Bice.House.Gov, [5/22/25](https://bice.house.gov/media/press-releases/bice-passage-one-big-beautiful-bill)]

### Voted Against Every Bill That Would Have Lowered Costs For Families

**Bice Voted Against Capping Insulin At $35 A Month Despite Over 450,000 Oklahomans Living With Diabetes.** According to KTUL, all five Oklahoma Republican congress members voted against the Affordable Insulin Now Act, which would have capped insulin copays at $35 per month for those with private insurance. The bill passed the House 232-193. Bice did not respond to requests for comment explaining her vote. Over 450,000 Oklahomans have diabetes, with Type 1 patients potentially facing $800-$1,000 in monthly insulin costs. [KTUL, [3/31/22](https://ktul.com/news/local/all-oklahoma-republican-congress-members-vote-no-on-bill-capping-insulin-prices)]

**Bice Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Medicare Insulin At $35 And Allowed Medicare To Negotiate Drug Prices.** According to a press release from Rep. Bice's office, she voted against the Inflation Reduction Act on August 12, 2022, calling it a "partisan bill" that would "increase taxes on Americans." The law capped insulin copays at $35 per month for Medicare beneficiaries and allowed Medicare to negotiate prices on high-cost prescription drugs for the first time. [Bice.House.Gov, [8/12/22](https://bice.house.gov/media/press-releases/bice-opposes-democrats-partisan-budget-bill)]

**Bice Voted Against A Gas Price Gouging Bill And Called It "Socialist."** According to a column by Rep. Bice, she opposed the Consumer Fuel Price Gouging Prevention Act, calling it the "Socialist Energy Price Fixing Act" and arguing it would lead to "long gas lines, rationing, and a greater reliance on imported energy." She said, "The price of gasoline is determined on a global market, therefore the way to lower prices is simple — to increase domestic supply." The bill passed the House 217-207. [Bice.House.Gov, [5/26/22](https://bice.house.gov/media/weekly-columns/democrats-socialist-energy-price-fixing-act)]

### Voted To Gut Medicaid And Let ACA Premiums Spike

**Bice Voted For The "One Big Beautiful Bill," Which Could Strip Medicaid Coverage From Up To 174,000 Oklahomans.** According to KFF, the reconciliation law's Medicaid provisions could cause up to 174,000 Oklahomans to lose SoonerCare coverage. The bill cuts $880 billion from Medicaid over the next decade and imposes work requirements designed to reduce enrollment. [KFF, [6/4/25](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/allocating-cbos-estimates-of-federal-medicaid-spending-reductions-and-enrollment-loss-across-the-states/)]

**Oklahoma ACA Premiums Rose An Average Of 20-21% After Enhanced Subsidies Expired, With Some Plans Spiking 60-75%.** According to KTUL, Oklahoma ACA insurance premiums surged after enhanced federal tax credits expired at the end of 2025, with a "statewide average increase of 20-21%" and "some plans rising 60-75%." Nearly 300,000 Oklahomans relied on ACA subsidies, and 14-15% of enrollees dropped coverage entirely rather than pay higher costs. [KTUL, [2/3/26](https://ktul.com/news/local/oklahoma-aca-insurance-premiums-to-rise-as-federal-subsidies-expire-medical-costs-coverage-medicaid-public-health-funding)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**More Than 7 In 10 Extremely Low-Income Oklahoma Renters Spent Over Half Their Income On Housing.** According to the Oklahoma Policy Institute, "more than 7 out of 10 extremely low-income families who rent in Oklahoma are severely cost-burdened, spending more than 50 percent of their income on rent." Oklahoma needs nearly 85,000 additional rental homes for extremely low-income renters — 7,374 more units than the year before, a roughly 10% decline in availability. Only 38 affordable homes are available for every 100 extremely low-income families. [Oklahoma Policy Institute, [5/29/25](https://okpolicy.org/new-data-shows-oklahomas-housing-crisis-is-worsening/)]

**A Majority Of Americans Reported Stress Over Rising Grocery Costs.** According to KGOU, 53% of Americans reported grocery prices as a significant source of stress. Grocery prices have risen 29% since February 2020, with the most recent monthly jump being the biggest in nearly three years. [KGOU, [9/19/25](https://www.kgou.org/business-and-economy/2025-09-19/grocery-prices-have-jumped-up-and-theres-no-relief-in-sight)]

**Nine Rural Oklahoma Hospitals Have Closed Since 2005, And Medicaid Cuts Threaten More.** According to KGOU, nine rural Oklahoma hospitals have closed since 2005, and "67% of rural facilities operate at losses" with "59% facing closure risk." The reconciliation law's Medicaid cuts would further reduce revenue for these already-struggling hospitals. [KGOU, [5/22/25](https://www.kgou.org/health/2025-05-22/oklahoma-health-care-authority-addresses-impacts-to-medicaid-proposed-in-house-budget-bill)]
