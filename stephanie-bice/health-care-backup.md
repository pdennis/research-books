# Rep. Stephanie Bice (OK-05): Health Care

Rep. Stephanie Bice has repeatedly broken her promises on health care. While campaigning in 2020, Bice pledged to protect pre-existing conditions, lower prescription drug costs, and make health care more affordable for Oklahoma families. In office, she voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, which capped insulin at $35 per month and allowed Medicare to negotiate prescription drug prices for the first time. She then voted for the "One Big Beautiful Bill," which could strip Medicaid coverage from up to 174,000 Oklahomans, impose work requirements on SoonerCare enrollees, and let enhanced ACA subsidies expire — driving Oklahoma premiums up by an average of 20-21%, with some plans spiking 60-75%. Bice campaigned on replacing the ACA with health savings accounts, an approach experts say would leave low-income Oklahomans unable to afford meaningful coverage.

### Promised to Lower Drug Costs, Then Voted Against Every Bill That Did

**Bice Campaigned On Lowering Prescription Drug Costs, Including Insulin And EpiPens.** According to ReadFrontier, Bice said during her 2020 campaign that she supported "lowering prescription drug costs" and her campaign specifically cited "life-saving drugs like insulin and Epi-pens" as priorities. [ReadFrontier, [10/21/20](https://www.readfrontier.org/stories/horn-vows-to-protect-affordable-care-act-bice-promotes-use-of-health-savings-accounts-as-a-replacement/)]

**Bice Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Insulin At $35 And Let Medicare Negotiate Drug Prices.** According to a press release from Rep. Bice's office, she voted against the Inflation Reduction Act on August 12, 2022, calling it a "partisan bill" that would "increase taxes on Americans." The law capped insulin copays at $35 per month for Medicare beneficiaries and allowed Medicare to negotiate prices on high-cost prescription drugs for the first time. [Bice.House.Gov, [8/12/22](https://bice.house.gov/media/press-releases/bice-opposes-democrats-partisan-budget-bill)]

### Voted to Gut Medicaid Coverage for 174,000 Oklahomans

**Bice Voted For The "One Big Beautiful Bill," Which Could Strip Medicaid Coverage From Up To 174,000 Oklahomans.** According to KFF, the reconciliation law's Medicaid provisions could cause up to 174,000 Oklahomans to lose SoonerCare coverage. Bice was one of all five Oklahoma representatives who voted for passage. The bill cuts $880 billion from Medicaid over the next decade and imposes work requirements designed to reduce enrollment. [KFF, [6/4/25](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/allocating-cbos-estimates-of-federal-medicaid-spending-reductions-and-enrollment-loss-across-the-states/)]

- **The Oklahoma Policy Institute Condemned The Bill As A Wealth Transfer From The Poor To The Rich.** According to the Oklahoma Policy Institute, the legislation "threatens up to 1 in 6 Oklahomans who get their healthcare from SoonerCare" and characterized it as "a transfer of wealth from America's poor and middle class into the pockets of the nation's richest 1 percent." [Oklahoma Policy Institute, [5/24/25](https://okpolicy.org/oklahoma-policy-institute-condemns-u-s-house-passage-of-federal-budget-bill-that-harms-vulnerable-oklahomans/)]

- **The Bill Imposes New Work Requirements That Experts Say Will Push Eligible Oklahomans Off SoonerCare.** According to the Oklahoma Policy Institute, the law's frequent re-verification requirements create "churn — repeated cycles of coverage loss and re-enrollment due to paperwork issues rather than actual ineligibility." The policy institute warned that "working parents, caregivers, low-paid workers, and people with disabilities are among those most at risk." [Oklahoma Policy Institute, [10/23/25](https://okpolicy.org/how-the-not-so-beautiful-bill-will-push-eligible-oklahomans-off-soonercare/)]

**Nine Rural Oklahoma Hospitals Have Closed Since 2005, And The Bill Threatens More.** According to KGOU, nine rural Oklahoma hospitals have closed since 2005, and "67% of rural facilities operate at losses" with "59% facing closure risk." The reconciliation law's Medicaid cuts would further reduce revenue for these already-struggling hospitals. [KGOU, [5/22/25](https://www.kgou.org/health/2025-05-22/oklahoma-health-care-authority-addresses-impacts-to-medicaid-proposed-in-house-budget-bill)]

### Let ACA Subsidies Expire, Spiking Oklahoma Premiums

**Oklahoma ACA Premiums Rose An Average Of 20-21% After Enhanced Subsidies Expired, With Some Plans Spiking 60-75%.** According to KTUL, Oklahoma ACA insurance premiums surged after enhanced federal tax credits expired at the end of 2025, with a "statewide average increase of 20-21%" and "some plans rising 60-75%." The Oklahoma Insurance Department attributed the increases to the subsidy expiration, rising medical costs, and the state's overall health status. [KTUL, [2/3/26](https://ktul.com/news/local/oklahoma-aca-insurance-premiums-to-rise-as-federal-subsidies-expire-medical-costs-coverage-medicaid-public-health-funding)]

- **Nearly 300,000 Oklahomans Who Relied On ACA Subsidies Faced Sharply Higher Costs.** According to the Oklahoma Policy Institute, "94%, or almost 300,000 people" enrolled in Oklahoma's ACA Marketplace relied on premium tax credits, and "average Marketplace premiums would more than double" without the enhanced subsidies. For example, a 60-year-old couple earning $85,000 would see their annual premium jump from $7,225 to $30,784 — an increase of $23,559. [Oklahoma Policy Institute, [11/7/25](https://okpolicy.org/what-oklahomans-need-to-know-about-expiring-aca-premium-tax-credits/)]

### Proposed Replacing Insurance With Health Savings Accounts for Low-Income Oklahomans

**Bice Proposed Replacing ACA Coverage For Low-Income Oklahomans With Health Savings Accounts.** According to ReadFrontier, Bice said during the 2020 campaign, "'Instead of offering coverage specifically to individuals under this very strict set of guidelines, (give) them a set amount every year through a health savings account' where individuals choose their doctors and services." In a separate interview, she stated, "'Instead of providing insurance, let's get an individual a health savings account.'" [ReadFrontier, [10/21/20](https://www.readfrontier.org/stories/horn-vows-to-protect-affordable-care-act-bice-promotes-use-of-health-savings-accounts-as-a-replacement/)]

- **Bice's HSA Proposal Drew Immediate Criticism For Leaving Low-Income Oklahomans Without Real Coverage.** According to ReadFrontier, Bice's opponent Kendra Horn "launched a television advertisement claiming Bice wanted to eliminate health insurance coverage for some Oklahomans." HSAs require individuals to pay out-of-pocket for care and are widely regarded as insufficient for low-income families who cannot afford to save. [ReadFrontier, [10/21/20](https://www.readfrontier.org/stories/horn-vows-to-protect-affordable-care-act-bice-promotes-use-of-health-savings-accounts-as-a-replacement/)]

**Bice Opposed Medicaid Expansion Even As Oklahoma Voters Approved It.** According to the DCCC, Bice "opposed expanding health care coverage to more low-income adults" despite Oklahoma voters approving Medicaid expansion via State Question 802 in June 2020. More than 330,000 Oklahomans have since gained coverage through the expansion. [DCCC, [8/25/20](https://dccc.org/the-case-against-stephanie-bice/)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**Oklahoma ACA Enrollees Faced Sticker Shock As Premiums Doubled.** According to the Oklahoma Policy Institute, a family of four earning $130,000 would see their annual ACA premium jump from $11,050 to $23,173 — an increase of $12,123 per year. A single 45-year-old earning $64,000 would see premiums rise from $5,440 to $8,189 annually. The Oklahoma Insurance Department confirmed that 14-15% of enrollees dropped insurance coverage entirely rather than pay the higher costs. [Oklahoma Policy Institute, [11/7/25](https://okpolicy.org/what-oklahomans-need-to-know-about-expiring-aca-premium-tax-credits/); KTUL, [2/3/26](https://ktul.com/news/local/oklahoma-aca-insurance-premiums-to-rise-as-federal-subsidies-expire-medical-costs-coverage-medicaid-public-health-funding)]

**When Oklahomans Lose Coverage, Costs Shift To Taxpayers And Hospitals.** According to KTUL, Oklahoma Insurance Department deputy commissioner Mike Rhoads explained that uninsured Oklahomans "typically [...] receive care through clinics and hospitals as uncompensated care." KGOU reported that 67% of Oklahoma's rural hospital facilities already operate at losses, and the Medicaid cuts would further strain their finances. [KTUL, [2/3/26](https://ktul.com/news/local/oklahoma-aca-insurance-premiums-to-rise-as-federal-subsidies-expire-medical-costs-coverage-medicaid-public-health-funding); KGOU, [5/22/25](https://www.kgou.org/health/2025-05-22/oklahoma-health-care-authority-addresses-impacts-to-medicaid-proposed-in-house-budget-bill)]
