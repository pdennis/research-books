Overview

Collins repeatedly exchanged donations for contracts that went to Navatek. She asked, then CEO Martin Kao, to host a fundraiser and bundle tens of thousands of towards her campaign in exchange for funding in the annual defense budget. 

Collins asked for additional donations, saying “you’ve seen me deliver.” Kao And Navatek funneled donations though a shell company to Collins’ super PAC in exchange for additional funding. 

The projects that federal funds were being used for were going towards research that didn’t result in use by the military. 

Collins Was At The Center Of A Pay-To-Play Probe By The FBI 

ProPublica: The FBI Anti-Corruption Squad Was Circling Susan Collins — Until Trump Got In The Way [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Collins Traded Contracts For Donations From Navatek 

Navatek CEO, Martin Kao, Met With Collins 

Kao Claimed That The First Time Meeting Collins, He Had To Donate To Her Campaign First. According to ProPublica, “The first time Kao came face-to-face with Collins, in 2018, he told the FBI, he had to pay for the privilege. Collins would not meet unless he agreed to donate to her campaign, he said. While it is not illegal for politicians to exchange face time for contributions — in this case, just a few thousand dollars — it was not the last time Collins would seek Kao’s support.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Navatek Hired Glen Mandigo To Help Them Work With Collins. According to ProPublica, “To work with Collins, Navatek hired a lobbyist, Glen Mandigo, who also lobbied for the University of Maine and was tight with her office. Mandigo asked how much Navatek wanted in funding and how much Kao was willing to support Collins, Kao told the FBI. The University of Maine did not reply to a request for comment.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Kao Pitched A Research Project Between Navatek And Maine University And Collins Wanted Him To Host A Fundraiser 

Kao Claimed That He Pitched Collins And Her Staff In The First Meeting An $8 Million Boat Hull Research Project For Navatek And The University Of Maine And After That Mandigo Called him And Said Collins Wanted Him To Bundle Tens Of Thousands For Her Reelection Suggesting A Fundraiser. According to ProPublica, “In that first meeting with Collins and her staff, Kao pitched an $8 million boat hull research project for Navatek and the university. Collins seemed supportive. Not long after, Mandigo called Kao and said Collins wanted him to bundle tens of thousands of dollars for her reelection, suggesting Navatek throw a fundraiser, Kao said.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Navatek Hosted A Fundraiser For Collins At The Company’s Maine Headquarters. According to ProPublica, “The principals settled on hosting Collins for a publicity event at Navatek’s Maine headquarters in August 2019, where she posed for pictures with Kao and a model of the company’s experimental boat.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

  • Emails Showed That Her Staff Told The Navy To Steer Funding To Navatek, FBI Also Had Evidence Of Kao And Mandigo Planning A Fundraiser And Their Emails With Collin’s Scheduler And Campaign Finance Director Freely Mixed Talk Of Navatek’s Collins-Backed Contract With Plans To Raise Money For Her. According to ProPublica, “The FBI had spotted his out-of-the-blue donations in the summer of 2018, just before Collins included $8 million for Navatek’s proposal in the defense budget. Emails showed her staff made it clear to the Navy that it should send the money to Navatek. FBI agents also had evidence of Kao and Mandigo planning a fundraiser starting in April 2019. Their emails — with her scheduler and her campaign’s finance director — freely mixed talk of Navatek’s Collins-backed contract with plans to raise money for her.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Emails And Company Records Showed That Kao Orchestrated Over $400,000 In Donations From Extended Family And Told Collins’ Team To Reallocate His Excess Contributions To His Father Which Was A Violation Of Election Law. According to ProPublica, “Behind the scenes, the FBI saw in emails and company records, Kao orchestrated over $40,000 in donations from extended family in advance of the event. To avoid the legal cap on individual campaign contributions, the emails show, he told Collins’ team to reallocate his excess contributions to his father — which an agent highlighted and noted is against election law in a presentation to prosecutors — and sent them his father’s full name and address.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

  • Collins Finance Director, Amy Abbott, Emailed “This Is Perfect” About The Reallocating Of Donations To His Father. According to ProPublica, “‘This is perfect,’ Amy Abbott, the reelection campaign finance director, emailed Kao after discussing his father’s contribution. ‘We are so grateful for ALL the Kao support!’” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Before The Event, Kao Claimed That Collins, Her Campaign Finance Director And Another Staffer Met With Him Saying They Expected More Donations And That Collins Said “You’ve Seen Me Deliver.” According to ProPublica, “Before the event, Kao said, Collins, Abbott and another staffer met with him in private. One of the staffers told Kao the campaign expected more donations. It was in this meeting that Collins said, ‘You’ve seen me deliver,’ he told the FBI.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Less Than A Month After The Event, The Senate Released The Draft Defense budget Which Contained $21.5 Million For Navatek’s Pet Projects In Maine And Kao Emailed A Collins Campaign Fundraiser Saying “Thanks Again For All The Support From Sen Collins.” According to ProPublica, “Less than one month after the event, the Senate released a draft of the defense budget containing $21.5 million for Navatek’s pet projects in Maine. Kao emailed a Collins campaign fundraiser — who would in theory have nothing to do with a government contract — four days later, saying, ‘Thanks again for all the support from Sen Collins.’ ‘I’ve been involved in many tight races in the past and understand last minute ‘needs’ come up,’ he continued. ‘We are here to help anyway we can … financially or whatever.’” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Collins Super PAC Accepted Donations From Navatek In Exchange For Contracts

Scott Reed, Head Of A Collins Aligned Super PAC, Requested A $500,000 Donation From Navatek Whose Company, Collins Helped Land A Multimillion-Dollar Navy Research Contract In Maine A Year Earlier. According to ProPublica, “Scott Reed, head of the Collins super PAC, was on a mission to close that gap. Reed was meeting that day with three executives from a Hawaiian defense contractor, Navatek. A year earlier, Collins had helped their company land a multimillion-dollar Navy research contract in Maine. Now, seated at a coffee shop not far from the U.S. Capitol, Reed asked them for a $500,000 donation.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Navatek Proposed That They Would Provide A Large Donation If Collins Guaranteed Tens Of Millions In Additional Federal Funding. According to ProPublica, “But the company did have such a proposal: Navatek was hungry for more government contracts in Maine. If they cut a big check, the CEO told Reed, Navatek wanted Collins to guarantee tens of millions of dollars in additional federal funding.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

To Skirt Campaign Finance Laws That Barred Defense Contractors From Making Political Contributions, Navatek Planned To Funnel The Donation Through A Shell Company. According to ProPublica, “To skirt campaign finance laws and conceal the source of the funds, Navatek planned to funnel the donation through a shell company. The CEO wanted assurance that Collins would know where the money came from. Reed confirmed that she would, the executive said — and that Navatek would get its government contracts.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

  • The Shell Company Was “The Society Of Young Women Scientist And Engineers.” According to ProPublica, “In early 2020, the Campaign Legal Center, a nonprofit good government group, noticed something strange in the public filings for the Collins super PAC. The PAC had received a $150,000 donation from a newly created LLC with a typo in its name: the Society of Young Women Scientist and Engineers, with no S at the end of ‘Scientist.’ This was the $150,000 Kao donated after the Corner Bakery meeting. The money had come from Navatek’s account, not Kao’s, violating a ban on government contractors making donations.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]
  • Kao’s Wife’s Name Was On The Paperwork For The Society. According to ProPublica, “The center suspected the society was not a real group but a pass-through to hide the identity of a major political donor. It filed a complaint with the Federal Election Commission. It took only a few days for a Hawaii journalist to discover Kao’s wife’s name on the society’s paperwork, linking the shell company to Navatek.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Kao, Wanted Assurance That Collins Would Know Where The Money Came From And Reed Claimed That She Would. According to ProPublica, “To skirt campaign finance laws and conceal the source of the funds, Navatek planned to funnel the donation through a shell company. The CEO wanted assurance that Collins would know where the money came from. Reed confirmed that she would, the executive said — and that Navatek would get its government contracts.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Kao Sent An Initial $150,000 To Collins Super PAC Using A Shell Company And Than He Later Told Executives That Collins Committed To Getting The Company $32 Million In Naval Contracts. According to ProPublica, “After the Corner Bakery meeting, Navatek’s CEO, Martin Kao, sent an initial $150,000 to the Collins super PAC using the shell company. Two months later, he told Navatek executives that Collins committed to getting the company $32 million in naval contracts, according to an internal company email reviewed by ProPublica.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

  • Reed Knew Kao Was Behind The $150,000 Anonymous Donation And Replied In An Email “Very Smart.” According to ProPublica, “Reed knew Kao was behind the $150,000 anonymous donation, emails showed, because Kao told Reed he planned to donate through a shell company. ‘Very smart,’ Reed replied in an email viewed by ProPublica.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Seven Days After The Super PAC Cashed The Check From Kao’s Shell Company, One Of Reed’s Subordinates Emailed A Navatek Lobbyist Asking For Kao’s Number Because “Senator Collins Would Like To Call Martin To Thank Him.” According to ProPublica, “Seven days after the super PAC cashed the check from Kao’s shell company, one of Reed’s subordinates emailed a Navatek lobbyist asking for Kao’s phone number: ‘Senator Collins would like to call Martin to thank him.’” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

A Few Weeks After The $150,000 To Collins Super PAC, Kao And His Team Met With Collins’ Office And Secured A New Round Of Funding. According to ProPublica, “A few weeks after the $150,000 check to the Collins super PAC cleared, in February 2020, Kao and his team met with Collins’ office and secured a new round of funding. ‘We were very warmly received,’ Kao reported to his colleagues in an email obtained by the FBI. ‘Excellent meeting. Total of $32M will be supported.’ Records show the Senate allocated at least $10 million that year based on Navatek’s proposals.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

After Being Caught, Kao Attempted To Cover Up The Donation 

The Super PAC’s Lawyer Suggested That The “Society” Which Was the Shell Company, Make A Charitable Donation In Maine To Appear Like A Legitimate Nonprofit. According to ProPublica, “Inside Navatek, Kao shifted into damage control mode. He spoke to Reed and the super PAC’s lawyer, Cleta Mitchell, and began to hatch a cover-up. In an email released in civil litigation, Mitchell suggested the society make charitable donations — preferably in Maine — which would make it seem like a legitimate nonprofit. ‘I want to be sure that the LLC proceeds with the ideas we discussed — giving scholarships and recognition to women in engineering, etc.,’ wrote Mitchell. ‘That would help both of us, I think.’ Mitchell added, ‘We should develop a plan and timetable, so there are some scholarships given over the next several months, and particularly, perhaps in Maine, where the bad press was.’” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

  • The Donations Went To Women In STEM To State Universities Where They Were Angling To Win Government Contracts. According to ProPublica, “Kao and his team settled on donating scholarships to women in STEM. They offered between $5,000 and $25,000 apiece to state universities where they were angling to win government contracts — that way, the cover-up would benefit them politically, too.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Kao Was Indicted For The Illegal Campaign Contributions But Nobody Working For Collins Was Charged. According to ProPublica, “Three years later, Kao holed up in a conference room to recount the Corner Bakery meeting to a group of four FBI agents and federal prosecutors. The FBI had seen through his shell company ruse, and in 2022 a grand jury indicted him for making illegal campaign contributions. No one working for Collins was charged.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Despite The Role Scott Reed And Amy Abbott Played, They Still Remined In Collins Inner Circle 

Despite The Role Scott Reed And Amy Abbott Played, They Still Remined In Collins Inner Circle. According to ProPublica, “Both Scott Reed and Amy Abbott remain in Collins’ inner circle. Abbott is the finance director for her 2026 reelection effort, and Reed again chairs the main Collins super PAC. Abbott, who is married to Collins’ campaign manager, referred questions to the senator’s communications staff. Clark told ProPublica that Abbott and other campaign staff were interviewed by the FBI and that the campaign was never a target of the investigation.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

After Kao’s Arrest, He Confessed That His Bribery Web Was Much LArger than Originally Thought 

Kao Confessed To The U.S. Authorities That There Was A Web Of Corruption Surrounding Novatek And Members Of Congress 

Kao Provided The Names Of Lobbyists, Congressional Staffers And Members Of Congress Who Helped Him Trade Cash For Contracts. According to ProPublica, “Over three days at the U.S. attorney’s office in Honolulu, Kao laid out in devastating detail how his operation worked. He gave agents a 50-page document naming dozens of lobbyists, congressional staffers and members of Congress who he said helped him trade cash for contracts. Kao and his close associates had donated nearly $900,000 to dozens of politicians, allowing Navatek to establish operations in half a dozen states with over $40 million a year in government funding.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

Kao Claimed That Navatek’s Work For The Government Was Of No Real Value And The Research Never Produced anything The Military Wanted To Buy. According to ProPublica, “Most damningly, Kao told FBI agents and prosecutors, the company’s work for the government was of no real value. Navatek’s research under his stewardship never resulted in products the military wanted to buy, ProPublica found.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

  • One Such Project Was Modifying Small Boats To Reduce The Slamming Impact Felt Oat High Speeds But Navatek Knew That The Navy Didn’t Want The Designs. According to ProPublica, “In Maine, Navatek was studying ways to modify small boats to reduce the “slamming” impact felt by passengers at high speeds. With the help of the University of Maine’s giant 3D printer, Navatek made a prototype and unveiled it at a press conference where a Guinness World Records representative declared it the world’s largest 3D-printed boat. But Navatek executives knew the Navy had no plans to use the new design, former employees said. ‘[The work] got rolled into a few PowerPoint slides and a white paper, and that was the deliverable,’ recalled one who worked on the project. ‘The boats weren’t delivered to the Navy — the Navy didn’t even want them.’” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]
  • The Navy Awarded The Funding To Navatek Despite The Research Because “There’s Only So Many Battles You Can Fight” And They Felt Pressure From Members Of Congress. According to ProPublica, “Once the budget passed, lawmakers’ staff leaned on Navy officials to award Navatek the money. Former contracting officers told ProPublica they felt pressure to go along because money from those contracts funded their office — and because members of Congress had confronted dissenting naval officials in the past. ‘There’s only so many battles you can fight,’ one said. So Congress sometimes got its way even when Navatek’s projects made little sense.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

The FBI Asked Extensive Questions About His Dealings With Collins And Her Office. According to ProPublica, “Even so, by the end of 2024, the agents had enough evidence to pursue a sweeping bribery probe that could ensnare top lawmakers of both political parties. They asked their supervisors to approve a new investigation and contemplated using undercover operatives to gather more evidence. Although their effort was in its early stages, and it was unclear where it would lead, FBI agents asked Kao extensive questions about his dealings with Collins and her office.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

A Former Navatek Executive Claimed That They Targeted Small State Senators Like Collins. According to ProPublica, “‘It was a simple enough play. Let’s find the small states that have complementary universities … [and] let’s get access to their senators,’ Eric Schiff, a former Navatek executive, told ProPublica. ‘I’ve met Susan Collins. You can get access to Susan Collins. Once we got the first things working with Maine, then we said, ‘Well, let’s keep reaching.’ And so we did.’” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

The Corruption Probe Ended After Trump’s Re-Election 

After Trump Came In, He Eviscerated And Pushed Out Many Of The Agents Working On The Case Effectively Ending The Probe. According to ProPublica, “The specialized FBI and DOJ teams handling public corruption investigations, some of which were involved in Trump-related cases, were eviscerated. One of the agents who had taken Kao’s confession was pushed out as retribution for her role in investigating Trump’s attempt to overturn the 2020 election. Dozens of agents and prosecutors quit amid the department’s destruction, including the career attorney assigned to Kao’s case. Trump’s Justice Department no longer takes on public corruption in any meaningful fashion, former officials said. The investigation sparked by Kao’s revelations is dead. And the government is no longer talking to an informant who had offered a road map to corruption in Congress.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]

  • Kash Patel Purged The FBI’s Elite Anti-Corruption Unit Before Agents Could Continue Working On The Corruption Case. According to ProPublica, “By late 2024, Ball and Gounaud, the FBI agents, had come to believe there was enough evidence to warrant a broader investigation into bribery of members of Congress, according to a memo seen by ProPublica. Before they could embark on their new mission, however, they became casualties of Trump’s retribution campaign. Ball and Gounaud worked for the FBI’s elite anti-corruption unit known as CR-15, which specialized in investigating misconduct by elected officials. When Trump retook power, his new FBI director, Kash Patel, purged the unit agent by agent.” [ProPublica, 9/22/26]
