Begich Voted For Devastating Health Care Cuts 

  • Begich enthusiastically voted for a bill that would kick 17 million Americans off their health insurance, including nearly 12 million Americans off of Medicaid. More than 37,000 Alaskans could lose their health coverage as a result of his votes. 
  • Begich was previously president of the board of directors at an organization that recommended reconsidering Medicaid expansion, which provided more than 60,000 Alaskans with healthcare. 
  • Since 2020, Begich has been a fellow at an organization that advocated for repealing the Affordable Care Act even though more than 28,000 Alaskans rely on the law for health insurance. Alaskans have the 2nd highest average monthly premiums across America and by voting against renewing enhanced ACA premium tax credits, one Alaskan small business owner said she felt that Begich was “punishing us” with high healthcare costs. 

Message: Begich voted to take health coverage from Alaskans to give billionaires tax breaks.

Begich Supported Massive Cuts To Medicare

  • Begich voted for Republicans’ reconciliation bill, which the Congressional Budget Office estimated would trigger nearly $500 billion in cuts to Medicare absent future congressional action. Begich defended cuts saying he had “no doubt” he would vote for the reconciliation bill and called it a “transformative victory.” 
  • Additionally, Begich worked at an organization that advocated for changing Medicare to use a premium support function that would likely result in beneficiaries paying more for healthcare than they do under the current system. In congress, he was a member of the Republican Study Committee, which has advocated for raising the Social Security retirement age and turning Medicare into a voucher system.

Message: Begich worked to make health care more expensive and less accessible for Alaska’s seniors. 

Begich Voted To Cut Food Assistance His Constituents Rely On To Afford Groceries

  • Begich voted for a bill that made the largest SNAP cut in history. More than 28,000 households in Alaska relied on SNAP to afford groceries.
  • Begich defended the cuts saying they were “protecting the integrity” of SNAP and “ensuring benefits go to those who truly need them.” Local food assistance organizations raised alarm bells and said cuts to SNAP would not eliminate need but will “make that need harder to meet, and push more families into crisis.”

Message: Begich voted to cut food assistance his constituents relied on to afford groceries, all to give tax cuts to billionaires.
