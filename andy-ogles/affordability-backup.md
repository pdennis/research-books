# Rep. Andy Ogles (TN-05): Can't Be Trusted on Affordability

Rep. Andy Ogles has positioned himself as a champion against rising prices, but his voting record tells a different story. He voted to preserve Trump's 25% tariffs on Canadian goods -- tariffs that are driving up costs across Nashville, from coffee shops reporting 20-45% price increases to auto repair shops warning of $100 hikes on routine brake jobs. Three-quarters of Tennessee business leaders report negative tariff impacts, with the state facing $8 billion in projected losses and 44% of businesses passing costs directly to consumers. Beyond tariffs, Ogles introduced a resolution to block the CFPB's $8 credit card late fee cap, voted to repeal overdraft fee protections, voted to expand SNAP work requirements that threaten food assistance for 246,000 Middle Tennesseans, and voted against the Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act. All of this comes as Nashville families face a 25% increase in grocery prices, 49% of renters are cost-burdened, and local food pantries report a 40% surge in hungry families.

### Ogles Voted To Keep Tariffs That Are Driving Up Costs Across Nashville

**Ogles Voted Against A Bipartisan Resolution To Terminate Trump's 25% Tariffs On Canadian Goods.** According to NBC News, the House passed H.J.Res. 72 on February 11, 2026, by a vote of 219-211 to terminate the national emergency and 25% tariffs on Canadian goods. Only six Republicans broke with the party to support the resolution: Thomas Massie (KY), Kevin Kiley (CA), Don Bacon (NE), Dan Newhouse (WA), Jeff Hurd (CO), and Brian Fitzpatrick (PA). Ogles was not among them. [NBC News, [2/11/26](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/house-votes-end-trump-tariffs-canada-rcna258661)]

**Ogles Called Judges Who Blocked Trump's Tariffs "Woke Activist Judges" And Proposed A Third Presidential Term To "Reset" The Courts.** According to Newsweek, Ogles appeared on Fox Business Network and stated, "I'd love for Trump to have a third term so we can reset these courts and actually have folks who follow the law and the Constitution versus their ideological positions." He characterized the Court of International Trade's unanimous ruling blocking Trump's global tariffs as "judicial overreach," stating, "Enough is enough." [Newsweek, [5/29/25](https://www.newsweek.com/andy-ogles-trump-third-term-reset-courts-tariffs-2078645)]

**Three-Quarters Of Tennessee Business Leaders Reported Negative Impacts From Tariffs, With 44% Passing Costs To Consumers.** According to the University of Tennessee's Boyd Center for Business and Economic Research, a survey conducted between July 31 and August 22, 2025 found 25% of employers said tariffs affected their business "a lot" and approximately 50% experienced minor effects. The survey found 44.2% of business owners are passing some tariff costs to customers. U.S. trade agreements ranked as a top-three business challenge for the first time. [UT Haslam College of Business, [9/24/25](https://haslam.utk.edu/news/tennessee-business-leaders-feel-tariff-impact-but-stay-optimistic-about-economy/)]

- **Tennessee Faced An Estimated $8 Billion In Tariff-Related Losses In 2025.** According to Here Nashville, a Trade Partnership Worldwide study found tariffs would cost Tennessee $8 billion in 2025, with $3.7 billion from Mexico tariffs, $2.6 billion from China tariffs, and $1.4 billion from Canada tariffs. Canada, Mexico, and China account for nearly one-third of Tennessee's $111 billion in total imports. [Here Nashville, [2025](https://www.herenashville.com/tennessee-tariffs-impact/)]

### Tariffs Hit Nashville's Small Businesses And Consumers Hardest

**Nashville Coffee Roaster Reported Costs Up 20-45% Due To Tariffs.** According to NewsChannel 5, Bongo Java owner Bob Bernstein stated, "The few coffees we've had to reorder are now 20% to 45% more expensive." Bernstein added, "When you add in extra dock fees because the ports can't get the coffee out, so they sit there longer, or they're being hit by tariffs [...]it's like we're getting hit every which way." Bongo Java imports from Ethiopia, Mexico, Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, Rwanda, and Honduras. [NewsChannel 5, [3/5/25](https://www.newschannel5.com/news/its-gonna-be-a-mess-how-tariffs-are-threatening-the-local-coffee-industry)]

- **Bernstein Warned Nashville Customers Would Face Higher Prices And Shortages.** According to NewsChannel 5, Bernstein stated, "We're gonna start running out of coffees, we're gonna have to charge more for other coffees [...]it's gonna be a mess." [NewsChannel 5, [3/5/25](https://www.newschannel5.com/news/its-gonna-be-a-mess-how-tariffs-are-threatening-the-local-coffee-industry)]

**Nashville Auto Mechanic Warned Tariffs Would Raise Routine Repair Costs By 20%.** According to WSMV, Chris Fowle of Precision Tune Auto Care stated, "Once the tariffs are in effect, they're going to have to readjust their pricing just to make it worthwhile. Generally, a $500 brake job may be a $600 brake job coming up soon." Fowle noted that 25% tariffs affect auto parts crossing the Mexican and Canadian borders, with at least one-quarter of vehicles serviced from overseas manufacturers. [WSMV, [3/5/25](https://www.wsmv.com/2025/03/05/nashville-car-mechanic-warns-increasing-prices-tariffs/)]

**Jack Daniel's Sales To Canada Dropped 62% As Retaliatory Tariffs Hit Tennessee Whiskey.** According to Supply Chain Dive, Brown-Forman reported Jack Daniel's sales to Canada dropped 62% during its latest fiscal quarter after Canadian provinces stripped shelves of U.S. liquor in retaliation for Trump's 25% tariffs on Canadian products. The CEO said Canadian retailers removing U.S. alcohol from shelves was "worse than a tariff because it's literally taking your sales away completely." [Supply Chain Dive, [3/13/25](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/jack-daniels-brown-forman-canada-tariffs-taken-off-shelves-stores-ontario-alcohol-bourbon-trump/741970/)]

### Ogles Fought To Keep Credit Card Late Fees High And Killed Overdraft Fee Protections

**Ogles Introduced A Resolution To Block The CFPB's $8 Credit Card Late Fee Cap.** According to Congress.gov, Ogles introduced H.J.Res. 121 on March 26, 2024, to nullify the CFPB's Credit Card Penalty Fees rule, which had lowered the safe harbor threshold for late fees to $8 from $30 and $41 for larger card issuers. [Congress.gov, [3/26/24](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-joint-resolution/121)]

**Ogles Voted To Repeal CFPB Protections Limiting Overdraft Fees.** According to the Office of the Clerk, Ogles voted yea on S.J. Res. 18 on April 9, 2025, which disapproved the CFPB's overdraft lending rule. The resolution passed 217-211. [Office of the Clerk, [4/9/25](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/202596)]

**Ogles Voted To Eliminate Consumer Protections For Digital Payment Apps.** According to Congress.gov, Ogles voted yea on S.J. Res. 28 on April 9, 2025, which repealed CFPB protections for users of digital consumer payment applications. The resolution passed 219-211 and became Public Law 119-11. [Congress.gov, [4/9/25](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/28/all-actions-without-amendments)]

### Ogles Voted To Cut Food Assistance As Nashville Hunger Surged

**Ogles Voted Twice For The "One Big Beautiful Bill," Which Expanded SNAP Work Requirements To Ages 18-64.** According to the Congressional Research Service, the enacted law expanded the SNAP time-limit population subject to work requirements "to ages 18-64 and adults whose youngest child dependent is age 14 or older." Ogles voted yea on initial House passage on May 22, 2025 (215-214) and again on the motion to concur in the Senate amendment on July 3, 2025. [Congressional Research Service, [8/15/25](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48552.html)]

- **The Law Also Shifted SNAP Costs To States And Ended Nutrition Education Funding.** According to the Congressional Research Service, the law increased "the state cost-share for SNAP administrative costs from 50% to 75%" and ended "mandatory funding for the Nutrition Education and Obesity Prevention Grant Program (SNAP-Ed)." [Congressional Research Service, [8/15/25](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48552.html)]

**Second Harvest Food Bank Called Federal Cuts "The Largest Rollback Of Hunger Relief In U.S. History."** According to WSMV, Second Harvest President and CEO Nancy Keil stated the cuts represented the "largest rollback of hunger relief in U.S. history." SNAP brought $1.9 billion in federal food dollars into Tennessee, supporting more than 6,600 local grocery stores and farmers' markets. Second Harvest projected it would lose 30% of USDA program food supplies. [WSMV, [7/9/25](https://www.wsmv.com/2025/07/09/second-harvest-food-bank-lose-significant-amount-food-supplies-due-federal-cuts/)]

- **About 11% Of Davidson County Households Used SNAP Benefits.** According to WSMV, about 11% of Davidson County households used food stamps, compared to 6% in Wilson County and only 2% in Williamson County. [WSMV, [12/11/25](https://www.wsmv.com/2025/12/11/heres-which-tennessee-county-has-highest-percentage-households-using-snap-see-where-your-county-ranks/)]

### Ogles Voted Against Tax Relief For Working Families

**Ogles Voted Against The Tax Relief For American Families And Workers Act.** According to the Office of the Clerk, Ogles voted nay on the Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act on January 31, 2024. The bipartisan bill, which passed 357-70, would have expanded the child tax credit and provided tax relief for working families. [Office of the Clerk, [1/31/24](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/202430)]

**Ogles Voted For A Tax Bill Where The Top 0.1% Received An Average $314,000 Tax Cut.** According to AP News, a Treasury Department analysis found the top 0.1% of earners would receive a tax cut of $314,000 under the full extension of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, which Ogles supported as part of H.R. 1. [AP News, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://apnews.com/article/tax-cuts-jobs-act-trump-treasury-agenda-f4031196e0d69d0a1630e3b06b6d3cd7)]

### Ogles Blamed Everyone But Himself For Rising Costs

**Ogles Cited Rising Grocery And Housing Costs On The House Floor While Supporting Policies That Raised Them.** According to C-SPAN, Ogles stated on July 24, 2024, "The cost of groceries are up 30%, 40%. Rents are up. It's impossible for some to buy a home. We've got to recognize that the Biden-Harris administration is a failure." He blamed the Inflation Reduction Act for raising energy costs, yet has supported tariff policies projected to cost Tennessee $8 billion and drive further price increases. [C-SPAN, [7/24/24](https://www.c-span.org/video/?537298-1&start=50708)]

### Impact On Everyday People

**Nashville Food Pantries Reported A 40% Surge In Hungry Families.** According to WSMV, Nashville experienced a 40% increase in families experiencing hunger in 2025, reaching a 10-year high. The Store, a Nashville food pantry, served 1,000 families monthly but had 2,400 families on its waitlist. CEO Collen Mayer stated, "Hunger is just a growing problem that we've got to address as a community." [WSMV, [10/9/25](https://www.wsmv.com/2025/10/09/nashville-food-pantry-struggling-handle-40-surge-hungry-families/)]

**Nashville Grocery Prices Increased 25% While Nearly Half Of Renters Were Cost-Burdened.** According to the Nashville Tennessean, Bank of America research found grocery prices in Nashville increased by 25%. Separately, a Metro Nashville Planning Department study found nearly half (49%) of all Nashville renters were cost-burdened, with an alarming 74% of single-parent household renters cost-burdened. [Nashville Tennessean, [1/15/25](https://www.yahoo.com/news/nashville-grocery-stores-increased-prices-183104652.html); Metro Nashville Planning Department, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/UHS-Executive-Summary.pdf?ct=1752864877)]

**Nashville's Homeownership Affordability Gap Exceeded $90,000 In Davidson County.** According to the Metro Nashville Planning Department, there was "more than a $90,000 gap between the average home value ($421,722) and the price affordable" for typical residents ($329,962). Rent growth outpaced incomes over the past five years, creating an almost $900 monthly gap. [Metro Nashville Planning Department, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/UHS-Full-Report.pdf?ct=1759160211)]

- **Black And Hispanic Families Could Afford Homes In Less Than 1% Of Nashville Neighborhoods.** According to Axios Nashville, "typical Black and Hispanic families can afford median-valued homes in less than 1% of the city's 481 neighborhoods -- specifically, just 0.8% for Black families." [Axios, [3/28/25](https://www.axios.com/local/nashville/2025/03/28/nashville-housing-study-black-hispanic-homeownership-gap)]
