# Rep. Andy Ogles (TN-05): Can't Be Trusted on Jobs and the Economy

Rep. Andy Ogles claims to be fighting for Nashville's economy, but his record tells a different story. He voted to preserve tariffs that are projected to cost Tennessee $8 billion and have already slowed the state's economic growth to 1.7% -- with Tennessee adding just 24,000 jobs in 2025, less than half its typical 60,000 annual average. Three-quarters of Tennessee business leaders report negative tariff impacts, with 44% passing costs directly to consumers, and Nashville-area businesses are bearing the brunt: coffee roasters report 20-45% cost increases, auto mechanics warn of $100 hikes on routine brake jobs, and Jack Daniel's saw sales to Canada plummet 62% from retaliatory tariffs. Meanwhile, Ogles voted twice for the "One Big Beautiful Bill," which the Commonwealth Fund projects will eliminate 1.22 million jobs nationally through Medicaid and SNAP cuts -- including a projected $500 million hit to Davidson County's economy alone. He voted against the bipartisan Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act, introduced a resolution to block the CFPB's $8 credit card late fee cap, and voted to repeal overdraft fee protections -- all while his constituents face a 40% surge in food insecurity, 25% grocery price increases, and a $90,000 homeownership affordability gap. Ogles calls himself an economist, but he earned a C in his only economics course and fabricated his college major.

### Ogles Voted To Keep Tariffs That Slowed Tennessee's Economy And Halved Job Growth

**Ogles Voted Against A Bipartisan Resolution To Terminate Trump's 25% Tariffs On Canadian Goods.** According to NBC News, the House passed H.J.Res. 72 on February 11, 2026, by a vote of 219-211 to terminate the national emergency and 25% tariffs on Canadian goods. Only six Republicans broke with the party to support the resolution. Ogles was not among them. [NBC News, [2/11/26](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/house-votes-end-trump-tariffs-canada-rcna258661)]

- **Tennessee's Economy Grew At Just 1.7% In 2025, Down From 3.2% In 2023, With Tariffs Cited As The Primary Cause.** According to the Nashville Post, the Boyd Center for Business and Economic Research's 2026 Economic Report to the Governor found Tennessee's real GDP growth fell to 1.7% in 2025, down from 3.2% in 2023 and 2.7% in 2024. Research associate professor Larry Kessler characterized the outlook as "precariously positive," noting that "federal trade policy -- primarily surrounding tariffs -- had an outsized impact on overall economic growth through the first half of 2025." [Nashville Post, [1/26](https://www.nashvillepost.com/business/report-state-s-economy-grew-at-slower-pace-in-2025/article_0f6f05ab-686f-4e43-814d-dd61ea29f08a.html)]

- **Tennessee Added Just 24,000 Jobs In 2025, Less Than Half Its Typical 60,000 Annual Average.** According to the Nashville Post, the Boyd Center report found Tennessee was expected to add just 24,000 new jobs in 2025, well below the state's typical annual average of 60,000 new jobs. Manufacturing saw slight employment declines. [Nashville Post, [1/26](https://www.nashvillepost.com/business/report-state-s-economy-grew-at-slower-pace-in-2025/article_0f6f05ab-686f-4e43-814d-dd61ea29f08a.html)]

**Three-Quarters Of Tennessee Business Leaders Reported Negative Impacts From Tariffs, With 44% Passing Costs To Consumers.** According to the University of Tennessee's Boyd Center for Business and Economic Research, a survey conducted between July 31 and August 22, 2025 found 25% of employers said tariffs affected their business "a lot" and approximately 50% experienced minor effects. The survey found 44.2% of business owners are passing some tariff costs to customers, and U.S. trade agreements ranked as a top-three business challenge for the first time. [UT Haslam College of Business, [9/24/25](https://haslam.utk.edu/news/tennessee-business-leaders-feel-tariff-impact-but-stay-optimistic-about-economy/)]

- **Tennessee Faced An Estimated $8 Billion In Tariff-Related Losses In 2025.** According to Here Nashville, a Trade Partnership Worldwide study found tariffs would cost Tennessee $8 billion in 2025, with $3.7 billion from Mexico tariffs, $2.6 billion from China tariffs, and $1.4 billion from Canada tariffs. [Here Nashville, [2025](https://www.herenashville.com/tennessee-tariffs-impact/)]

**Ogles Called Judges Who Blocked Tariffs "Woke Activist Judges" And Proposed A Third Presidential Term To "Reset" The Courts.** According to Newsweek, Ogles appeared on Fox Business Network and stated, "I'd love for Trump to have a third term so we can reset these courts and actually have folks who follow the law and the Constitution versus their ideological positions." He characterized the Court of International Trade's unanimous ruling blocking Trump's global tariffs as judicial overreach. [Newsweek, [5/29/25](https://www.newsweek.com/andy-ogles-trump-third-term-reset-courts-tariffs-2078645)]

### Tariffs Hit Nashville's Small Businesses And Signature Industries

**Nashville Coffee Roaster Reported Costs Up 20-45% Due To Tariffs.** According to NewsChannel 5, Bongo Java owner Bob Bernstein stated, "The few coffees we've had to reorder are now 20% to 45% more expensive." Bernstein added, "When you add in extra dock fees because the ports can't get the coffee out, so they sit there longer, or they're being hit by tariffs [...]it's like we're getting hit every which way." [NewsChannel 5, [3/5/25](https://www.newschannel5.com/news/its-gonna-be-a-mess-how-tariffs-are-threatening-the-local-coffee-industry)]

**Nashville Auto Mechanic Warned Tariffs Would Raise Routine Repair Costs By 20%.** According to WSMV, Chris Fowle of Precision Tune Auto Care stated, "Once the tariffs are in effect, they're going to have to readjust their pricing just to make it worthwhile. Generally, a $500 brake job may be a $600 brake job coming up soon." Fowle noted that 25% tariffs affect auto parts crossing the Mexican and Canadian borders. [WSMV, [3/5/25](https://www.wsmv.com/2025/03/05/nashville-car-mechanic-warns-increasing-prices-tariffs/)]

**Jack Daniel's Sales To Canada Dropped 62% As Retaliatory Tariffs Hit Tennessee Whiskey.** According to Supply Chain Dive, Brown-Forman reported Jack Daniel's sales to Canada dropped 62% during its latest fiscal quarter after Canadian provinces stripped shelves of U.S. liquor in retaliation for Trump's 25% tariffs on Canadian products. The CEO said Canadian retailers removing U.S. alcohol from shelves was "worse than a tariff because it's literally taking your sales away completely." [Supply Chain Dive, [3/13/25](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/jack-daniels-brown-forman-canada-tariffs-taken-off-shelves-stores-ontario-alcohol-bourbon-trump/741970/)]

**Tennessee Layoffs More Than Doubled In Early 2025 Amid Tariff Uncertainty.** According to Here Nashville, Tennessee saw over 2,000 workers laid off from companies including FedEx, Dollar General, and Nissan in early 2025, compared to 822 layoffs during the same period in 2024. Contributing factors included "uncertain political climate, a cooling labor market, and lingering impacts of inflation." Bridgestone closed its La Vergne plant affecting 700 workers, and Adient shut down two Middle Tennessee auto parts facilities in Columbia and Lexington, cutting over 400 jobs. [Here Nashville, [2025](https://www.herenashville.com/tennessee-layoffs-2025/)]

### Ogles Voted For A Bill Projected To Eliminate Over A Million Jobs

**Ogles Voted Twice For The "One Big Beautiful Bill," Which The Commonwealth Fund Projected Would Eliminate 1.22 Million Jobs By 2029.** According to the Commonwealth Fund, the Medicaid and SNAP cutbacks in the bill would trigger 1.22 million job losses and shrink state economies by $154 billion by 2029. Nearly 500,000 of those jobs would be lost in health care, with the rest spread across food-related and other sectors. Ogles voted yea on initial House passage on May 22, 2025 (215-214) and again on the motion to concur in the Senate amendment on July 3, 2025. [Commonwealth Fund, [6/25](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/jun/how-medicaid-snap-cutbacks-one-big-beautiful-bill-trigger-job-losses-states)]

- **Davidson County Was Projected To Lose $500 Million From The Bill's Cuts.** According to the Tennessee Lookout, Tennessee's most populous counties would lose the most from the bill's Medicaid and SNAP cuts, with Shelby County expected to see a net $1.2 billion decrease, followed by Davidson County at $500 million, Knox County at $301 million, Hamilton County at $283 million, and Rutherford County at $167 million. [Tennessee Lookout, [7/11/25](https://tennesseelookout.com/2025/07/11/three-charts-showing-the-impact-of-the-big-beautiful-bill-on-tennessee/)]

- **Tennessee SNAP Cuts Shifted $110 Million In Annual Costs To State Taxpayers.** According to the Tennessee Lookout, the Tennessee Commission on Children and Youth estimated that preserving SNAP benefits for children alone would require $110 million in additional state revenue. Over 700,000 Tennesseans -- one in every 10 residents -- rely on SNAP, and the average family receives approximately $340 monthly. Signe Anderson of the Tennessee Justice Center stated, "These are the deepest cuts to SNAP we have ever seen [...]It will make it harder for Tennesseans to make ends meet, and that hurts our communities and our economy." [Tennessee Lookout, [9/22/25](https://tennesseelookout.com/2025/09/22/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-food-assistance-cuts-come-with-hefty-price-tag-for-tennessee-taxpayers/)]

The bipartisan bill, which passed 357-70, would have expanded the child tax credit and provided tax relief for working families. [Office of the Clerk, [1/31/24](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/202430)]

**Ogles Voted For A Tax Bill Where The Top 0.1% Received An Average $314,000 Tax Cut.** According to AP News, a Treasury Department analysis found the top 0.1% of earners would receive a tax cut of $314,000 under the full extension of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, which Ogles supported as part of H.R. 1. [AP News, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://apnews.com/article/tax-cuts-jobs-act-trump-treasury-agenda-f4031196e0d69d0a1630e3b06b6d3cd7)]

**Ogles Introduced A Resolution To Block The CFPB's $8 Credit Card Late Fee Cap.** According to Congress.gov, Ogles introduced H.J.Res. 121 on March 26, 2024, to nullify the CFPB's Credit Card Penalty Fees rule, which had lowered the safe harbor threshold for late fees to $8 from $30 and $41 for larger card issuers. [Congress.gov, [3/26/24](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-joint-resolution/121)]

**Ogles Voted To Repeal CFPB Protections Limiting Overdraft Fees And Digital Payment App Protections.** According to the Office of the Clerk, Ogles voted yea on S.J. Res. 18, repealing the CFPB's overdraft lending rule (passed 217-211), and on S.J. Res. 28, eliminating consumer protections for digital payment apps (passed 219-211, became Public Law 119-11). [Office of the Clerk, [4/9/25](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/202596)]

[Nashville Tennessean, [1/15/25](https://www.yahoo.com/news/nashville-grocery-stores-increased-prices-183104652.html); Metro Nashville Planning Department, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/UHS-Executive-Summary.pdf?ct=1752864877)]
