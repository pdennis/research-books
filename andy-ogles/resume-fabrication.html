Rep. Andy Ogles: Fabricated His Resume And Professional Credentials

Ogles Fabricated His Resume And Professional Credentials

Ogles Claimed To Be An Economist But Earned A C In His Only Economics Course. According to Newsweek, Ogles's college transcripts from Middle Tennessee State University confirmed he received a Bachelor of Science in liberal studies — not economics or international relations as he had claimed. He took only one economics course, Principles of Economics at Columbia State Community College, and earned a C. His congressional biography stated he "studied policy and economics." [Newsweek, 2/27/23]

  • Ogles Also Failed Every Political Science Course He Took At MTSU. According to Newsweek, Ogles received failing grades in Presidency, Problems in Government, Political Theory, and National Security Policy across both 1995 and 1998 semesters, and graduated in 2007 with a 2.4 GPA. [Newsweek, 2/27/23]

Ogles Claimed Graduate Work At Vanderbilt And Dartmouth That Could Not Be Verified. According to NewsChannel 5, Ogles claimed to have done graduate work at Vanderbilt's Owen School of Management and Dartmouth, but the investigation found no evidence of completed graduate work at either institution. [NewsChannel 5, 2/23/23]

Ogles Inflated His Anti-Trafficking Credentials From A $4,000 Part-Time Job To "Overseeing Operations In 12 Countries." According to NewsChannel 5, Ogles's congressional website originally claimed he was "overseeing operations and investments in 12 countries" for Abolition International, but the organization's tax records showed Ogles worked in a part-time position that paid him $4,000 total. The website was later quietly amended to say "several countries." [NewsChannel 5, 2/23/23]

  • A Former Tennessee Republican Party Official Said The Lies Raised Broader Credibility Questions. According to The New Republic, former Tennessee Republican Party political director Randy Stamps stated, "If he is willing to run around and say, 'Hey, I'm an economist,' who knows what else he is going to tell you." [New Republic, 2/27/23]
