According to CNN, "As billionaire Republican Rick Jackson blanketed Georgia’s airwaves this spring with ads vowing a crackdown on undocumented immigrants if elected governor, a company in his healthcare empire was in the throes of a costly legal battle over the treatment of its immigrant workforce. A lawsuit filed by foreign-born nurses accused Avant Healthcare Professionals of luring them to the United States with the promise of the American Dream, only to pay them substandard wages and trap them in rigid contracts for years. According to their lawsuit, when they tried to quit, Avant threatened to contact Immigration and Customs Enforcement and sought to charge them five-figure fees for leaving. ‘Avant’s ‘employment’ is essentially indentured servitude,’ the plaintiffs wrote in their complaint. Avant denied wrongdoing. But on June 22 — days after Jackson became the GOP nominee for governor — the company agreed to a settlement that would give more than 5,000 current and former immigrant workers a share of a $3 million payout and waive unpaid fees." [CNN, 8/20/26]

The Foreign Nurses Sued Jackson’s Avant Healthcare For Violating The Trafficking Victims Protection Act And Fair Labor Standards Act And Argued Working For Avant Was “Essentially Indentured Servitude”

2023: Foreign Nurses Latoya Lewis And Lucinda Byron Sued Avant For Violating The Trafficking Victims Protection Act And Fair Labor Standards Act After Avant Allegedly Threatened To Report Them To USCIS And Misled Them To Believe Quitting Would Affect Their Immigration Status. According to CNN, "Nurses Latoya Lewis and Lucinda Byron saw Avant as a ticket to new lives in the United States. Lewis arrived in 2021 from Jamaica; Byron, originally from St. Vincent and the Grenadines, began working at a South Dakota hospital in 2022. Both signed multi-year contracts with Avant. According to a lawsuit first filed in 2023, they soon found themselves in an inflexible arrangement where they could take little time off, were paid less than colleagues employed directly by the hospitals and had little opportunity for advancement. They struggled to get by on their wages, the complaint said. When they tried to quit, Avant warned them they would face fees to recoup the costs of bringing them to the US, the complaint said. Staff allegedly threatened to contact Immigration and Customs Enforcement and misled them into believing that leaving could jeopardize their immigration status. Like many foreign nurses, they arrived on EB3 visas, which come with a green card that generally doesn’t require them to stay with the company that helped bring them to the US. Avant wanted $41,832 from Byron when she left and had 30 days to pay, the complaint said. Lawyers for Lewis and Byron argued these contracts and Avant’s efforts to enforce them violated the Trafficking Victims Protection Act and the Fair Labor Standards Act. Lewis and Byron were not available to speak to CNN, their lawyers said." [CNN, 8/20/26]

2023: The Foreign Nurses Contended Working For Avant Was “Essentially Indentured Servitude,” Arguing The Case Was About Labor Trafficking And Its Impact On The Workforce, Including The Failure To Pay Minimum Wages, Suppression Of Wages, And Unfair Competition. According to the Daily Caller, "In 2023, two nurses — Latoya Lewis of Jamaica and Lucinda Byron of St. Thomas and the Grenadines — filed a lawsuit against Avant Healthcare Professionals in connection with EB-3 visas. The complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Florida, read, ‘This case is about labor trafficking and its impact on an essential workforce, including failure to pay minimum wages, suppression of wages, and unfair competition.’ [...] ‘Avant led Plaintiffs and its other foreign-recruited healthcare workers to believe that they would come to the United States to practice nursing in a safe environment with a good employer who would treat them fairly,’ the lawsuit alleged. ‘But working for Avant was nothing like that. Rather, Avant’s ‘employment’ is essentially indentured servitude.’" [Daily Caller, 3/17/26]
