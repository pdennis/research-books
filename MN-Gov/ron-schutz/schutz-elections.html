Overview

In 2025, Schutz shared claims of election fraud in Minnesota. During an investigation into elections in Carver County, Minnesota Secretary of State Steve Simon testified that two Nevada residents attempted to submit fraudulent voter registrations, but asserted there was no evidence of fraudulent votes. Still, Schutz criticized Democrats’ “lack of accountability,” which he claimed led to voter fraud in Carver County.

Additionally Schutz pledged to make “secure elections” a main priority of his campaign, and said he would prosecute noncitizens that attempt to vote in Minnesota elections.

Schutz Lamented “Voter Fraud In Carver County” Despite The Secretary Of State Asserting The Case Was Not About “Fraudulent Votes”

October 2025: Schutz Said Democrats’ “Lack Of Accountability” Caused “Voter Fraud In Carver County.” According to Schutz’s Facebook, “Democrats have a habit of doubling down when the system fails and their leadership is called into question. The lack of accountability has resulted in billions lost in fraud and now voter fraud in Carver County. We must tackle issues head-on and fix the system so it works better for everyone.”

[Facebook, Ron Schutz For MN Attorney General, 10/21/25]

  • Minnesota Republicans Suggested Proof Of Citizenship For Voter Eligibility Despite The Secretary Of State Asserting The Case Was “About Individuals Who Conspired To Submit False Voter Registration Applications” And Not “Fraudulent Votes” According to KTTC, “Minnesota’s Secretary of State, Steve Simon, went before the Minnesota House Fraud Committee on Tuesday morning, testifying about a case of voter registration fraud that was charged this past June. Several months ago, the U.S. Attorney announced charges against two Nevada residents who’d allegedly committed voter registration fraud during the 2022 election cycle. The pair is said to have submitted several fraudulent voter registrations, using the fraudulent submissions to collect money from a foundation that encourages voters to register. ‘This is a case about individuals who conspired to submit false voter registration applications,’ said Simon. ‘This is not a case about fraudulent votes.’ [...] ‘Would you support having proof of citizenship as part of the way we verify eligibility in Minnesota?’ asked Rep. Kristin Robbins (R-Maple Grove), Chair of the House Fraud Prevention Committee. Simon responded with an anecdote about Kansas, which enacted a similar law. ‘30,000 eligible Kansas voters—as eligible as you are or I am—were turned away because they couldn’t produce the proper proofs. I don’t think we want to be that in Minnesota,’ he said.” [KTTC, 10/15/25]
