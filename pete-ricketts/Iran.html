The Iran War Spiked Fuel And Fertilizer Costs For Farmers

Nebraska Farmers Were Paying A Dollar More Per Gallon Of Diesel Year-Over-Year

Nebraska Farmers Were Paying A Dollar More Per Gallon Of Diesel Year-Over-Year After The Start Of The Iran War. According to 1011 Now, “The price of gas is up more than 30 cents from just a couple of weeks ago, and those prices are magnified for Nebraska farmers, who are paying, on average, $1 more for diesel than this time last year. On top of that, fertilizer prices have gone up more than 30% since the end of February, when the war in Iran began. The Middle East accounts for nearly half of all urea exports — the most widely used solid nitrogen fertilizer worldwide. And around half of that nitrogen is applied to corn in the spring. It’s got farmers concerned not just about their bottom line, but about the food supply chain.” [1011 Now, 3/10/26]

At The Start Of The War, Nebraska Fertilizer Prices Spiked 30 Percent

At The Start Of The War, Nebraska Fertilizer Prices Spiked 30 Percent, With One Farmer Noting Fertilizer Had Gone Up By $1,000 A Ton. According to 1011 Now, “On top of that, fertilizer prices have gone up more than 30% since the end of February, when the war in Iran began. The Middle East accounts for nearly half of all urea exports — the most widely used solid nitrogen fertilizer worldwide. And around half of that nitrogen is applied to corn in the spring. It’s got farmers concerned not just about their bottom line, but about the food supply chain. […] Henderson-area farmer Jason Lewis said the spiking cost of diesel and unknowns on fertilizer are crunching Nebraska farmers right as they’re preparing to plant. Lewis said fertilizer prices in his area jumped from around $800 a ton to $1,000, if not more.” [1011 Now, 3/10/26]
