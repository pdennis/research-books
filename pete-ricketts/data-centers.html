Overview

Pete Ricketts is all in on data centers. He personally recruited Facebook to build a data center in Nebraska while he was Governor. He even implemented tax incentives for data centers, costing the state over half a billion in revenue before the incentives were axed by his successor. Despite broad opposition in Nebraska to data centers raising energy prices and eating up farmland, Ricketts is gung ho on Nebraska becoming the “Silicon Prairie.”

Ricketts Signed Tax Incentives Into Law For Data Centers

2020: As Governor, Ricketts Signed Tax Incentives For Data Centers Into Nebraska Law. According to Lutz Accounting, “On August 17, 2020, Governor Pete Ricketts signed into law Legislative Bill 1107, which includes a new business tax incentive program called the ImagiNE Nebraska Act. […] The ImagiNE Nebraska Act is similar to the Nebraska Advantage Act but updated to encourage high-wage jobs, promote investment in distressed and rural areas, simplify administrative requirements, and allow for more transparency and accountability. […] Refunds and use of credits will be capped each year, with only $25 million available in each of 2021 and 2022, increasing to $100 million in 2023 and 2024. In addition, applications may not be approved if future refunds and credit utilization are expected to exceed the calendar year limits.” [Lutz Accounting, Viewed 9/4/26]

Nebraska Data Centers Received Property Tax Exemptions To The Tune Of $519 Million From The Law Ricketts Signed. According to 1011 Now, “Nebraska Gov. Governor Pillen Rescinded Ricketts’s Data Center Tax Incentives

Governor Pillen Rescinded Ricketts’ Data Center Tax Incentives. According to the Nebraska Examiner, “New data center projects coming to Nebraska will no longer benefit from tax incentives provided under the ImagiNE Act, according to a new executive order. Gov. Jim Pillen signed the executive order Monday alongside State Sens. Mike Jacobson, Rob Clements, Tony Sorrentino and Eliot Bostar, Department of Economic Development Director Maureen Larsen and Department of Water, Energy and Environment Director Jesse Bradley. Data Centers Hiked Nebraska Energy Prices

Nebraska Data Centers Created A Surge In Energy Demand

2024: Data Centers Accounted For 21 Percent Of Electricity Sold By the Omaha Public Power District

Data Centers Accounted For 21 Percent Of Electricity Sold By the Omaha Public Power District in 2024, Up From One Percent In 2018. According to Nebraska Public Media, “Increased demand from data centers shows up dramatically in sales figures from the Omaha Public Power District. Nebraskans, like many Americans, are dealing with rising demand and prices for electricity. In 2018, data centers used 1% of the electricity sold by the utility. In 2024, they used 21% And by 2035, data centers are projected to account for 41% of OPPD’s electricity sales.” [Nebraska Public Media, 12/26/25]

2026: Nebraska Ranked Second In The Nation For Percentage Of Electricity Spent On Data Centers

2026: Nebraska Ranked Second In The Nation For Percentage Of Electricity Spent On Data Centers. According to the Nebraska Examiner, “Pillen said Nebraska ranks second in the U.S. for the percentage of electricity spent on data centers, at 11.5%. He said this has prevented other businesses from expanding in Nebraska when they would have otherwise.” [Nebraska Examiner, 7/20/26]

Nebraska Utility Providers Raised Consumer Rates Thanks In Part To Data Center Demand

Nebraska Utility Providers Raised Consumer Rates Thanks In Part To Data Center Demand. According to The Wire, “OPPD’s board of directors on Thursday approved a new budget proposal to help keep pace with surging demand for electricity, rising costs and new regulations that will require even more generation. […] On average, residential customers will see a 6% increase, or about $7 on a $115 bill. Commercial customers will see an average increase of 3.7%, while industrial customers will see an average increase of 8.9%. Each customer class has several rates available to them, so customers may see more or less of these amounts depending on their specific customer class rate. […] OPPD is seeing unprecedented demand as Nebraska’s economy grows and relies more than ever on electricity. The utility has added 23,000 new meters over the past five years. That’s roughly the same number that serves the city of Grand Island. New manufacturing, biotech companies, data centers, and food and agricultural processing all play a role, as does demand from residential customers. The more the system grows, the larger the reserve margin required by SPP.” [Wire, 12/18/25]

Otoe County Temporarily Suspended Data Center Permits

Otoe County Temporarily Suspended Data Center Permits. According to Nebraska Public Media, “Standing before the Otoe County Board and a room of neighbors, Wynee Benedict ticked through a long list of concerns. Do we have enough water for them? Who pays for their power? What if they create a heat island? The source of Benedict’s worries: data centers. Since learning their county could become home to a new data center, Otoe County has been abuzz with questions and concerns like Benedict’s, leading some residents to call for a temporary ban on the industry.  That’s effectively what the board did Tuesday, voting to suspend the permits needed for a new data center for up to a year, according to commissioner Chuck Cole.” [Nebraska Public Media, 5/24/26]

Seward County Approved A Moratorium Against Data Centers

Seward County Approved A Moratorium Against Data Centers. According to Nebraska Public Media, “Seward County commissioners voted Tuesday morning to approve a moratorium against data center construction. The moratorium will last for one year, barring a vote to extend it, or until the county can draft and finalize regulations around data centers. Seward County is now one of at least 10 Nebraska counties with a moratorium in place, according to preliminary reporting by Nebraska Public Media News. Five more counties are considering data center moratoriums.” [Nebraska Public Media, 6/30/26]

Gage County Officials Voted To Implement An 18-Month Ban On Data Center Construction

Gage County Officials Voted To Implement An 18-Month Ban On Data Center Construction. According to Nebraska Public Media, “It was 10 o’clock Tuesday night, and the district courtroom in Beatrice was still filled with expectant Gage County residents. After four hours of hearings, their patience paid off. The Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously voted in favor of an 18-month moratorium on data centers, and the courtroom erupted into applause and cheers. The moratorium fends off conditional and special use building permits for data centers while Gage County officials consider regulations around their construction. The board’s decision will be sent to the County Board of Supervisors for approval.” [Nebraska Public Media, 6/17/26]

Data Centers Ate Up Nebraska Farmland

Data Center Projects Consumed Nebraska Farmland And Introduced Environmental Hazards

Google And Meta Were Spending Tens Of Millions To Acquire Hundreds Of Acres Of Nebraska Farmland

Google And Meta Were Spending Tens Of Millions To Acquire Hundreds Of Acres Of Farmland. According to the Flatwater Free Press, “In Douglas County, Google dropped another $21 million to accumulate more than 400 acres northwest of Omaha, 90% once farmland. And in Lincoln, Google owns some-500 acres, close to 500 of which used to be farmland, according to Lancaster County Assessor’s records. Google isn’t the only tech giant gobbling up land once used for crops. Meta, formerly known as Facebook, has poured more than $50 million into nearly 900 acres of agricultural land in Sarpy County since 2017, according to records compiled by the Flatwater Free Press from the Sarpy County Assessor’s Office.” [Flatwater Free Press, 12/14/23]

Nebraska Farmers Felt Pressured To Give Up Their Land

Farmers Felt Pressured To Give Up Their Land. According to the Flatwater Free Press, “Deborah Bowman’s family farmed 77 acres of the land now under Meta’s data center for more than a century. Bowman, in her 70s, grew up two miles south of there. She felt backed into a corner to give up the land, she said, noting her lawyer encouraged her to sell her property. ‘I did not want to sell it or have Facebook take it,’ she said. ‘That was absolutely the last thing I wanted done. … I grew up where that land (is).’ After two years of negotiation, she eventually swapped her land for a similarly-sized plot owned by her neighbor, who got paid by Raven Northbrook, she said. The neighbor sold three other parcels separately to Meta for $23 million, sales records show. ‘My heart was in their original land. I felt I had no other alternative. So I crossed the road and went to the other land,’ she said.” [Flatwater Free Press, 12/14/23]

Data Centers Harmed Local Air And Water Quality

Data Centers Harmed Local Air And Water Quality. According to the Sierra Club, “Beyond dirty power plants, Data centers intensify air pollution in the communities that house them. All data centers in Virginia come with large concentrations of shipping container sized diesel generators on-site. Why is this a problem? These machines leak a multitude of toxic chemicals like Benzene and Arsenic that cause chronic respiratory illnesses like asthma and life threatening conditions like cancer. There are virtually no restrictions on the locations of data centers. This poses an enormous health threat to communities like schools, neighborhoods, and population centers that reside within a few miles away. […] There are virtually no restrictions on how much water one data center can consume. Some of the largest data center campuses consume as much as 1-5 million gallons of water per day.” [Sierra Club, 8/13/26]
