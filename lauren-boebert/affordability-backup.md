# Rep. Lauren Boebert (CO-04) Can't Be Trusted on Affordability

Rep. Lauren Boebert has sided against Colorado families on nearly every major affordability issue. She voted to keep Trump's 25% tariffs on Canada — tariffs the Yale Budget Lab estimates cost the average American household up to $2,400 per year — while Colorado's own agricultural sector faces a projected $39 million revenue drop in beef alone and potato farmers in the San Luis Valley lose an estimated $150,000 per crop circle. She voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill, which cuts SNAP benefits for an estimated 131,000 low-income Coloradans and slashes Medicaid for up to 230,000 more. She voted against the Inflation Reduction Act's $35 insulin cap and Medicare drug price negotiation, calling it "a complete scam" — even as it funded an $200 million expansion creating 850 jobs in Pueblo. Eighty-six percent of Colorado businesses surveyed say tariffs are a challenge, and the state's effective tariff rate has increased sevenfold — yet Boebert told CPR News, "I think President Trump is doing just fine on the tariffs issue."

---

### Supported Tariffs That Are Raising Costs for Colorado Families

**Boebert Voted To Keep Trump's 25% Tariffs On Canada While Colorado Families Face Higher Prices.** According to CPR News, the House voted 219-211 to disapprove of Trump's 25% tariff on Canada, but Boebert voted to keep the tariffs in place, telling CPR News, "'I think President Trump is doing just fine on the tariffs issue, and I'm voting with the White House on this one.'" Fellow Colorado Republican Jeff Hurd broke with Boebert and voted to end the tariffs. [CPR News, [2/11/26](https://www.cpr.org/2026/02/11/u-s-house-votes-against-trumps-tariff-on-canada-with-help-of-hurd-and-five-other-republicans/)]

**Yale Budget Lab Estimated Tariffs Cost The Average American Household Up To $2,400 Per Year, With Low-Income Families Hit Hardest.** According to the Yale Budget Lab, "the price level from all 2025 tariffs rises by 1.7% in the short-run, the equivalent of an average per household income loss of $2,400." The burden falls disproportionately on lower-income households, costing 2.4% of post-tax income for the poorest families compared to 0.8% for the wealthiest. [Yale Budget Lab, [9/26/25](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/state-us-tariffs-september-26-2025)]

**Eighty-Six Percent Of Colorado Businesses Surveyed Said Tariffs Were A Challenge, Not A Benefit.** According to The Denver Post, a state report coordinated by three Colorado agencies found that "86% of the businesses view the tariffs levied this year as challenges and only 14% see them as beneficial," with manufacturers "incurring costs as high as $2 million." Colorado's effective tariff rate has increased sevenfold since last year. [Denver Post, [11/30/25](https://www.denverpost.com/2025/11/30/tariffs-colorado-business-agriculture/)]

### Tariffs Are Devastating Colorado's Agriculture Industry — Boebert's Own Backyard

**Colorado's Beef Industry Faced A $39 Million Revenue Drop From Tariffs, Threatening The State's Top Agricultural Export.** According to The Denver Post, a state report projected tariffs caused a "$39 million revenue drop" in the beef industry in the first half of the year compared to 2024, along with "265 fewer jobs" and an "$80 million decline" in economic output statewide. Beef is Colorado's top agricultural export, and the state's herd of 2.5 million head is its third-lowest in 50 years. [Denver Post, [10/23/25](https://www.denverpost.com/2025/10/23/colorado-agriculture-tariffs-beef/)]

**San Luis Valley Potato Farmers Lost An Estimated $150,000 Per Crop Circle Due To Tariff-Driven Market Instability.** According to the Governor's office, the cost of production of potatoes in the San Luis Valley has hovered from "$8.00 to $11.00 per hundredweight" while return pricing sat at "$5.00 to $6.00 per cwt," and with a typical yield of 50,000 cwt per circle, "farmers are losing an estimated $150,000 per circle." [Colorado Governor's Office, [11/20/25](https://www.colorado.gov/governor/news/governor-polis-releases-new-report-detailing-how-trumps-tariff-taxes-are-squeezing-colorado)]

**Rocky Mountain Farmers Union President Said Tariffs Were Forcing U.S. Producers Into Secondary Markets At Lower Prices.** According to ColoradoBiz, Rocky Mountain Farmers Union president Chad Franke identified wheat as "disproportionately impacted," explaining that tariffs push premium markets to source from Brazil or Argentina, "forcing U.S. wheat into secondary markets at lower prices." Fertilizer costs from Canada, packaging from China (up 50% in five years), and crop chemicals all face tariff-driven increases. [ColoradoBiz, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://coloradobiz.com/what-tariffs-will-mean-for-colorados-agriculture-industry/)]

**Colorado Agriculture Commissioner Said Tariff Chaos Made It 'Incredibly Difficult' For Producers To Plan.** According to The Denver Post, Agriculture Commissioner Kate Greenberg stated that tariff-related chaos has made it "incredibly difficult" for producers to plan for upcoming seasons, especially regarding operating loans and market access. Eighty percent of agricultural respondents anticipated negative tariff impacts. [Denver Post, [11/30/25](https://www.denverpost.com/2025/11/30/tariffs-colorado-business-agriculture/)]

### Voted To Cut Food Assistance for 131,000 Low-Income Coloradans

**Boebert Voted For The One Big Beautiful Bill, Which Could Strip SNAP Benefits From 131,000 Coloradans.** According to The Denver Post, the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities estimated that 131,000 people — "roughly 20% of the people who receive SNAP" in Colorado — could lose access under the bill's expanded work requirements, which now cover adults up to age 64 and parents of children over age 7. More than 615,000 Coloradans currently use SNAP. [Denver Post, [6/2/25](https://www.denverpost.com/2025/06/02/colorado-snap-trump-tax-bill/)]

- **Hunger Free Colorado Director Called The SNAP Cuts 'Slashing Support For 1 In 10 Coloradans.'** According to The Denver Post, Anya Rose, director of public policy at Hunger Free Colorado, stated, "'This is slashing support for 1 in 10 Coloradans who rely on SNAP. And then it's shifting billions in costs to the states.'" Colorado faces approximately $259 million in new annual costs by fiscal year 2028 under the bill's cost-shifting provisions. [Denver Post, [6/2/25](https://www.denverpost.com/2025/06/02/colorado-snap-trump-tax-bill/)]

**Boebert Defended SNAP Cuts By Saying 'If You're Able To Work, Then You Should Go To Work.'** According to CPR News, Boebert said she supported stricter work requirements to ensure "able-bodied adults without dependents" are working, stating, "'If you're able to work, then you should go to work.'" Research suggests such requirements reduce access to food assistance without increasing employment. [CPR News, [7/11/25](https://www.cpr.org/2025/07/11/one-big-beautiful-bill-colorado-patients-medicaid-planned-parenthood-snap/)]

### Voted Against Lowering Prescription Drug and Health Care Costs

**Boebert Voted Against The Inflation Reduction Act, Which Capped Insulin At $35 And Allowed Medicare To Negotiate Drug Prices For The First Time.** According to Newsweek, Boebert called the Inflation Reduction Act "a complete scam," despite the law capping insulin costs at $35 per month for Medicare recipients and allowing Medicare to negotiate prescription drug prices for the first time. The law also funded CS Wind's $200 million expansion in Pueblo, creating 850 new jobs in her own district. [Newsweek, [11/29/23](https://www.newsweek.com/biden-boebert-colorado-inflation-reduction-act-cs-wind-1847864)]

**Boebert Voted Against The Affordable Insulin Now Act, Which Would Have Capped Insulin Costs At $35 For All Americans.** According to NPR, the House passed the Affordable Insulin Now Act on March 31, 2022, by a vote of 232 to 193, with only 12 Republicans voting in favor. Boebert was among the 193 members who voted against capping the price of insulin at $35 per month. [NPR, [3/31/22](https://www.npr.org/2022/03/31/1090085513/house-passes-bill-to-cap-insulin-prices)]

**Boebert Voted For The Largest Medicaid Cuts In History, Threatening Coverage For Up To 230,000 Coloradans.** According to The Colorado Sun, all four Colorado Republicans in the House voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill, which the Congressional Budget Office projects will cause "11.8 million more people" to "lose health insurance coverage" nationwide, with "$1.2 trillion in cutbacks to Medicaid and food stamps." Roughly 16% of Boebert's district relies on Medicaid, including nearly 45,000 children. [Colorado Sun, [7/3/25](https://coloradosun.com/2025/07/03/colorado-votes-big-beautiful-bill-congress/)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**A Boebert Constituent Said Her Son Would Die Without Medicaid.** According to Westword, Dawn Caldwell of Highlands Ranch confronted Boebert at a protest, stating, "'My son will die without Medicaid. This will make life almost impossible for most people with disabilities.'" [Westword, [5/29/25](https://www.westword.com/news/boebert-evans-face-denver-protest-after-snap-medicaid-cuts-24668981/)]

**Colorado Rural Hospitals Were Already Laying Off Workers As Medicaid Cuts Loomed.** According to CPR News, Arkansas Valley Regional Medical Center in La Junta was "laying off approximately 5% of its workforce" and "previously closed its obstetric services in April," citing "'cost increases, inflationary pressures, and declining reimbursement rates.'" The hospital is one of six rural Colorado facilities at risk of closure. [CPR News, [6/27/25](https://www.cpr.org/2025/06/27/la-junta-hospital-lay-offs-medicaid-cuts/)]

**Colorado Ranchers Said Tariff-Driven Cost Increases Made It Harder To Stay In Business.** According to The Denver Post, ranchers finally achieved fair returns as beef prices climbed 14%, but Trump's proposal to import Argentine beef and ongoing tariff uncertainty threatened those gains. Equipment costs surged — tractor tires jumped from $1,500 to $5,000 each — and global fertilizer prices rose 15%, with much of the supply sourced from tariff-affected Canada. [Denver Post, [10/23/25](https://www.denverpost.com/2025/10/23/colorado-agriculture-tariffs-beef/)]
