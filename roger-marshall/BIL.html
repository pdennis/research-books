Overview

Roger Marshall turned his back on Kansans when he failed to support the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law. Marshall panned the bill, called it names, and voted it down. Thankfully, the bill still passed, because Kansas saw billions of dollars invested in a sprawling rejuvenation of infrastructure across the board, benefiting Kansans from all walks of live in every corner of the state. Marshall seems to be thankful, too, because he has made a habit of taking credit for projects around Kansas that stem from the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law he so vehemently opposed.

Marshall Opposed The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law

Marshall Voted To Tank The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law

2021: Marshall Voted Against The Bipartisan Infrastructure Package, Which Would Provide $550 Billion In New Infrastructure Funding, Including For Surface Transportation, Broadband, Water And Energy Infrastructure. In August 2021, Marshall voted against the Infrastructure Investment And Jobs Act which would, according to Congressional Quarterly, “provide approximately $550 billion in new infrastructure spending, including for surface transportation, broadband, water and energy infrastructure.” The vote was on passage. The Senate passed the bill by a vote of 69-30, resolved differences with the House, sent it to the President and ultimately became law. [Senate Vote 314, 8/10/21; Congressional Quarterly, 8/10/21; Congressional Actions, H.R. 3684]

  • The Infrastructure Investment And Jobs Act Received Bipartisan Support, In Which 19 Republicans Voted With Democrats, Including Senator Mitch McConnell, Despite Donald Trump’s Calls To Oppose The Legislation. According to NPR, “The 19 Republicans who voted for the bill, including Senate Minority Leader Mitch McConnell, R-Ky., did so despite pressure from former President Donald Trump to deny Democrats a victory. Trump released a statement that called the bill ‘the beginning of the Green New Deal.’” [NPR, 8/10/21]

The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law Fueled Billions Of Dollars In Investments In Kansas Infrastructure Projects

2024: The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law Had Secured $3.3 Billion In Funding For Over 376 Infrastructure Projects Across Kansas

2024: The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law Had Secured $3.3 Billion In Funding For Over 376 Infrastructure Projects Across Kansas. According to KCLY, “On the three-year anniversary of the bipartisan infrastructure law, Representative Sharice Davids highlighted how the law is already benefiting Kansas. The law is bringing about $3.3 billion to the state, funding over 376 projects to improve roads, water quality, broadband access, and more. Davids, who serves on the U.S. House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee, worked with the President and White House officials to help pass this legislation. The law focuses on supporting communities across Kansas, including rural areas and historically underserved populations, with investments that will improve life for residents and help the state succeed.” [KCLY, 11/18/24]

  • Kansas Was Slated To Receive $2.8 Billion Over Five Years For Roads And Bridges. According to KCLY, “In Kansas, there are 1,294 bridges and more than 1,967 miles of highway in poor condition. Kansas is expected to receive approximately $2.8 billion over five years for highways and bridges.” [KCLY, 11/18/24]
  • Kansas Was Slated To Receive $259 Million Over Five Years For Public Transit Development. According to KCLY, “Kansas is expected to receive $259 million over five years under the bipartisan infrastructure law to improve public transportation options across the state.” [KCLY, 11/18/24]
  • Kansas Was Slated To Receive $40 Million Over Five Years To Build Up Electric Vehicle Charging. According to KCLY, “Through the National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure Program alone, Kansas should expect to receive roughly $40 million over five years to support the expansion of electric vehicle charging in the state.” [KCLY, 11/18/24]
  • Kansas Was Slated To Receive $110 Million To Improve Its Airports. According to a press release from the Department of Transportation, “Under the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, airports in Kansas would receive approximately $110 million for infrastructure development for airports over five years (7). This funding will address airside and landside needs at airports, such as improving runways, taxiways and airport-owned towers, terminal development projects, and noise reduction projects.” [Press Release – Department of Transportation, 11/18/21]

The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law Unlocked Over $218 Million For Kansas Water Infrastructure Upgrades

The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law Unlocked Over $218 Million For Kansas Water Infrastructure Upgrades. According to a press release from the Environmental Protection Agency, “Today, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)  announced $56,829,000 from President Biden’s Investing in America Agenda for Kansas drinking water and clean water infrastructure upgrades. […] The funding is part of the over $50 billion investment in water infrastructure upgrades from President Biden’s Bipartisan Infrastructure Law – the largest such investment in American history. Today’s announcement will support essential water infrastructure that protects public health and treasured water bodies across the state. Almost half of this funding will be available as grants or principal forgiveness loans, ensuring that funds reach underserved communities most in need of investments in water infrastructure.[…] Since 2022, the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law has injected over $218 million into water infrastructure projects across the state, protecting public health, preserving water resources, and creating jobs.” [Press Release – Environmental Protection Agency, 2/10/24]

The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law Provided $42.5 Million To Strengthen Internet Access Across Kansas

The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law Provided $42.5 Million To Strengthen Internet Access Across Kansas. According to a press release from Rep. Davids, “Today, Representative Sharice Davids announced the U.S. Department of Commerce’s National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) awarded Kansas with $42.5 million in bipartisan infrastructure law funding to expand middle mile high-speed internet across the state, including in all five counties in Kansas’ Third Congressional District. […] ‘The internet is used to access crucial resources such as employment opportunities, business software capabilities, virtual learning, and health care,’ said Davids. ‘This new federal investment will help close the digital gap and keep our communities connected – particularly those who have not had access to high-speed internet in the past. I am proud to have voted for the bipartisan infrastructure law that continues to make a positive impact on the lives of Kansas families.’” [Press Release – Rep. Davids, 6/16/23]

Marshall Took Credit For The Infrastructure Law He Voted Against

Marshall Touted Funding For Highway And Port Projects Enabled By The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law

Marshall Touted Funding For Highway And Port Projects Enabled By The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law. According to The Kansas City Star, “When the U.S. Senate passed a bipartisan bill to invest in the nation’s infrastructure in 2021, Sen. Roger Marshall attacked the legislation as “wasteful spending” and voted against it. Nearly five years later, the Republican senator celebrated that he had secured $191.7 million to support Kansas in 2025. He pointed to $98 million for a highway project in Olathe and a $25 million grant for the Stafford County Port Authority. ‘My team and I are here for Kansans,’ Marshall said in a statement touting the funding. But money for both of those projects came from the 2021 law that Marshall voted against and actively opposed, called the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act.” [Kansas City Star, 7/8/26]
