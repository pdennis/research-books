# Rep. Buddy Carter: Big Pharma's Favorite Son

### Big Pharma's Favorite Son

**Carter Accepted Over $500,000 From The Pharmaceutical And Health Industry Over His Congressional Career.** According to Federal Election Commission records, Carter's principal campaign committee received at least $392,894 in contributions matching pharmaceutical-related PACs, plus additional contributions from major drug companies including Johnson & Johnson ($46,049), McKesson ($45,000), Amgen ($41,000), AbbVie ($33,000), Sanofi ($30,500), Pfizer ($27,500), Genentech ($25,500), Merck ($24,500), and Eli Lilly ($20,500). His leadership PAC, BUDDY PAC, received an additional $82,000 from pharmacy and pharmaceutical PACs. [FEC, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00543967/)]

**Carter's Pharmacy Distributed Nearly 3 Million Opioid Pills In Chatham County.** According to The Current, "from 2006 to 2014, Carter Pharmacy Inc. received the fourth-highest number of oxycodone and hydrocodone pills in Chatham County: 2,876,010 pills." The county overall received over 80 million pain pills during this period — approximately 34 pills annually per resident. [Current, [10/31/22](https://thecurrentga.org/2022/10/31/carter-removed-from-wrongful-death-lawsuit-involving-savannah-opioid-overdose-victim/)]

**Carter Accepted $55,000 From His Former Opioid Supplier McKesson.** According to FEC records, McKesson Corp. — Carter's primary opioid supplier from 2006 to 2014 and the nation's largest prescription opioid distributor — contributed $45,000 to Carter's principal campaign committee and $10,000 to his leadership PAC BUDDY PAC between 2013 and 2025, including contributions as recently as May 2025. [FEC, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00543967/)]

**Carter Voted Against Capping Insulin Costs At $35 A Month, Called The Bill A "Socialist Plan."** According to NPR, the House passed the Affordable Insulin Now Act 232-193 on March 31, 2022, capping insulin costs at $35 per month or 25% of an insurance plan's negotiated price. Carter — a pharmacist — voted against the measure, saying, "'We want lower prices for drugs, particularly for insulin. But instead of fixing a broken system, this bill aims to control it,'" and called it a "socialist plan." [NPR, [3/31/22](https://www.npr.org/2022/03/31/1090085513/house-passes-bill-to-cap-insulin-prices)]

**Carter Transferred His Pharmacies To His Wife, Then Co-Sponsored Bills That Would Benefit Them.** According to The Atlanta Journal-Constitution, Carter co-sponsored legislation that could benefit his pharmacy business after transferring his pharmacies to his wife, backing measures that would give seniors on Medicare more access to prescription drugs at their pharmacies and expand pharmacists' scope of practice. [Atlanta Journal-Constitution, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.ajc.com/blog/politics/buddy-carter-faces-conflict-interest-questions-over-pharmacy-bills/cS6MRQq7C7V2F4WVWURLbO/)]

- **2016: Carter Called Himself "A Big Fan Of Big Pharma" On C-SPAN.** According to C-SPAN, during a March 2016 hearing on opioid addiction and treatment, Carter stated he is "a big fan of big pharma" because he has "seen miracles in drugs" throughout his career as a pharmacist. [C-SPAN, [3/23/16](https://www.c-span.org/video/?436157)]

**Carter Backed Repealing The Inflation Reduction Act's Drug Pricing Provisions, Calling It "The Worst Legislation I Have Ever Seen."** According to Axios, Carter replied "yes" when asked if he backed repeal of the drug pricing law, saying "'My hope is that it will be eliminated'" and "'In 10 years in the state legislature and 10 years in Congress, this is the worst legislation I've ever seen.'" The IRA allows Medicare to negotiate drug prices directly with pharmaceutical companies for the first time. [Axios, [9/23/22](https://www.axios.com/2022/09/23/gop-drug-price-repeal-target)]

- **Carter's 2022 Opponent Called Him "Big Pharma's Favorite Son."** According to The Current, Carter's opponent Wade Herring "alleged Carter receives substantial pharmaceutical industry donations," dubbing him "'Big Pharma's Favorite Son'" in campaign materials after Carter's public statements opposing drug price regulations. [Current, [10/18/22](https://thecurrentga.org/2022/10/18/coastal-ga-house-debate-sheds-light-on-healthcare-costs-personal-wealth/)]

**Carter Opposed The Insulin Price Cap While Claiming Pharmacist Credentials.** According to The Current, Carter invoked his pharmacy background during a 2022 debate, stating "'For 40 years, I was behind the counter'" and "'I was the one who had to tell the patient how much their prescription was going to be'" — yet voted against the measure that would have capped that very cost at $35 per month. [Current, [10/18/22](https://thecurrentga.org/2022/10/18/coastal-ga-house-debate-sheds-light-on-healthcare-costs-personal-wealth/)]
