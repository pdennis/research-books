# Rep. Buddy Carter (GA-01): Can't Be Trusted on Health Care

Carter — a pharmacist whose own pharmacy distributed nearly 3 million opioid pills and who has accepted over $100,000 from his former opioid supplier — has spent a decade working to strip health coverage from his constituents. He voted to repeal the ACA, voted for $625 billion in Medicaid cuts that could leave 750,000 Georgians uninsured, refused to restore Medicaid funding to avert a government shutdown, voted against extending ACA subsidies that 95% of Georgia marketplace enrollees depend on, and pushed legislation to expand junk insurance plans that don't cover preexisting conditions. Meanwhile, rural hospitals in and around his district — like Evans Memorial in Claxton — are closing critical services, and Carter has declined to address the crisis his votes created.

### Pharmacy Profits and Industry Ties

**Carter's Pharmacy Distributed Nearly 3 Million Opioid Pills In Chatham County.** According to The Current, "from 2006 to 2014, Carter Pharmacy Inc. received the fourth-highest number of oxycodone and hydrocodone pills in Chatham County: 2,876,010 pills." The county overall received over 80 million pain pills during this period — approximately 34 pills annually per resident. [Current, [10/31/22](https://thecurrentga.org/2022/10/31/carter-removed-from-wrongful-death-lawsuit-involving-savannah-opioid-overdose-victim/)]

**Carter Accepted $108,000 From His Former Opioid Supplier.** According to The Current, McKesson Corp. was Carter's primary opioid supplier from 2006 to 2014 and the nation's largest prescription opioid distributor. The corporation and its affiliates contributed $108,000 to Carter and his leadership PAC between 2014 and 2022, including $42,500 during the 2020-2022 cycles after McKesson pledged to suspend contributions to certain members of Congress. [Current, [10/31/22](https://thecurrentga.org/2022/10/31/carter-removed-from-wrongful-death-lawsuit-involving-savannah-opioid-overdose-victim/)]

**Carter Faced Conflict Of Interest Questions Over Pharmacy Bills.** According to The Atlanta Journal-Constitution, Carter co-sponsored legislation that could benefit his pharmacy business after transferring his pharmacies to his wife, backing measures that would give seniors on Medicare more access to prescription drugs at their pharmacies and expand pharmacists' scope of practice. [Atlanta Journal-Constitution, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.ajc.com/blog/politics/buddy-carter-faces-conflict-interest-questions-over-pharmacy-bills/cS6MRQq7C7V2F4WVWURLbO/)]

### Voted to Gut the ACA and Medicaid

**Carter Voted For The American Health Care Act, Which Weakened Protections For Preexisting Conditions.** According to GovTrack, Carter voted for the American Health Care Act on May 4, 2017, which allowed states to waive community rating requirements for people with preexisting conditions, meaning insurers could charge them dramatically higher premiums. [GovTrack, [5/4/17](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/votes/115-2017/h256)]

**2017: Carter Called Medicaid A "Safety Net" That Had Been Abused By "Able-Bodied Adults."** According to NPR, Carter stated: "Medicaid was intended to be a safety net. Instead, you know, we've called this Obamacare. It should have been called Obamacaid [...] we've taken able-bodied adults and put them on a safety net program." [NPR, [3/8/17](https://www.npr.org/2017/03/08/519170578/rep-buddy-carter-on-the-republican-health-care-plan)]

**Carter Called For "Blanket Repeal" Of The Affordable Care Act.** According to a press release from Carter's office, Carter stated: "Today, I am calling for a vote on a blanket repeal of Obamacare." Carter warned against letting Washington dysfunction prevent action, saying "The games have gone on for too long." [Rep. Buddy Carter Press Release, [7/18/17](https://buddycarter.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=2215)]

**Carter Voted For $625 Billion In Medicaid Cuts On The Energy And Commerce Committee.** According to The Current, the Energy and Commerce Committee — on which Carter sits — approved legislation cutting $625 billion in federal Medicaid spending over the next decade. The Congressional Budget Office estimated approximately 10.3 million people would lose Medicaid or CHIP access, with 7.6 million becoming uninsured. [Current, [5/15/25](https://thecurrentga.org/2025/05/15/u-s-house-panel-passes-gop-plan-that-cuts-medicaid-by-625b-adds-work-requirement/)]

- **Georgia's Work Requirements Showed The Consequences Of Carter's Approach.** According to The Current, committee Democrats noted that in Georgia, "fewer than 7,000 of the 400,000 people eligible for Medicaid were able to prove to the government they met the standards" under work requirements — demonstrating that the red tape Carter supported would strip coverage from people who technically qualify. [Current, [5/15/25](https://thecurrentga.org/2025/05/15/u-s-house-panel-passes-gop-plan-that-cuts-medicaid-by-625b-adds-work-requirement/)]

**Carter Refused To Restore Medicaid Funding To Avert A Government Shutdown.** According to Georgia Public Broadcasting, Carter refused to consider restoring Medicaid cuts to prevent a government shutdown, defending the cuts by saying: "What we did is, simply, save that program, stabilize that program." Carter cited a disputed claim that 1.4 million undocumented immigrants were enrolled in Medicaid, which experts called inaccurate. [GPB, [9/30/25](https://www.gpb.org/news/2025/09/30/gas-buddy-carter-says-he-wont-consider-restoring-medicaid-money-avert-shutdown)]

### Voted Against Affordable Premiums

**Carter Voted Against ACA Subsidy Extension As Premiums Could Spike Up To $885 Per Month In His District.** According to The Atlanta Journal-Constitution, Carter voted against extending ACA tax credits for three years, even as the Kaiser Family Foundation found premiums could increase up to $885 per month in his district. Approximately 1.4 million Georgians face premium increases, and an estimated 460,000 Georgians are expected to drop coverage entirely. [Georgia Democrat, [1/9/26](https://georgiademocrat.org/ajc-reps-buddy-carter-and-mike-collins-once-again-vote-against-aca-tax-credit-extension/)]

**Carter Pushed Junk Insurance Plans That Do Not Cover Preexisting Conditions.** According to The Washington Times, Carter filed legislation to expand the use of "short-term" insurance plans for up to three years. These plans do not have to cover people with preexisting medical conditions and do not have to include benefits like prescription drugs or maternity care — directly contradicting Carter's 2017 promise on NPR that "people with preexisting conditions would be able to keep their insurance." [Washington Times, [1/14/25](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/jan/14/gop-rep-buddy-carter-files-bill-expand-use-short-t/)]

### Local Impact: Rural Hospitals in Crisis

**Evans Memorial Hospital In Claxton Faced $3.3 Million In Annual Cuts And Closed Its OB/GYN Unit.** According to Sen. Warnock's office, Evans Memorial Hospital — located adjacent to Carter's district in rural Evans County — faces a $3.3 million annual budget shortfall due to the legislation Carter supported. The hospital has already closed its labor and delivery unit and may need to cut intensive care or cardio-pulmonary rehabilitation services. [Sen. Warnock Press Release, [Viewed 2/16/26](https://www.warnock.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/icymi-warnock-visits-rural-hospital-facing-3-3-million-in-annual-cuts-due-to-gop-tax-bill/)]

**Carter Declined To Address Rural Hospital Funding Losses When Asked By Local Press.** According to The Effingham Herald, Carter declined to directly address questions about rural hospital Medicaid funding losses during a Q&A about the One Big Beautiful Bill, instead pivoting to talking points about tax savings and border security. [Effingham Herald, [8/19/25](https://www.effinghamherald.net/local/qa-rep-buddy-carter-explains-support-for-one-big-beautiful-bill/)]

**Georgia Hospitals Face $3.7 Billion Revenue Loss If ACA Subsidies Carter Opposed Expire.** According to The Atlanta Journal-Constitution via GPB, Georgia hospitals and health care providers face a potential $3.7 billion revenue loss in 2026 if ACA subsidies expire — subsidies Carter voted against extending. [GPB, [9/30/25](https://www.gpb.org/news/2025/09/30/gas-buddy-carter-says-he-wont-consider-restoring-medicaid-money-avert-shutdown)]

**Georgia Has The Second-Highest Uninsured Rate In The Country.** According to GPB, Georgia is tied with Oklahoma for the second-highest uninsured rate in the nation — a crisis Carter's votes to cut Medicaid and block ACA subsidies would worsen. [GPB, [9/30/25](https://www.gpb.org/news/2025/09/30/gas-buddy-carter-says-he-wont-consider-restoring-medicaid-money-avert-shutdown)]

### Impact on Everyday People

**2.2 Million Georgians On Medicaid Face Coverage Uncertainty Due To Cuts Carter Championed.** According to Georgia Public Broadcasting, 2.2 million Georgians are currently covered by Medicaid, including 42% of Georgia's children. Advocates warned the proposed cuts threaten optional services like home-based care for people with disabilities, with one advocate stating: "We can't afford to lose any money. We're already scraping the bone." [GPB, [5/21/25](https://www.gpb.org/news/2025/05/21/advocates-warn-proposed-medicaid-cuts-threaten-georgias-safety-net-programs)]

**Georgia Residents Caught In The Coverage Gap Accumulated Tens Of Thousands In Medical Debt.** According to Georgia Public Broadcasting, Alton Fry, a Franklin County stone mason with prostate cancer, accumulated $37,000 in medical debt within six months — caught in the coverage gap earning too much for Medicaid but too little for private insurance. Georgia's refusal to expand Medicaid, which Carter supports, leaves residents like Fry with no affordable options. [GPB, [5/21/25](https://www.gpb.org/news/2025/05/21/advocates-warn-proposed-medicaid-cuts-threaten-georgias-safety-net-programs)]

**Proposed Medicaid Changes Could Lead To Significant Medical Debt For Families Of Children With Disabilities.** According to Georgia Public Broadcasting, the proposed rollback of retroactive Medicaid coverage from three months to one month could "lead to significant medical debt, especially for families of children with disabilities," with 8,000 people already waiting for home and community-based services in Georgia. [GPB, [5/21/25](https://www.gpb.org/news/2025/05/21/advocates-warn-proposed-medicaid-cuts-threaten-georgias-safety-net-programs)]
