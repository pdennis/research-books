Overview

  • Until recently, Lujan had refused to take a position on school vouchers, but when it came time to stand up for public schools, Lujan failed them. He has supported Governor Greg Abbott’s push to allow taxpayer dollars to flow to private schools. In 2023, he sided with Abbott on an unsuccessful plan that sought to direct taxpayer money to private schools while holding public school funding hostage as leverage. 
  • Most recently, he voted for Abbott’s plan that rebranded vouchers as “education savings account” and provided $1 billion for families to claim in taxpayer funding to be used for private school tuition – the third and fourth tiers of the eligibility criteria would allow higher-income earners to claim the credit. Following the failure of his voucher agenda in years past, Abbott had spent millions of dollars supporting pro-voucher Republican candidates in the 2024 state legislative elections, including endorsing Lujan, in order to shift the votes in favor of his anti-public school agenda. 
  • Lujan has claimed to support public schools in the past but still supported school vouchers anyway and failed to help public schools when they needed him. Public schools in Lujan’s state legislative district, including his alma matter, have been forced to close due to budget shortfalls. Almost every single school district in the San Antonio area faced a funding shortfall after the Texas legislature did not use its $33 billion surplus to alleviate the public school financial crisis. One of the largest school districts in Lujan’s district was forced to close schools, but Lujan did not show up to important hearings to discuss the state’s funding position.

Senate Bill 2 Provided $1 Billion For Families To Claim In Taxpayer Funding To Be Used For Private School Tuition – The Third And Fourth Tiers Of Eligibility Would Allow Higher-Income Earners To Claim Some

May 2025: Abbott Signed Senate Bill 2 Into Law, Which Allocated $1 Billion For Families To Use To Send Their Children To Schools Outside Of The State Public School System. According to the Houston Public Media, “It was a moment Texas Gov. Greg Abbott had been waiting years for. Flanked by supporters, the state's top Republican signed Senate Bill 2 into law Saturday afternoon at the governor's mansion in Austin. The sweeping $1 billion school voucher legislation creates Education Savings Accounts (ESAs), which will allow parents to use state tax dollars toward costs associated with educating their children outside of Texas' public school system.” [Houston Public Media, 5/2/25]

  • Abbott Had Pushed To Pass School Voucher Legislation Since 2022. According to the Houston Public Media, “Saturday's signing ceremony was the culmination of a yearslong effort from top Texas Republicans and the governor. ‘When I ran for reelection in 2022, I promised school choice for the families of Texans. Today we deliver on that promise,’ Abbott said outside the governor's mansion in front of a crowd of hundreds. […] It has been a hard fought path to get the bill to Abbott's desk — including millions of dollars spent on media campaigns by various supporters and tough primary battles last year to oust Republicans who voted against a similar proposal in 2023.” [Houston Public Media, 5/2/25]

Senate Bill 2 Allowed Parents To Get $10,000 In Taxpayer Funding To Use For Private School Tuition. According to the Houston Public Media, "Here in the Lone Star State, the program will allow parents to receive around $10,000 — or 85% of what the state gives students per year, on average — to put toward private school tuition. Students with special needs will be able to use up to $30,000 in state funds. Families who homeschool their children can receive $2,000 to be used on approved expenses." [Houston Public Media, 5/2/25]

Eligibility For Texas’ School Voucher Program Was Based On A Four-Tiered System With The Third And Fourth Tiers Allowing Higher-Income Families To Claim The Credit. According to the Houston Public Media, "When it comes to ESA eligibility, SB 2 dictates they be awarded to families based on a four-tiered system. First dibs will go to students with disabilities from families earning below a certain income threshold. Second in line are families making at or below $62,400 for a family of four. Third in line are higher income families. From there, any leftover slots in the program will be available to any family, regardless of income. During the fierce debate around the bill at the Capitol, several Texas Democrats focused on that fourth tier." [Houston Public Media, 5/2/25]

Texas Opponents Of School Vouchers Argued The Legislation Would Be Abused By Wealthier Families And Harm Already Struggling Public Schools

Texas Democrats And Rural Republicans Argued School Vouchers Would Harm Already Struggling Public Schools Who Were A Major Employers For Working Families And A Resource Center For Texas Students, A Majority Of Whom Resided In Low Income Households. According to Texas Tribune, “Gov. Greg Abbott on Saturday signed legislation authorizing a private school voucher program into law, marking the grand finale of an oftentimes ugly conflict that has largely defined Texas politics this decade. Senate Bill 2 will allow families to use public taxpayer dollars to fund their children’s education at an accredited private school or to pay for a wide range of school-related expenses, like textbooks, transportation or therapy. The program will be one of the largest school voucher initiatives in the nation. [...] The law’s passage follows years of discord in the Legislature over school vouchers. The Democrats and rural Republicans who fought against it argued that the program would harm already-struggling public schools, a major employer for working families and a resource center for many Texas students — the majority of whom reside in low-income households.” [Texas Tribune, 5/3/25]

Most Texas Families Were Eligible To Participate In The States’ Voucher Program, Including Wealthier Families Who Earned More Than 500% Or More Of The Poverty Rate. According to Texas Tribune, “Almost any school-age child in Texas can apply for and participate in the voucher program, including students already attending private schools. Up to 20% of the program’s initial $1 billion budget could flow to wealthier families who earn 500% or more of the poverty rate — roughly $160,000 or above for a family of four.” [Texas Tribune, 5/3/25]

Public Schools In Texas Were Funded Based On The Number Of Students In Attendance; Meaning Student Departures Due To School Vouchers Would Deplete Public School Funding. According to San Antonio Express News, “In other states, vouchers have largely gone to students who already attend private schools, but any voucher system would likely result in some students leaving public schools for private ones. Since public schools in Texas are funded by the state based on the number of students in attendance, any student departures would deplete their dollars.” [San Antonio Express News, 10/3/24]
