Overview

  • Lujan has proven he will say and support whatever Republican party leaders want in order to get elected. In 2021, he was accused of conspiring with Governor Abbott during the Texas House District 118 special runoff election when he dispersed campaign literature that told voters the date of the election when it had not been officially announced yet. He also supported gerrymandering Texas’ congressional maps, a public desire of President Trump. His reasons for keeping the maps gerrymandered were poor, one being that he had already started raising money to run in the new TX-35 so it would be unfair if Texas reverted to the 2021 map, and the second being that he would not live in TX-35 if Texas reverted to the 2021 maps. 
  • Perhaps Trump snubbed Lujan of an endorsement because he has not always been consistent about whether he supported Trump. While running for TX-35, Lujan has said he will fully support Trump’s agenda if he gets elected to Congress and he has admitted to voting for him in the 2020 presidential election. However, in 2016, Lujan said Trump’s presidential campaign would hurt down ballot Republicans in Texas and he criticized the president’s tweeting in 2018. 
  • In 2006, he even admitted that he had not voted in a Republican primary since 1998 and that he was “more of a general election voter.” If he couldn’t even be bothered to show up to support Republican candidates in primary elections, how can Texans count on him to show up and vote in Congress?

According to ABC KVUE, “The battle over Texas' congressional district map is now in the hands of the U.S. Supreme Court. The high court is set to decide whether the state can use it in next year's midterm elections. For now, Texas can temporarily use its 2025 congressional map while the court battle over its legality plays out. […] Republican candidates also filed briefs on Monday to support the state's argument in the Supreme Court. ‘There have been candidates who've been actively campaigning all across the state, which is an enormous outlay of money,’ Trey Trainor, who is running for Congress in the 21st Congressional District, said. ‘People had already decided that they are living in certain districts, and the voters and everybody have kind of adjusted to that.’ He is one of six republican candidates who filed an amicus brief in support of the redrawn map. Eric Flores (CD-34), Chris Gober (CD-10), John Lujan (CD-35), Alexandra del Moral Mealer (CD-9), and Jessica Steinman (CD-8) were also a part of the brief.” [ABC KVUE, 11/25/25]
