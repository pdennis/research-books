Overview

  • Lujan had supported scandal-ridden San Antonio City Councilman Marc Whyte. Lujan endorsed Whyte’s 2023 and 2025 city council campaigns and Whyte had endorsed Lujan in return. In 2023, Whyte was arrested for drunk driving and eventually pleaded “no contest” to “obstruction of highway,” receiving probation, community service, and a fine as a sentence. Whyte has also twice been caught violating San Antonio’s ethics code. In 2024, the San Antonio Ethics Review board found Whyte guilty of using his position for private interests and using city resources in a way that was not lawfully available to the public. In 2025, Whyte was sanctioned, formally reprimanded, and fined over accusations that he used city resources for political purposes.

2023: Whyte Was Arrested For Intoxicated Driving

12/29/23: Marc Whyte Was Arrested For Driving While Under The Influence, A Class B Misdemeanor. According to a press release from the City of San Antonio, “On Friday, December 29, 2023, an SAPD Officer assigned to the DWI Unit conducted a traffic stop on a vehicle at the 300 block of East Bound NE Loop 410 Access Rd. The driver, identified as Marc Whyte District 10 Councilmember, showed signs of intoxication, and was evaluated at the scene for Driving While Intoxicated (DWI). Mr. Whyte was arrested for DWI 1st, a Class B misdemeanor.” [Press Release – City of San Antonio, 1/11/24]

  • A Class B Misdemeanor In Texas Was Punishable By Up To 180 Days In Jail And Up To A $2,000 Fine. According to Bexar County, TX, "Levels of Offenses: Class A A Class A Misdemeanor is punishable by up to one year in jail, and up to a $4,000.00 fine.  Class B A Class B Misdemeanor is punishable by up to 180 days in jail, and up to a $2,000.00 fine.  Class C A Class C Misdemeanor is punishable by a fine up to $500.00. There is no jail time for a Class C misdemeanor." [Bexar County, TX, Viewed 2/28/26]

In A Deal, Marc Whyte Pleaded “No Contest” To “Obstruction Of Highway,” Which Meant He Would Not Be Convicted Of A Dwi If He Completed Six Months Of Probation, 48 Hours Of Community Services, And Paid An $850 Fine. According to KSAT, "San Antonio Councilman Marc Whyte agreed to a plea deal Wednesday that changed his December 2023 drunk driving case to a non-DWI charge.  In an appearance before Bexar County Court 11 Judge Erica Pena, the District 10 councilman pleaded ‘no contest’ to ‘obstruction of a highway’ in exchange for deferred adjudication. That means he will not be convicted if he completes his six months of probation, which includes not drinking alcohol. Whyte was also given 48 hours of community service, fined $850, charged for $270 in court costs, and must pay monthly supervisory fees.  He must also undergo a DWI education course and a victim impact panel, though Whyte’s attorneys asked for credit for him already taking at least one of them. Though ‘obstruction of a highway’ is the same level offense as Whyte’s original DWI charge, a Class B Misdemeanor, it does not carry the same stigma." [KSAT, 11/6/24]

2024 & 2025: Marc Whyte Violated The San Antonio Ethics Code

June 2025: Whyte Was Sanctioned And Dealt A $2,500 Fine For Violating The San Antonio City Ethics Code

June 2025: Marc Whyte Was Sanctioned, Formally Reprimanded, And Dealt A $2,500 Fine Over Accusations He Used City Logos And Personnel On His Campaign Facebook Page. According to KSAT, “District 10 Councilman Marc Whyte and District 1 Councilwoman Sukh Kaur were both accused by their political opponents of violating a portion of the code that prohibits using public resources for campaign purposes. The panel considered the complaints at a Monday night meeting. Kaur was accused over a neighborhood meeting in which her chief of staff appeared to attend in his official capacity while also distributing campaign materials. Whyte was accused of using city logos and personnel on his campaign Facebook page. Both were sanctioned and required to take ethics training. But Whyte also received a letter of reprimand and a $2,500 civil fine.” [KSAT , 6/17/25]

August 2024: Marc Whyte Was Found Guilty Of Violating Three Sections Of The San Antonio Ethics Code

August 2024: The San Antonio Ethics Review Board Found Marc Whyte Guilty Of Using His Position As Councilman For Private Interests And Using City Resources In A Way Not Lawfully Available To The Public. According to KSAT, “In a rare hearing Wednesday night, the City of San Antonio’s Ethics Review Board ruled Councilman Marc Whyte (D10) abused his position when he tried to get local law enforcement to respond to a local attorney’s home this spring. [...] The council-appointed Ethics Review Board voted 6-1 to find Whyte had violated three sections of the city ethics code related to using his position for private interests and using city resources in a way not lawfully available to the public. It voted by the same margin to issue Whyte a letter of reprimand and referred him to ethics training.” [KSAT, 8/15/24]

  • May 2024: An Attorney Filed A Complaint Alleging That Marc Whyte Used His Position As A Councilman To Interfere In A Private Custody Matter. According to KSAT, "In a rare hearing Wednesday night, the City of San Antonio’s Ethics Review Board ruled Councilman Marc Whyte (D10) abused his position when he tried to get local law enforcement to respond to a local attorney’s home this spring.  The attorney, Martin Phipps, filed a complaint against Whyte in May, accusing him of using his position as a councilman to ‘interfere’ in a private custody matter. Whyte argued he was worried about possible child abuse and had a duty to act." [KSAT, 8/15/24]
  • An Outside Attorney Said Marc Whyte Knew Police Were Already Involved In The Matter By The Time He Tried Contacting The Heads Of Two Law Enforcement Agencies. According to KSAT, "However, Ross Fischer, an outside attorney hired by the city to assist the board, said that Whyte knew police were already involved by the time he tried contacting the heads of two law enforcement agencies. At least one board member also questioned why Whyte didn’t simply call 911.  In a presentation to the board, Fischer pointed to comments Whyte made to KSAT about how calling 911 ‘I don’t think would have given me information about what was going on.’" [KSAT, 8/15/24]
  • A Board Member Questioned Why Whyte Didn’t Just Call 911 If He Was Concerned. According to KSAT, "However, Ross Fischer, an outside attorney hired by the city to assist the board, said that Whyte knew police were already involved by the time he tried contacting the heads of two law enforcement agencies. At least one board member also questioned why Whyte didn’t simply call 911.  In a presentation to the board, Fischer pointed to comments Whyte made to KSAT about how calling 911 ‘I don’t think would have given me information about what was going on.’" [KSAT, 8/15/24]

The San Antonio Ethics Board Chairman Said The Ethics Complaint Against Marc Whyte Was The First In 12 Years To Make It To A Hearing, And Whyte’s Reprimand Was One Of The Board’s Most Severe Punishments. According to KSAT, “Board Chairman Patrick Lang said this was the first time in 12 years an ethics complaint had made it to a hearing and said the reprimand was one of the board’s most severe options. ‘So unfortunately, we’re only allowed to use what’s available to us,” Lang said. “So that ranges from that is a reprimand — you can have a civil fine and things of that nature. We decided that these two were best for us just because I think it covers, the gamut of what was — what took place, what we felt was accurate, and just the best case of action — the best course of action.’” [KSAT, 8/15/24]
