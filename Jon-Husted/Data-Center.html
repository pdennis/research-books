Ohioans' Rates Went Up Due To Data Centers

Ohioans' Rates Went Up Due, In Part, To Data Centers

Ohioans Had an Average 17 Percent Utility Bill Increase In Part Due To Data Centers Pushing Energy Demand. According to the Ohio Capital Journal, “Average Ohio residential utility bills are now projected to be about $800 for the summer, a new report says. That’s a 17% increase over the $682 Ohioans paid last summer, said the report, by Washington, D.C.-based Third Way. The estimates are of total average electricity costs from June to September. They were done using data from Heatmap News, which collaborated with the Massachusetts Institute of Technology to create the Electricity Price Hub. ‘The United States is currently experiencing record energy demand driven by data center growth, domestic manufacturing, and electrification,’ the report said. ‘And we simply aren’t bringing on new energy quickly enough or in large enough quantities to affordably meet rising demand.’” [Ohio Capital Journal, 7/7/26]

Last Summer, Ohio’s Electric Prices Spiked By 108% Due To Data Centers. According to the Ohio Capital Journal, “The estimated increase in Ohioans’ electricity bills comes after a big jump last year. Between May and July 2025, they saw a 108% spike, the report said.” [Ohio Capital Journal, 7/7/26]

Tax Breaks For Data Centers Cost Billions For Ohio Taxpayers

Tax Breaks for Data Centers Cost Ohio Over $1.6 Billion in Tax Revenue in 2025

Tax Breaks for Data Centers Cost Ohio Over $1.6 Billion in Tax Revenue in 2025. According to Signal, “Ohio’s biggest tax break for data centers is more expensive than once thought. A lot more expensive.  In 2024, the state sales tax exemption for data centers cost Ohio about $555 million in revenue, four times more than the state Department of Taxation forecasted. In 2025, it cost a whopping $1.6 billion, eleven times more than the original estimate of $136 million And that’s to say nothing of the local sales taxes – another $166.8 million in lost revenue in 2024, according to new actual cost data provided this week by Ohio Department of Taxation spokesperson Andrea Lannom.” [Signal, 5/21/26]
