Opponents Of A Medicare Voucher System Argue It Would Raise Costs For Enrollees And Lower The Quality Of Care

Under A Voucher System, Payments For Providers Would Be A Fixed, Pre-Determined Amount Of Money Per Patient, For A Set Time Period, Regardless Of How Many Medical Services The Patient Actually Receives. According to Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, “In 2016, House Republicans proposed the idea to transition to a voucher system, which they called a premium system, according to KFF, a nonpartisan, non-profit organization that focuses on health policy. Under that plan, healthcare plans would compete for enrollees, and then those on Medicare would choose among plans for their coverage. One big difference from the current system, however, is that under a voucher system, payments for providers would be a fixed, pre-determined amount of money per patient, for a set time period, regardless of how many medical services the patient actually receives. The current system ties payments to specific services.” [Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, 10/23/26]

Opponents Of A Medicare Voucher System Argued That Costs Could Go Up For Beneficiaries If Vouchers Do Not Keep Pace With Healthcare Costs, Leaving People Responsible For Covering The Difference. According to Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, “But some opponents have argued that costs could go up for beneficiaries if vouchers do not keep pace with healthcare costs, leaving people responsible for covering the difference, Medical News Today reported.” [Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, 10/23/26]

Opponents Of A Medicare Voucher System Argued It Could Lower The Quality Of Benefits And Create A Gap That Enrollees Would Have To Fill With Their Own Dollars. According to Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, “There are also concerns about lower quality of benefits, and an increase in complexity to an already confusing system. Naomi Zewde, an assistant professor in health policy at the University of California, Los Angeles, said in an interview that under a voucher system, Medicare payments likely would not keep pace with rising health care prices and would create a gap that enrollees have to fill with their own dollars.” [Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, 10/23/26]
